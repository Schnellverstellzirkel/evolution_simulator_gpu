# Architecture

## Scoring and replays

The GPU scores every creature, and nothing else simulates one. The game needs an NVIDIA GPU with the CUDA driver and NVRTC. `shaders/creature.cu` runs one creature per thread (`src/kernel.rs`). A unit runs as waves of up to 262,144 creatures, one launch each, and a thread runs its creature to the end of its trial and takes the next from the wave, so there are no trial segments. Kernels compile per world (the effects that are on), rate and recording. The engine thread submits each unit as one whole-trial run. A GPU has 24 slots for standard units, a slot of its own for confirmation trials and one for replays. The last two run on streams of the highest priority, and a confirmation unit goes to its own slot first, so it never waits for a standard slot to free up. The GPU result and the GPU confirmation trial are final.

`engine::replay` sends the creature to the primary GPU's engine thread. It runs the scoring kernel with a frame output (node positions, muscle energy and force, contact forces) on a slot and queue of its own, and returns the frames and the result of that same run. When the GPU does not answer in time, the replay viewer holds the first pose and says the replay is unavailable.

## Evaluation flow

The creatures in flight form a ring of blocks whose shape (`storage::RingShape`) is fixed when a game starts. The ring is 8 blocks of 196,608 while the host's p95 time per block is above 0.2 s, because a block's confirmation trials come back 1 to 6 s after they are asked for, behind the units already running, and a short ring starves the GPU. Otherwise a block is 50 ms of GPU work at the engine's rate (32k to 256k creatures), and the ring holds 5 times that host time or the generation boundary plus 2 blocks, whichever is longer, kept between 0.3 s and 1 s of GPU work and at least 2 blocks. The worker sizes a new game from the host times it measured this session, and from a prior before it has measured any. The shape is saved with the game and written into every generation's statistics. It never follows the rate while a game runs, because the number of blocks absorbed before a child is bred decides its parents.

Ring slot `i` fixes a creature's island and random stream. Each block has its own gene arenas. It goes to the engines as one unit for the creatures of the main islands and one unit for each wild island that has creatures in the block, because a wild island runs in a world of its own. Engines finish units in any order, but the ring (`ring.rs`) absorbs blocks strictly in ring order. A block is decided against the archives as they stand, gets the confirmation trials that decision asks for, is offered to the archives, and is bred again from them and queued at the back. A block keeps the trial settings it was bred with, so a fixed seed repeats whatever the timing. A generation is a count of 3M evaluations: history rows, autochange, nursery graduation and hub migration happen when the count passes the generation size. The worker runs ring steps on a helper thread, each absorbing at most one block, and handles UI commands between steps. No command waits for the engines.

A standard trial stops at 5 s when the creature is below the bar, the 5 s distance the top 10% reached. The bar of the evolved creatures is the one the top 10% of every result would have reached if the nursery bodies fell short of it. A body of a nursery has a bar of its own kind, from the results of the nursery of new random bodies or of the nursery of reshaped bodies (`physics::Screen`, creature flags `rungs::YOUNG` and `rungs::RESHAPED`). Each wild island has a bar of its own from its own results. A screened creature enters no archive.

Every other standard result of a main island is final, with two exceptions that get one confirmation trial (`Experiment::verdict`, `scheduler::confirm_config`). It runs from the same pose in the same world at twice the standard step rate, 120 steps per second, and the creature keeps the lower distance. The first exception is a creature that would tie or beat the record of its island or nursery. The record-setters of an archive are taken fastest first, each against the record the ones before it set, so no unconfirmed score becomes a record. The second is the best creature of each cell in a block that would take the cell, in an island archive, a nursery or the global archive, when it has reached at least half of that archive's best distance (`Experiment::entrants`). Wild islands get no confirmation trials. A block asks for its confirmations as soon as its standard results are in, so they run while the blocks before it are absorbed.

Early rungs (`rungs`, the metrics block of `shaders/creature.cu`). A standard trial may also stop at 1 s (R1) or 2.5 s (R2). A rung is a linear score of six features the kernel already measures: the distance, the speed over the last half second, the share of nodes that touched the ground, the head shake, the mean muscle energy store and the rhythm period. The creature stops when the score is below the rung's bias, keeps its distance there and enters no archive, like a screened one. The weights are Fisher's discriminant of the creatures that reach the 5 s bar (at 5 s or at an earlier fall) against those that do not. The bias leaves 1 in 1,000 of the first class below it. The fit runs at each generation boundary on the audit lane: 1 creature in 128, picked by a hash of the seed, the breeding round and the slot, runs with the 5 s screen and both rungs off, so its rows sample what the rules would have stopped. An audit creature that lived past 5 s below the bar enters no archive, as it would not have with the screen. The last 8 generations of audit rows are pooled and saved with the experiment. A block carries the rules it was bred with in its `Config`, so the history depends on ring order only. Nurseries and immigrants skip both rungs, because most of their children stop at 5 s anyway, and so do the wild islands, whose worlds the fit says nothing about. Three guards keep a rule from stopping what the archives grow from. A rung arms only while the rule that would have been in force stopped at most 3% of the entrants the 5 s screen keeps (over 4 generations and at least 60 entrants), or, once the global archive's best has stood for 30 generations, at most 3% of the audit creatures that reach the 5 s bar (at least 1,000 of them). An elite stores its own features at the rungs, and when it is above the median of its island and the rules would stop it, its children skip that rung. A circuit breaker per cadence band (the live gait frequency at the rung) turns a rung off for a band after three generations in which the rules would have stopped more than 100 entrants that the 5 s screen keeps per 10k audit rows of the band, and on again after three calm ones. Confirmations, replays and elite re-tests run no rung. `EVOLUTION_NO_RUNGS` removes the audit lane and the rungs for measurement.

A failed GPU is reopened and its unfinished units, including pending confirmations, run again with the same creatures and settings, so they give the same results. A GPU that does not reopen after three tries stops evolution with an error. A GPU out of memory keeps the unit, frees idle buffers and retries with fewer units in flight.

## Archives and breeding

An island's behavior archive starts with one cell per way of moving: `6 x 8 x 6 x 5 = 1,440` cells of ground contact, gait cadence, logarithmic body height and feet that touched down and lifted off. A cell keeps its fastest eligible creature. New body plans are protected against a different topology for three generations. When a main island is `qd::REFINE_AFTER` (30) generations old, the generation boundary refines it (`Experiment::refine_archives`): every elite moves to the cell of its body class. That makes `6 x 8 x 6 x 5 x 3 x 3 = 12,960` cells, for each way of moving 9 body classes of 3 shapes by the start pose's width over height (under 1.2, 1.2 to 2.0, longer) and 3 sizes by node count (up to 8, 9 to 11, 12 or more). From then on a body of another shape or size has a cell of its own. Local competition and novelty compare the cells of one size class. The isolated islands refine 10 generations apart, at 30, 40, 50 and 60, so climbing and refined islands exist side by side. A climbing archive that kept the classes from its first generation climbed more slowly, because a better gait spreads through its cells one body class at a time (`docs/design-decisions.md`). The global archive never breeds, so it has no climb to protect: it is refined from the first generation and has finer classes, 4 shapes (tall under 0.9, square to 1.4, wide to 2.5, long) by 4 sizes (up to 8 nodes, 9 to 10, 11 to 13, 14 or more), `23,040` cells (`qd::GLOBAL_CLASSES`; the islands use `qd::ISLAND_CLASSES`). The nurseries of new random bodies and every archive of a wild island never refine. A world change keeps the layout of every archive, so the elites tested again in the new world keep the cells of their body classes. A save written by this game tells an island's layout by the cells its elites hold. A save of an older version that still loads (53 and newer) is loaded by moving each elite to its cell in the layout of its archive.

There are 105 islands: four isolated islands, the hub and 100 wild islands. Each island has three archives, the island archive and its two nurseries, so there are 315. Ring slot `i` breeds for island `qd::island_of_slot(i, 105)`. The main islands, the isolated ones and the hub, take 8 of every 10 slots, and the wild islands share the other 2.

A wild island runs in a fixed world of its own: one to three environment effects, each at a level that is neither calm nor the harshest, and a different mix for every island. The 100 worlds are the same in every game (`environment::wild_levels`), and the player's buttons change only the main world. A wild island has its own archive, nurseries and emitters. Its children run without rungs, with a 5 s screen bar from its own results and without confirmation trials. They are offered to its own archives and not directly to the global archive.

Islands 1 to 4 are isolated: parents, mates, limb donors, reserve parents and CMA emitters all come from their own archive. Three small exchanges cross between main islands. Every 50 generations each isolated island sends the fastest elite of its rarest body plan to the next isolated island in a ring (`Experiment::step_stones`). A founder bank keeps the first elite of each new body plan that enters a main island (the newest 1,024), and 1% of the main islands' own slots breed from it. The hub breeds 2% of its own slots from a hall of fame, the fastest elite of each body plan of the main islands (up to 2,048 plans).

Island 5 is the hub. Every 25 generations (`storage::MIGRATION_INTERVAL`) it receives copies of the fastest 10% of each isolated island's elites, and nothing flows back. Each wild island sends its fastest 10%, by the distance in its own world. These creatures run once in the hub's world and take a cell if they are fast enough there. For 30 generations 10% of the hub's own slots also breed from them, the hub's pen, so a wild line has time to adapt to the hub's world before it is dropped. The global archive collects the main islands' elites for display and saves, and no parent comes from it.

Each island keeps a 64-entry morphology reserve of new body plans. It gets 10% of the island's structural-emitter trials and does not count toward coverage or QD score.

Emitter shares start at 14.5% CMA, 62.5% structural and 23% novelty (18% of novelty children also get a structural operator), and immigrants seed empty archives. Each isolated island moves five points from one emitter to another, so the islands develop different habits. Each island also keeps two protected archives (`qd::Arena`, `storage::nursery_of`, `storage::reshaped_of`) that compete only inside themselves. A nursery of new random bodies takes 20% of its island's slots: half hold new random bodies, the rest breed from the nursery's members, and every 10 generations the survivors enter the island archive where they beat the elite of their cell. A nursery of reshaped bodies takes another 10% and, in a main island, uses the refined layout. With these slots, a generation is about 10% new random bodies, 60% children with a structural mutation and 30% children that only change numbers.

`archive_block` sends the reshaped nursery the bodies the island turned away: a child of a structural or novelty emitter whose body plan differs from its parent's and that took neither a cell nor a reserve place. The reshaped nursery keeps the best of them in each cell and its slots tune them with the usual emitters. Every 10 generations the island takes the bodies that beat its elites (`Experiment::graduate_nurseries`) and the nursery keeps them all. A world change clears the nurseries of the main islands, and while the reshaped nursery is empty its slots breed structural children of the island's elites. Its behavior scores refresh once a generation, because it takes offers in every block.

Half the CMA parents come from the fastest 1% of their island. The other parents of a refined island come from a tournament of 8 elites that the best local competition wins, and a bonus for an elite of a rare clade (the elites that share the oldest recorded ancestor, `Experiment::clade_rarity_of`) joins its score. The bonus counts for the share of the island's elites within 10% of its best distance, twice over, up to its full weight, so it decides little while distance still separates the elites and a lot where they are level. Each island also runs a separable CMA-ES on its fastest body plan and rotates to another fast design after 30 generations without an island record. Half of the optimizer's children of a refined island work on the fastest elite of the island's rarest clade instead.

Genomes live in contiguous arenas with per-creature offsets, one set of arenas per block. Breeding a block writes its children in batches straight into new arenas, and the old block's arenas are freed when the last unit holding them returns, so nothing needs compaction.

## Saves and catastrophes

A save (magic header, then a compressed payload) holds the configuration, generation, history, archives, CMA and emitter state, lineage and the queued elites. It holds the islands and their nurseries of new bodies, not the nurseries of reshaped bodies. It holds no ring. Loading starts the saved generation again with a ring bred from the archives. The lineage keeps the record of every living elite without its creature (loading puts it back from the archive), and the ancestors of the global archive's elites and of each island's fastest ten. The header carries the physics version, so an older save is turned down before it loads, unless its version is `qd::OLDEST_LOADABLE` (53) or newer. Autosave is off. Manual saves go through a temporary file that is flushed and renamed.

Meteor strike removes each elite with probability one half from the global archive and from every island and nursery, and spares the fastest elite of each of an archive's 64 fastest body plans. Extinction clears the island archive with the slowest best elite. Both keep the removed entries as fossils in memory, and Undo returns them to empty cells or cells with a slower elite. Fossils are not saved. After either, the survivors of each main island breed from a refuge for a few generations, as a radiation into the emptied cells.

A world change applies at once. Blocks already run or running in the old world enter no archive, blocks not yet on an engine run in the new world, and each main island's elites are queued to be tested again under the new world in that island's own slots, into archives that keep the layout they had. The wild islands keep their archives. For 5 generations, and up to 10 for an island that lost more than half of its best distance, 15% of a main island's own slots breed children of its old champions (the fastest elite of each of up to 64 body plans), so an old design has time to retune its gait to the new world. An island that wins back 90% of its best distance after at least 2 generations closes its refuge. The first 3 generations only retune the gait, and then half of the children also get a structural mutation.

## Threads

The general Rayon pool (archive insertion, breeding, packing) takes every logical CPU but two, as SCHED_BATCH threads at nice 10 pinned to the CPUs after the first two (hardware threads 2 to 15 on the 16-thread workstation, `src/threads.rs`). `RAYON_NUM_THREADS` can lower it. The worker thread runs on the first CPU and the GPU engine thread on the second. The worker runs ring steps on a helper thread and reads commands every millisecond, so a control never waits behind a block being absorbed and bred. Commands that change the experiment apply when the step in progress ends. The UI thread asks the scheduler for a 1 ms slice. Each GPU has its own engine thread that packs the next unit while earlier units run. `EVOLUTION_DEVICES=primary` keeps evaluation to the primary GPU. The UI has its own render device and targets 60 FPS.
