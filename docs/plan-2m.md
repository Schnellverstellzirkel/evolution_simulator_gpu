# PLAN: the architecture for 2M creatures per second on this laptop

Chair: the HPC systems architect. Date: 2026-09-30, after five rounds with eight experts (gpu, cpu, igpu, os, ga, physics, data, ml). Sources: arch/round-1 to round-5, 00-facts.md, hpc.md, the code on main at 29bb266. Every number below names the measurement that confirms it. No lever above 1.5x is still contested on argument; each is contested on a measurement that runs in the first two weeks.

Revision of 2026-10-01 after phase 0 (docs/plan-2m-measurements.md, debate round 6): the per-lane stub missed its rate gate by 2.6x (1,160 warp instructions per creature-step against the estimate of 256; instruction count, not issue rate, is the limit), the lane-group kernel already issues at 62% and sits at the MIO wall, and the host rows landed. Sections 2, 3, 4 and the phase-2 tracks are rewritten; the summary lines 2, 8 and 9 below are superseded by them: the kernel lever is 2.4x to 4.8x for the general per-lane kernel and 4.4x to 8.9x with per-plan baked kernels, the power budget is the measured FMA cap of 124 G warp instructions/s, and the honest ceiling is about 1M/s at generation 51, so 2M/s sustained is not reachable on this laptop with this physics.

## 1. Summary

1. Today the CUDA lane-group kernel runs 43 to 45M creature-steps/s at the generation-3 body and about 27M at the generation-51 body (8.7 nodes and 19 muscles mean, p90 12 and 29, 9% of offspring at 17 to 32 nodes). At 480 steps per creature that is 90 to 100k creatures/s sustained. The goal is 20x from there.
2. The kernel lever is a per-lane kernel: one maximal-coordinate CUDA source templated on W lanes per creature (W = 2, 4, 8 by body size), a chordal rod solve, a direct active-set contact solve, no shuffles or barriers in the tree passes. Estimated 240 to 400M creature-steps/s on the generation-51 mix at 2 substeps (300 to 500M with the body tail held), 9 to 15x on the mean body, confirmed first by a stub kernel gate (128 registers, 32 B spill, 45% issue-active).
3. The physics lever is fewer substeps with the invariants closed by construction: a realized-friction-work ledger, anchored friction, a split-impulse penetration recovery, a compliant tendon, and spin-adaptive substeps (2 when a rod spins above 5 rad/s or a foot approaches above 0.5 m/s, else 1). Worth 1.5 to 1.7x at 65% odds (1.85x at 45% for 1 substep everywhere), decided by an honesty ladder on the current kernel in one day. It needs the owner's yes on one posture rule (what a planted foot is) and a qd::VERSION bump.
4. The steps lever is a rung ladder: R1 at 1 s (a calibrated stop on running state), R2 at 2.5 s, R3 at 5 s with per-arena and per-cell bars, R4 at 10 s (elite-relative, floored at the emitter's own median), a 1-in-128 audit lane with every rule off that calibrates all of it, nurseries and immigrants exempt from R1 and R2. Worth 1.5x (480 to 315 steps) with R1 to R3 and 1.13x more with R4, decided by one dump of a generation-50 generation and an offline replay tool before any kernel work.
5. The host leaves the per-creature path: breeding stays on the CPU in bounded arrays with a counter-based RNG, genes are written once into pinned SoA arenas, DMA'd per block into VRAM, unpacked by a prologue kernel; the kernel computes descriptor bins and a candidate bit; the CPU commits candidates only. The host ceiling becomes 4 to 5M/s. Its cost is the Dynamic Boost tax, carried at 15 to 21% until measured (rows 1 to 3 of the power table), with a frequency cap or burst pacing as the fix.
6. The engine becomes persistent per-class kernels fed by VRAM queues bucketed by muscle count (1.15x by keeping warps uniform after regeneration), with a host-written tail, a spanning take-up counter, results in pinned memory, 3 reserved fine-fidelity slots and 1 replay slot, and ring depth and block size as experiment constants saved with the experiment (determinism). Replay latency falls from up to 1.5 s to 20 to 30 ms; a world change discards under 10k creatures instead of 26% of a generation.
7. The Radeon 780M draws the window and nothing else. Radeon physics is closed by arithmetic (0.2 to 0.25 of the RTX per clock, from the CPU's package budget). A Radeon breeder exists only as a documented fallback with a three-condition trigger. The amdgpu lockup timeout is 2,000 ms and the safety rules go in docs/building.md.
8. The power budget is unknown within 1.6x (3T thread instructions/s sustained under the 100 W cap if the kernel keeps an MIO-heavy mix, 5T if it is FMA-dense). One owner session of five 5-minute pauses plus a 10-minute run fixes it, the host tax, the sustained clock, the Radeon cost and four root settings.
9. Honest ceiling at generation 51: 0.8 to 1.5M/s with 2 substeps; 1.3 to 2.4M/s with spin-adaptive substeps; at generation 100 multiply by 0.65 to 0.82 without a growth rule on bodies, by 0.9 with it. 2M/s sustained needs the substep rung to pass and either the 5T budget or the 3T budget with the host tax under 9%, R4 firing and the growth rule. At 3T with 2 substeps this laptop tops out at 1.0 to 1.3M/s, and the plan says so to the owner rather than promising the goal.
10. Thirty tracks in five phases, each one worktree and one coding agent, each with a measurable gate, ordered so cargo run --release is the better game at every merge. Phase 0 is measurements only and runs in the first two weeks; nothing in the kernel phases commits to a budget before it.

## 2. The architecture

One pipeline, breeding to archive. Revised after phase 0 (docs/plan-2m-measurements.md and round 6 of the debate): the kernel paragraph and the physics paragraph changed; the rest stands.

Breeding (CPU, cpu's design). Each ring block is bred at its absorption from the archive as it stands then. plan_offspring picks emitter, parent and mate per slot. Parametric children (CMA samples and local mutation, about 60% of offspring) go through a 16-lane AVX-512 path over a per-parent cached layout (genes, sigma, clamp bounds, wrap mask), then repair runs on every child; structural children (the 64 operators, none removed) run over bounded arrays (Bounded<T, N>, 32 nodes and 96 muscles, the config caps), sorted by operator and then by parent so one thread runs one operator over hundreds of children. The RNG is a counter-based splitmix hash keyed by (seed, generation, breed round, slot, gene, draw) with a 12-uniform integer gaussian; a changed stream is a new seed. Every child is written once, as SoA genes with a 24 B header and a canonical hash of its bone tree (the plan key, about 100 cycles; a parametric child inherits its parent's), into a pinned host arena owned by the ring block: process memory registered with cuMemHostRegister, allocated once, grown one block ahead. Measured after host-bounded: 10,957 cycles per child (parametric 8,084, of which about 6,000 is the scalar gaussian hashing that host-vector vectorizes; structural 17,260, of which 6,000 is the same embedded pass). Expected after host-vector and host-order: about 2,300 and 9,000, weighted 5,000. breed_bench prints the rows separately and the plan row per slot.

Submission (data's engine). The engine DMAs the block's genes into VRAM on a copy engine, runs a per-block prologue kernel that unpacks 32 creatures per warp (Model::new on the device with pinned rounding intrinsics, bit-equal to the CPU packer) into lane records in VRAM, appends 8 B refs to device queues keyed by (class, plan, muscle bucket), and writes the queue tails and the block's bar table with cuStreamWriteValue32 on the submit stream. Block size and ring depth are experiment constants chosen at experiment start from the first measured rate, saved with the experiment, changed only at a generation boundary with the value recorded in the history; the engine measures the rate for the stage log and never acts on it inside a generation. Measured: the archive stage is 55 ms per 196k block (30 ms at 100k), the boundary at most 41 ms, candidates 2.1%, the whole serial chain about 290 ms per 196k today and about 200 ms after host-bounded; so the ring floor of 0.3 s stands, the saved depth constant is about 0.6 s at 100k blocks, and the candidate bit is deferred (the prefilter it would remove is 15 of the 55 ms).

Scoring (gpu and physics). Persistent kernels hold 92 of the 96 block slots (24 SMs x 4); 3 slots run a persistent fine-fidelity kernel for confirmations and 1 runs replays. The kernel is per-lane: one maximal-coordinate CUDA source in which W lanes share a creature (W = 2 for up to 8 nodes, 4 for 9 to 16, 8 for 17 to 32), each lane owning 4 nodes and up to 16 muscles. The stub of that design measured 1,160 warp instructions per creature-step at W = 2 and 2 substeps against the debate's estimate of 256, issue-active 48% at 15 warps and insensitive to occupancy, so the kernel is instruction-bound. The count decomposes (gpu, physics, round 6): about 290 to 415 warp instructions of physics (the muscle model at 19 muscles is the largest inherent block, about 700 of 3,000 thread instructions per lane-substep), and the rest is overhead from a runtime tree: every rod update does a parent_mask (8 LOP3 and shifts) and a predicated scan over 8 register slots where a baked tree needs one operand (LOP3 alone is 20% of the stream; the tree solves, the factor and the Delassus assembly carry most of the rest), plus 3 active-set rounds at the warp maximum, an 8x8 LDL whose L lives in a 424 B stack frame, and the shared-memory muscle scatter. So the kernel has two forms. The general kernel serves every plan: the tree stays runtime but the removable overhead goes (muscle forces accumulated in registers in a fixed order, parent one-hots hoisted out of the row loops, the state word in registers, the stash gone, the solve at one warm-started round, the projection's second factor only when a node is anchored, no spill), about 750 to 960 warp instructions per creature-step. The baked kernel serves the common plans: NVRTC compiles one kernel per bone tree (the parent array in canonical breadth-first order; muscle ends, lengths and masses stay runtime) with the elimination order and the clique structure as constants, so the tree solve is straight-line code, about 415 warp instructions per creature-step. A plan bucket routes to its baked kernel when the cubin exists and to the general kernel otherwise; the compiler works the plan list by lane-step share in the background; warps are plan-uniform by data's bucket rules; the gate is bit-equal results between the two kernels on the plan's creatures. The lane-group kernel stays only until the per-lane general kernel passes its gate: it sits at the MIO wall (about 430 shared and shuffle instructions per warp-substep against a pipe measured at 0.18 issues per SM per clock, a 50M ceiling that matches its 53 to 55M), so no trimming of it reaches the per-lane numbers.

The physics is maximal coordinates: node positions and velocities as state, rods as exact equality rows eliminated by a chordal LDL in reverse index order (no fill), contacts (deepest 4), joint limits, the spin cap and stretched tendons as at most 16 extra rows, explicit joint damping, one drift projection per step in which contact and anchored nodes have infinite mass, penetration recovery as a split impulse, four ledgers (momentum; angular momentum about the center of mass across every internal operation, because a position-only rod projection drains (omega HS)^2 / 2 of a spinning limb's angular momentum per step, corrected as one uniform rotation; realized friction work including the projection's displacement times rod tension; first law in flight with the tightened allowance), anchored friction with an energy store bounded by absorbed work, spin-adaptive substeps, the lagged Delassus matrix, the waveform and drive target once per step, the first-law ledger only in flight. The contact solve is revised: the Delassus matrix is formed (every solver needs it and the ledgers need consistent impulses), then one warm-started active-set round with a 2-sweep projected Gauss-Seidel polish when the round violates a bound (about 42 warp instructions per substep including the matrix, against 81 for the stub's 3 rounds); the 3-round solve stays as a developer diagnostic; a full projected Gauss-Seidel on the formed matrix is the fallback at 2 substeps, where it holds elites (ratio 1.03); projected Jacobi is dead (every contact updates from the same stale velocities, coupled contacts over-correct together, and the non-negativity clamp keeps the push and discards the pull: random bodies gained 2.1 m). Rung checks read one L2-resident table entry per rung. At the end the group computes the descriptor bins, writes the 80 B result (rung distances and the R1 and R2 features as fp16 pairs in the seven free floats) into a pinned results ring, fences, and decrements the block's remaining count; the last one flips the completion word.

Absorption (CPU, ring order). The worker polls completion words, reads results in place, asks the fine kernel to confirm record candidates (score = min), commits per island in position order through offer and offer_morphology exactly as today, feeds every result to the screen window, the emitter counters and the CMA sample recipes, tells an emitter when 200 results of its state version are absorbed (independent of block size), and breeds the freed block. A versioned append-only elite slab per island lets breed(k+1) on the pool overlap absorb(k) on the worker when the chain needs it (it does not below about 1M/s). At the generation boundary: history, nursery graduation, migration, the streaming screen-bar histogram, ml's discriminant fit on the audit rows (about 20 ms, single-threaded, absorption order), physics_audit on every new island record, and the 4x re-run of the audit lane's entrants before their commit.

The UI. egui on the Radeon as today, the archive map as one texel-per-cell texture and the thumbnails as an atlas rebuilt per snapshot epoch, deltas from the worker, rayon at 14 threads with SCHED_BATCH and nice 10, a 1 ms EEVDF slice on the UI thread, no worker step longer than 5 ms between command reads.

Determinism. Results are addressed by (block, index), never by completion order; absorption, commit and breeding run in ring order; everything a trial reads is fixed when its block is bred (genes, Config, bar table, rung parameters, world tag) except the occupant table, whose effect is result-invariant because occupants only rise and the CPU commits against the real archive; which kernel (baked or general) ran a creature does not change its bits, by the same arithmetic in the same order, which is a gate; atomics only where order does not matter (take-up heads, remaining counts, integer histogram bins); no float atomics; no runtime-measured constant changes a history. Two runs of one seed on one GPU give one history; the set of creatures live at a world change is the one allowed exception.

Faults and recovery. A context loss (an Xid, a CUDA error, a hung kernel past the 2 s drain timeout) frees nothing on the host; the engine reopens, loads cubins from the disk cache, re-registers the arenas, resets heads to tails, re-appends every block whose completion word is unset, and relaunches; blocks re-run whole from the same genes, so the history is invariant. After three failures the game pauses with a message; the CPU engine is deleted and there is no failover physics. The engine stays in-process with the 2 s drain timeout, decided on day one of the persistent-kernels track by one 30-line test: launch a while(1) kernel, call cuCtxDestroy from a second thread after 2 s, measure the return, then cuDevicePrimaryCtxReset and one cuMemAlloc. Pass is a return within 5 s and a working fresh context. Fail means the engine moves to a child process with memfd-shared arenas and results ring at one IPC per block. The result goes in docs/building.md either way.

World change, save, replay. A world change stops the appends, drains the kernels (about 20 ms), requeues the un-taken refs for the new world, excludes the live creatures' results by world tag, and compiles the three general kernels of a cold world in parallel (about 2 s) while the UI shows "compiling the new world"; the baked plan kernels of the new world arrive in the background by lane-step share (about 30 to 60 s for 30 plans on 3 threads) and take over their buckets as they load; the 14 single-toggle neighbour worlds are prefetched at idle priority for the general kernels and the top 10 plans. The kernel cache is evicted by bytes at 300 MB. A save holds archives and search state only (plus the emitters, pending recipes, ml's parameters and a 128 KB histogram window); the ring keeps running. A replay click launches one recording block on the replay slot: 20 to 30 ms. The stage log prints steps per creature, stop share per rung, entrant misses per 10k, mean and p90 nodes and muscles, distinct plans and the baked share of lane-steps, starved seconds and lane-steps per class per block.

## 4. The ceiling on this laptop, honestly

2M/s sustained is not reachable on this laptop with this physics. The measured floor of the physics is about 290 to 415 warp instructions per creature-step at 2 substeps for the p50 body, of which the muscle model at 19 muscles is about a quarter and grows with the population; at the measured FMA cap (124 G warp instructions/s at 1,965 MHz under 100 W) that is 300M creature-steps/s on the mean body with a perfect baked kernel, 170 to 240M at the generation-51 mix, and 265 steps per creature puts the best row at 0.65 to 1.3M/s before the thermal row. The honest number is about 1M/s at generation 51 (0.6 to 1.2M/s) with the per-plan bake, the growth rule and a passing substep rung, and about 0.75M/s at generation 100; about 0.5M/s at generation 51 with the general kernel and spin-adaptive substeps; 0.2 to 0.4M/s with the general kernel at 2 substeps and R1 to R3.

What would move it above 1M/s: a cheaper muscle model (about 700 of the 3,000 inherent thread instructions per lane-substep are 10 muscles at 70 each, and the muscle rules in docs/physics.md are the ones the owner has kept word for word; the owner would have to ask for a change), fewer steps per creature beyond R4 (no rung proposed keeps the spirit), or a second GPU, which is not a software plan. The three measurements that set every row now are the baked-tree stub (2 days), the distinct-tree count (half a day) and the MIO throughput probe (in the owner's session); the substep ladder decides the last 1.5x; none is arguable further.

What the plan does not promise: a fixed kernel number before the baked stub; a fixed steps number before the dump; the host tax before the exclusive rows.

## 5. What stays out and why

- Radeon physics: 0.2 to 0.25 of the RTX per clock, drawn from the same 54 W package the CPU needs; at most 1.0 to 1.1x over a rung the RTX runs alone and shrinking as the RTX gets faster; two desktop resets in the journal from a 2,000 ms timeout. Closed by igpu itself.
- Radeon packing and breeding: nothing to pack once the RTX unpacks from genes; breeding on the Radeon moves watts within the package and returns none to the RTX; a third implementation of every emitter is a determinism seam. Kept only as a fallback with a three-condition trigger (section 8).
- CPU physics: 1 TFLOPS at 54 W against the RTX's boost; a net loss of about 230k/s at the target through Dynamic Boost.
- In-kernel confirmation of record candidates: a second implementation of the fine trial, six live registers in the tightest kernel, a replay that would not match its score, and the nursery graduation makes records fall so the stale-table argument fails. The 3 reserved slots do the job for 3% of the GPU.
- Zero-copy gene reads at take-up: a divergent branch stalls the whole warp (4 to 13%); an in-kernel unpack with one lane active costs 12% of issue slots. DMA plus a prologue kernel replaces it. The one-day test still runs to confirm.
- The mapped-word spin: 4,608 pollers would saturate PCIe. Tail and drain flag in VRAM, host-written.
- Rate-derived ring constants: they change a seed's history. Frozen per experiment.
- Recipes for parametric children on the RTX: about 1% net over the CPU rewrite and a second implementation; insurance behind breed_bench (parametric row above 3,000 cycles).
- The structural operators on the RTX: 6,000 lines for 4 to 6% of rate; opened only if the structural row exceeds 15,000 cycles.
- The archive-following body cap: it holds a reserve plan below what it needs to grow; replaced by the growth-step rule.
- 16-bit muscle genes: the float32 record split per step and per substep keeps L2 under 25% of peak; withdrawn as a requirement.
- Tensor cores, FP16 arithmetic, wavefront kernels, CUDA graphs, general engines, huge pages, io_uring, IRQ affinity, a CPU pre-trial, a learned or fitted contact model, periodic-gait extrapolation (a score that is not a full simulation), a cheap-fidelity screen (a fallback only if every substep rung fails; nets 1.2x on top of R1 to R3), learned routing of confirmations (parked until the friction-work ledger on elites shows exploits in archives), surrogate best-of-2 breeding (parked until a Spearman above 0.6 exists), exact-duplicate elimination (measure the collision share from the dump first), more islands and finer grids (not needed for the rate; every island multiplies host work).

## 6. The measurements that gate the plan (phase 0, weeks 1 to 2)

1. The owner's calibration session (os, claude/power). Five 5-minute pauses plus one 10-minute window, no agent builds or GPU work during it. In order: rows 11 and 12 (a register-only FMA kernel and a 50% MIO kernel through NVRTC at full occupancy, 30 s each, clocks, power, live limit, limiter reasons, nsys issue rate) fix the budget between 3T and 5T; rows 0 to 4 and 19a (p2_speed with 0, 2, 4, 8, 16 busy CPU threads, then 4 and 8 at 20% duty) fix the host tax and decide burst pacing; row 19 (10 minutes) fixes the sustained clock; rows 7 to 10 (igpu's burner from a terminal with the Mesa vendor forced, at 100, 30 and 10% duty, and at 800 and 1,100 MHz forced, root for DPM) close or keep the Radeon fallback; rows 5, 6, 17, 18 (a 3.4 GHz cpufreq cap, EPP balance_performance, memory clock lock, platform profile max-power, root) each kept only if it moves the rate 3% or more. Output: docs/building.md "Power rows".
2. The generation-50 dump (ga and ml, claude/dump). EVOLUTION_DUMP_GENERATION runs one all-audit generation on the current kernel and writes 64 B per creature (distances at steps 60, 150, 300, 600 and final, fall time, final cell, parent cell, entered, operator, nodes, muscles) and 32 B per elite. dump_stats prints the three tail numbers (R1 stop share, entrant share by archive including the reserve, operator histogram), nursery entered counts, the 5 s, 10 s and 20 s cell match, entrant recall per per-cell factor, and mean final / d(10) of entrants. rung_replay fits a candidate ladder on half the rows and prints steps per creature, stop shares, misses per rung and per cadence band, the top 1% and 10% kept, and R4's fire rate. This one file decides the ladder, the per-cell bars, R4, the growth rule and whether W = 8 is built.
3. The substep ladder (physics with gpu, claude/substep-ladder). L0: EVOLUTION_WARP_SUBSTEPS=1 with no code, evolve 30 generations at 3M on one seed, re-test the top 300 at 2 and 4 substeps (about 25 minutes under the shared lock). L1: plus the realized-work ledger (about 40 kernel lines). L2: plus anchored friction (about 60 lines). L2.5: plus spin-adaptive substeps. Pass bar at every rung: median honesty ratio at least 0.9 at 4 substeps, p10 reported (a warning below 0.6), planted-foot slip 0.95 to 1.05, first_generation median within 0.05 m, realized friction work under 1% of muscle work. This decides the largest single lever.
4. The stub kernel (gpu, claude/stub-kernel). The real state layout, the shared tables, a 16-muscle loop from split L2 records, the 4-rod LDL with the W = 2 exchange, 8 tree solves and the 8x8 direct solve on synthetic data, 300 fake steps over 262k creatures; plus a 30-minute nsys warp-state sample of the lane-group kernel (the share of stalls that are barrier and short-scoreboard: under 30% means the per-lane layout cannot reach 45%). Gate: at most 128 registers, at most 32 B spill (per program region, stack frame and lmem reported separately), at least 45% issue-active at 16 warps, extrapolated at least 250M creature-steps/s on the mean body at 2 substeps. Below 35% issue the design goes to W = 1 at 255 registers for small bodies and the table drops 20%.
5. The mature host profile (cpu, claude/host-profile). A version-42 checkout built in its own worktree; EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED on save42.evo and night.evo for one generation: the seven archive sections per block, the boundary time, the candidate share, tells per block. Decides the archive track's shape (incremental novelty and a pool-side boundary if over 60 ms per block or 300 ms per boundary) and the ring floor (0.3 or 0.5 s).
6. breed_bench (cpu, inside claude/host-bounded). Ten blocks of 100k from a save or a grown archive with no GPU: thread time per child per emitter and per operator, plan per slot, allocations per child, a digest at 1 and 14 threads. Decides ruling 11: structural row above 9,000 cycles opens the frequency cap and burst pacing; parametric row above 3,000 opens recipes on the RTX; structural above 15,000 opens the structural port.
7. The one-day take-up test (gpu, inside claude/prologue-unpack). p2_speed on save42 in three forms: records from VRAM, records zero-copy from mapped pinned memory, genes with an in-kernel unpack. Any form within 2% of the first is acceptable.
8. The bucket test (data, claude/bucket-counters). A counter per (class, rounds) in today's lane-group kernel; p2_speed on save42 with and without. Gate: at least 5% faster, results identical per creature between runs of the same build (determinism), not against another kernel. Runs this month and is the first kernel-side merge.

## 7. Decisions the owner must make

1. Anchored friction (what a planted foot is: it holds where it landed and slides when the cone binds, with an energy store like the tendon's) and spin-adaptive substeps. Both are physics changes with a qd::VERSION bump and a re-evolved population. Asked when the ladder's rung passes its bar.
2. The growth-step body rule (a child gains at most 4 nodes and 4 muscles over its parent) as a body limit in the operators' fit check. Asked when the dump's three tail numbers exist.
3. The calibration session (section 6, item 1) and the four root settings it tests.
4. The disk: prune the finished worktrees (74 exist), keep at most 10, remove a worktree the day its branch merges; an agent refuses to seed below 30 GB free. The disk was at 51% with 220 GB free at the end of the debate.
5. The maximal-coordinate physics itself (a new physics3.rs reference, the contact solve as a direct complementarity system, one drift projection per step). The owner said yes to reduced coordinates on 2026-09-28; this is a second formulation change and it is asked explicitly, with docs/physics.md's changed paragraphs in the track.

## 9. How the debate closed

Five rounds. Round 2 removed a phantom lever (the settle phase, which the CUDA kernel does not run), merged four names for one steps lever into one ladder, and settled that breeding stays on the CPU. Round 3 produced one converged pipeline and the generation-51 population from save42.evo. Round 4 found eleven defects (in-kernel confirmation, zero-copy take-up, the mapped-word spin, rate-derived ring constants, two bucket-queue races, two physics holes in the drift projection and the push-out, the explicit tendon past its stability bound, R1 against nurseries, R4's sprinter drift, the archive-following cap against the reserve) and closed all of them in the design. Round 5: every expert backs the design above and none rejected a ruling. The levers still open are the power budget, the substep rung and the kernel's issue rate, each a 1.5 to 1.6x factor decided by a measurement in the first two weeks, so no further round was needed. Round 6 (2026-10-01) was the revision round after phase 0: gpu, physics, data and cpu counted the stub's instructions section by section (about 290 to 415 warp instructions per creature-step of physics, the rest a runtime tree, three active-set rounds and spills), agreed that the lane-group kernel is MIO-bound at about 50M, backed the per-lane general kernel now and per-plan baked kernels as the measured track behind it, replaced the 3-round active set with one warm-started round plus a PGS polish, and found the host tax near zero at today's rate so the recipes and port triggers do not fire yet. Track names in the experts' round-5 files map onto the tables above (data's ring-constants is ring-size, pinned-arenas is host-arenas plus prologue-unpack, persistent-queues is persistent-kernels, tables-candidates is bar-table plus candidate-bit; physics's ladder is substep-ladder, perlane-w2 is lane-w2, adaptive-substeps is substep-default, physics-cuts is lane-cuts, audit-rerun is inside audit-lane; cpu's host-threads is inside frames; igpu's ui-textures is inside delta-snapshot).

## 10. What would change this plan

Each in the first two weeks: the FMA calibration landing at 2.0 GHz or below at the cap (3T: the 5T column is deleted, every stall-removal lever is worth nothing, 2M/s needs L2 or L2.5 plus the growth rule plus the full ladder with no margin); the stub below 35% issue-active (lane-w1 opens, the table drops 20%); the ladder failing every rung (cheap-screen opens, the sustained target is stated as 1.2M/s); the dump showing entrants unpredictable at 5 s per cell (cell-bars is dropped and the ladder is worth 1.3x, not 1.7x); breed_bench's structural row above 15,000 (structural-rtx opens); rows 2 and 3 showing the host tax under 9% at 4 threads (the cap and pacing are withdrawn and every row gains 1.1x); the tail dump showing any 17-plus body entering an archive (W = 8 is built before W = 4).
