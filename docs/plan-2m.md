## 1. Summary

1. Today the CUDA lane-group kernel runs 43 to 45M creature-steps/s at the generation-3 body and about 27M at the generation-51 body (8.7 nodes and 19 muscles mean, p90 12 and 29, 9% of offspring at 17 to 32 nodes). At 480 steps per creature that is 90 to 100k creatures/s sustained. The goal is 20x from there.
2. The kernel lever is a per-lane kernel: one maximal-coordinate CUDA source templated on W lanes per creature (W = 2, 4, 8 by body size), a chordal rod solve, a direct active-set contact solve, no shuffles or barriers in the tree passes. Estimated 240 to 400M creature-steps/s on the generation-51 mix at 2 substeps (300 to 500M with the body tail held), 9 to 15x on the mean body, confirmed first by a stub kernel gate (128 registers, 32 B spill, 45% issue-active).
3. The physics lever is fewer substeps with the invariants closed by construction: a realized-friction-work ledger, anchored friction, a split-impulse penetration recovery, a compliant tendon, and spin-adaptive substeps (2 when a rod spins above 5 rad/s or a foot approaches above 0.5 m/s, else 1). Worth 1.5 to 1.7x at 65% odds (1.85x at 45% for 1 substep everywhere), decided by an honesty ladder on the current kernel in one day. It needs the owner's yes on one posture rule (what a planted foot is) and a qd::VERSION bump.
4. The steps lever is a rung ladder: R1 at 1 s (a calibrated stop on running state), R2 at 2.5 s, R3 at 5 s with per-arena and per-cell bars, R4 at 10 s (elite-relative, floored at the emitter's own median), a 1-in-128 audit lane with every rule off that calibrates all of it, nurseries and immigrants exempt from R1 and R2. Worth 1.5x (480 to 315 steps) with R1 to R3 and 1.13x more with R4, decided by one dump of a generation-50 generation and an offline replay tool before any kernel work.
5. The host leaves the per-creature path: breeding stays on the CPU in bounded arrays with a counter-based RNG, genes are written once into pinned SoA arenas, DMA'd per block into VRAM, unpacked by a prologue kernel; the kernel computes descriptor bins and a candidate bit; the CPU commits candidates only. The host ceiling becomes 4 to 5M/s. Its cost is the Dynamic Boost tax, carried at 15 to 21% until measured (rows 1 to 3 of the power table), with a frequency cap or burst pacing as the fix.
6. The engine becomes persistent per-class kernels fed by VRAM queues bucketed by muscle count (1.15x by keeping warps uniform after regeneration), with a host-written tail, a spanning take-up counter, results in pinned memory, 3 reserved fine-fidelity slots and 1 replay slot, and ring depth and block size as experiment constants saved with the experiment (determinism). Replay latency falls from up to 1.5 s to 20 to 30 ms; a world change discards under 10k creatures instead of 26% of a generation.
7. The Radeon 780M draws the window and nothing else. Radeon physics is closed by arithmetic (0.2 to 0.25 of the RTX per clock, from the CPU's package budget). A Radeon breeder exists only as a documented fallback with a three-condition trigger. The amdgpu lockup timeout is 2,000 ms and the safety rules go in docs/building.md.
8. The power budget is unknown within 1.6x (3T thread instructions/s sustained under the 100 W cap if the kernel keeps an MIO-heavy mix, 5T if it is FMA-dense). One owner session of five 5-minute pauses plus a 10-minute run fixes it, the host tax, the sustained clock, the Radeon cost and four root settings.
9. Honest ceiling at generation 51: 0.8 to 1.5M/s with 2 substeps; 1.3 to 2.4M/s with spin-adaptive substeps; at generation 100 multiply by 0.65 to 0.82 without a growth rule on bodies, by 0.9 with it. 2M/s sustained needs the substep rung to pass and either the 5T budget or the 3T budget with the host tax under 9%, R4 firing and the growth rule. At 3T with 2 substeps this laptop tops out at 1.0 to 1.3M/s, and the plan says so to the owner rather than promising the goal.
10. Thirty tracks in five phases, each one worktree and one coding agent, each with a measurable gate, ordered so cargo run --release is the better game at every merge. Phase 0 is measurements only and runs in the first two weeks; nothing in the kernel phases commits to a budget before it.

## 2. The architecture

One pipeline, breeding to archive. Revised twice: after phase 0 (docs/plan-2m-measurements.md, round 6) and after the owner removed the golden-reference premise (AGENTS.md at c198daf, round 7: docs/plan-2m-debate/round-7-*.md). The physics is now a lever, not a fixed input; the kernel paragraph and the physics paragraph below describe the lean physics as the candidate and today's rules as the fallback. The rest stands.

Breeding (CPU, cpu's design). Each ring block is bred at its absorption from the archive as it stands then. plan_offspring picks emitter, parent and mate per slot. Parametric children (about 60% of offspring) go through a 16-lane AVX-512 path over a per-parent cached layout, then repair runs on every child; structural children (the 64 operators, none removed) run over bounded arrays, sorted by operator and then by parent. Under the lean physics 27 of the 64 operators change what they read and write (ga, round 7): 15 that place a muscle end at a point along a bone place it at a node instead and split the bone there when the node does not exist (the existing split-bone path), 6 stroke operators act on strength, 6 stiffness operators act on strength, and local_mutation's three tendon moves act on the bone's ligament gene; every operator keeps its name and its property test. The genome loses anchor_a, anchor_b, short, long and tendon per muscle and gains a ligament compliance per bone; the CMA dimension on the p50 body falls from 220 to 151 and the emitter track is unchanged except the Layout table. The RNG is a counter-based splitmix hash keyed by (seed, generation, breed round, slot, gene, draw) with a 12-uniform integer gaussian. Every child is written once, as SoA genes with a 24 B header and a canonical hash of its bone tree, into a pinned host arena owned by the ring block: process memory registered with cuMemHostRegister, allocated once, grown one block ahead. Measured after host-bounded: 10,957 cycles per child (parametric 8,084, of which about 6,000 is scalar gaussian hashing that host-vector vectorizes; structural 17,260); expected after host-vector and host-order about 2,300 and 9,000.

Submission (data's engine). The engine DMAs the block's genes into VRAM on a copy engine, runs a per-block prologue kernel that unpacks 32 creatures per warp (the device replaces the CPU packer outright; its gate is the spirit checks on the unpacked population, never a comparison with the CPU packer's bits) into lane records in VRAM, appends 8 B refs to device queues keyed by (class, plan, muscle bucket), and writes the queue tails and the block's bar table with cuStreamWriteValue32 on the submit stream. Block size and ring depth are experiment constants chosen at experiment start from the first measured rate, saved with the experiment, changed only at a generation boundary with the value recorded in the history. Measured: the archive stage is 55 ms per 196k block, the boundary at most 41 ms, candidates 2.1%; the ring floor of 0.3 s stands and the candidate bit is deferred.

Scoring (gpu and physics). Persistent kernels hold 92 of the 96 block slots; 3 slots run a persistent fine-fidelity kernel for confirmations and 1 runs replays. The kernel is per-lane. The phase-0 stub of the per-lane kernel on today's rules measured 1,160 warp instructions per creature-step at W = 2 (against the debate's 256), issue-active 48%, occupancy-insensitive: instruction-bound, with about 290 to 415 of physics and the rest a runtime tree (parent_mask and predicated register scans, LOP3 20%), three active-set rounds, an 8x8 LDL in a 424 B stack frame and the muscle state traffic. Under the lean physics the three largest blocks go: the muscle model falls from 700 to 320 lane instructions per substep at W = 2 (no state word, no anchors, no tendon), the contact solve from 990 to 80 (per-node impulses against the node's own mass for every touching node, coupled by the exact chordal rod solve, two passes; joint limits and the spin cap as per-joint impulses), and the register arrays that spilled disappear, so W = 1 (one creature per lane, no exchange) fits for bodies up to 8 nodes at about 117 to 145 registers with nothing left to spill. The kernel is built in this order: first a general W = 1 kernel with a runtime tree whose rod state lives in shared memory in [rod][lane] layout so the parent index is an address rather than a predicated scan (about 1.2x over a baked tree, no per-plan compiles, one kernel per world and class); then, only if the distinct-tree count shows the top 100 plans covering 85% of a mature ring, per-plan baked kernels compiled by NVRTC with the parent array as constants for another 1.2 to 1.3x, routed by plan bucket with the general kernel serving the rest and stealing from plan queues so nothing starves. Counts at W = 1 on the p50 body (gpu, round 7, carried as the higher of the two experts' tables): baked 131 warp instructions per creature-step at 2 substeps, 182 at 4 substeps with the lagged factor, 77 at 1; general runtime tree 203 and 264; shared-indexed about 1.2x the baked count. The MIO count at W = 1 is 14 issues per creature-step through the shared-memory force scatter, which is the wall (770M on the mean body) only if the MIO throughput probe confirms the latency-bound 10.8 G/s; a register scatter by predicated selects (150 warp instructions, 3.5 MIO issues) is the alternative. The lane-group kernel keeps scoring the game until the per-lane kernel passes its gates, and it is the host of the 30-generation evolution under the lean muscle model, because it runs the whole game path and its muscle round takes the change in days; its contact solve stays as it is for that run (reduced coordinates have no rod solve to couple per-node impulses, and its PGS holds elites at ratio 1.03). The gaits evolved there are then re-scored on the per-lane kernel, and that transfer (median ratio at least 0.9) is the finer-solver retest the spirit asks for; no bit comparison between the two kernels is a gate.

The physics, lean form (physics round 7, amended by gpu, ga and ml). Maximal coordinates: node positions and velocities as state, rods exact by the chordal LDL each substep (the factor lagged once per step at 4 substeps), symplectic Euler, one drift projection per step with contact nodes at infinite mass, penetration recovery as a split impulse, four ledgers (momentum, angular momentum about the center, realized friction and projection work, first law in flight). Muscles join two nodes; force = cap x strength x a(t) x E x max(0, 1 - v / v_max), pull only, with the damper; a(t) is a trapezoid of period, phase and duty with a fixed ramp of two substeps on each edge (a constant, not a gene, so no force steps in one substep); E is one stamina store per creature, charged with the summed work and recovering half its deficit per second; the cap scaled by the driven mass and the muscle mass stay; there is no tendon and no per-muscle state; elastic storage is a ligament compliance gene on the joint-limit row, implicit and bounded by what it stored; per-limb touchdown clocks stay. Contacts: every node that would reach the ground within the substep gets a normal impulse against its own mass to stop the approach and a friction impulse toward its anchor (or zero slip) clamped to mu times the normal and by the clean rule, then the rod solve redistributes, then one more pass; not Jacobi (each node solves against its own mass and never overshoots; the exact rod solve is the coupling). Substeps: 4 of 1/240 s with the lagged factor as the primary, 2 of 1/120 s as the stretch if its retest passes; the honesty retest is at 4x the production substep count (16 of 1/960 for the primary, 8 for the stretch), median at least 0.9, p10 at least 0.7, share under 0.5 at most 2%. What stays from today's rules: pull-only, Hill, the energy cost, the rhythm genes, the touchdown clocks, the muscle mass, the four ledgers, the fall and break rules, distance-only fitness. Today's rules (anchored muscles with the cosine waveform, per-muscle energy, the tendon, the Delassus active set) are the fallback if the lean physics fails its spirit gates, at the round-6 counts (415 baked, 750 to 960 general).

Absorption (CPU, ring order). The worker polls completion words, reads results in place, asks the fine kernel to confirm record candidates (score = min), commits per island in position order, feeds every result to the screen window, the emitter counters and the CMA sample recipes, tells an emitter when 200 results of its state version are absorbed, and breeds the freed block. At the generation boundary: history, nursery graduation, migration, the streaming screen-bar histogram, ml's discriminant fit on the audit rows, physics_audit on every new island record (trial energy excess at most 1% of muscle work, which is the guard that catches a ligament released on the ground), and the 4x re-run of the audit lane's entrants before their commit (median at least 0.9, p10 at least 0.7, an entrant under 0.5 refused and counted).

The UI. egui on the Radeon as today, the archive map as one texel-per-cell texture and the thumbnails as an atlas rebuilt per snapshot epoch, deltas from the worker, rayon at 14 threads with SCHED_BATCH and nice 10, a 1 ms EEVDF slice on the UI thread, no worker step longer than 5 ms between command reads. The replay frame carries stamina once per creature and the force per muscle.

Determinism. Results are addressed by (block, index), never by completion order; absorption, commit and breeding run in ring order; everything a trial reads is fixed when its block is bred except the occupant table, whose effect is result-invariant; the lane a creature runs on must not change its result on one build; atomics only where order does not matter; no float atomics; no runtime-measured constant changes a history. One build on one GPU gives one search per seed. Nothing compares bits between implementations or kernel variants; the spirit checks and the finer-rate retest are the gates between them.

Faults and recovery. A context loss frees nothing on the host; the engine reopens, loads cubins from the disk cache, re-registers the arenas, resets heads to tails, re-appends every block whose completion word is unset, and relaunches; blocks re-run whole from the same genes. After three failures the game pauses with a message. The engine stays in-process with the 2 s drain timeout, decided on day one of the persistent-kernels track by the cuCtxDestroy hang test; a child process with memfd-shared arenas only if it fails.

World change, save, replay. A world change stops the appends, drains the kernels (about 20 ms), requeues the un-taken refs for the new world, excludes the live creatures' results by world tag, and compiles the general kernels of a cold world in parallel while the UI shows "compiling the new world"; baked plan kernels, if the track exists, arrive in the background by lane-step share. A physics change is treated as a world change by the rung ladder (disarm, discard the window, rearm after 8k audit rows). A save holds archives and search state only. A replay click launches one recording block on the replay slot: 20 to 30 ms. The stage log prints steps per creature, stop share per rung, entrant misses per 10k, mean and p90 nodes and muscles, distinct plans, the pacing report (share of elites with a pause, median final / d600), starved seconds and lane-steps per class per block.

## 4. The ceiling on this laptop, honestly

With the lean physics 2M/s at generation 51 is back within reach, and it rests on three things that are counted but not yet measured: the stub variants printing within 1.3x of gpu's table (phase 0's table overran by 4.5x, so until the counts exist ml's rule applies and the rows are worth half), the 2-substep physics passing its retest at 8 substeps (otherwise the primary at 4 substeps holds and the best row is 1.75M/s with baked kernels and R4, 1.45M/s with shared-indexed rods), and the kernel reaching 55% of its FMA bound on the mean body. On the primary row the honest number is 1.1 to 1.75M/s at generation 51 (general to baked) and 0.9 to 1.5M/s at generation 100; on the stretch row 1.4 to 2.45M/s and 1.2 to 2.1M/s. 2M/s sustained across generations needs the stretch physics plus the shared-indexed or baked kernel plus the ladder, or the primary physics plus baked kernels plus R4 with no margin. The host tax at 2M/s is the next wall after the kernel: 3.7 busy cores after host-vector and host-order, which the frequency cap, burst pacing or recipes on the RTX must bring under 3, decided by the exclusive power rows.

What would move the ceiling further: the MIO throughput probe at 60 G/s (removes the scatter cap), the tree count showing the top 30 plans covering 70% (baked kernels become the default), 1 substep passing an honesty retest at 4 (77 warp instructions per creature-step, 3.7M/s at 300 steps before the host tax, which physics rates at low odds).

What the plan does not promise: a kernel number before the stub variants print; a population before the 30-generation run; the host tax before the exclusive rows; today's generation-51 body histogram, which was measured under a physics that will not exist.

## 5. What stays out and why

- Radeon physics: 0.2 to 0.25 of the RTX per clock, drawn from the same 54 W package the CPU needs; at most 1.0 to 1.1x over a rung the RTX runs alone and shrinking as the RTX gets faster; two desktop resets in the journal from a 2,000 ms timeout. Closed by igpu itself.
- Radeon packing and breeding: nothing to pack once the RTX unpacks from genes; breeding on the Radeon moves watts within the package and returns none to the RTX; a third implementation of every emitter is a determinism seam. Kept only as a fallback with a three-condition trigger (section 8).
- CPU physics: 1 TFLOPS at 54 W against the RTX's boost; a net loss of about 230k/s at the target through Dynamic Boost.
- In-kernel confirmation of record candidates: a second implementation of the fine trial, six live registers in the tightest kernel, a replay that would not match its score, and the nursery graduation makes records fall so the stale-table argument fails. The 3 reserved slots do the job for 3% of the GPU.
- Zero-copy gene reads at take-up: a divergent branch stalls the whole warp (4 to 13%); an in-kernel unpack with one lane active costs 12% of issue slots. DMA plus a prologue kernel replaces it. The one-day test still runs to confirm.
- The mapped-word spin: 4,608 pollers would saturate PCIe. Tail and drain flag in VRAM, host-written.
- Rate-derived ring constants: they change a seed's history. Frozen per experiment.
- Recipes for parametric children on the RTX: about 1% net over the CPU rewrite and a second implementation; insurance behind breed_bench (parametric row above 3,000 cycles).
- The structural operators on the RTX: 6,000 lines for 4 to 6% of rate; opened only if the structural row exceeds 15,000 cycles.
- The archive-following body cap: it holds a reserve plan below what it needs to grow; replaced by the growth-step rule.
- 16-bit muscle genes: the float32 record split per step and per substep keeps L2 under 25% of peak; withdrawn as a requirement.
- Tensor cores, FP16 arithmetic, wavefront kernels, CUDA graphs, general engines, huge pages, io_uring, IRQ affinity, a CPU pre-trial, a learned or fitted contact model, periodic-gait extrapolation (a score that is not a full simulation), a cheap-fidelity screen (a fallback only if every substep rung fails; nets 1.2x on top of R1 to R3), learned routing of confirmations (parked until the friction-work ledger on elites shows exploits in archives), surrogate best-of-2 breeding (parked until a Spearman above 0.6 exists), exact-duplicate elimination (measure the collision share from the dump first), more islands and finer grids (not needed for the rate; every island multiplies host work).

## 6. The measurements that gate the plan (phase 0, weeks 1 to 2)

1. The owner's calibration session (os, claude/power). Five 5-minute pauses plus one 10-minute window, no agent builds or GPU work during it. In order: rows 11 and 12 (a register-only FMA kernel and a 50% MIO kernel through NVRTC at full occupancy, 30 s each, clocks, power, live limit, limiter reasons, nsys issue rate) fix the budget between 3T and 5T; rows 0 to 4 and 19a (p2_speed with 0, 2, 4, 8, 16 busy CPU threads, then 4 and 8 at 20% duty) fix the host tax and decide burst pacing; row 19 (10 minutes) fixes the sustained clock; rows 7 to 10 (igpu's burner from a terminal with the Mesa vendor forced, at 100, 30 and 10% duty, and at 800 and 1,100 MHz forced, root for DPM) close or keep the Radeon fallback; rows 5, 6, 17, 18 (a 3.4 GHz cpufreq cap, EPP balance_performance, memory clock lock, platform profile max-power, root) each kept only if it moves the rate 3% or more. Output: docs/building.md "Power rows".
2. The generation-50 dump (ga and ml, claude/dump). EVOLUTION_DUMP_GENERATION runs one all-audit generation on the current kernel and writes 64 B per creature (distances at steps 60, 150, 300, 600 and final, fall time, final cell, parent cell, entered, operator, nodes, muscles) and 32 B per elite. dump_stats prints the three tail numbers (R1 stop share, entrant share by archive including the reserve, operator histogram), nursery entered counts, the 5 s, 10 s and 20 s cell match, entrant recall per per-cell factor, and mean final / d(10) of entrants. rung_replay fits a candidate ladder on half the rows and prints steps per creature, stop shares, misses per rung and per cadence band, the top 1% and 10% kept, and R4's fire rate. This one file decides the ladder, the per-cell bars, R4, the growth rule and whether W = 8 is built.
3. The substep ladder (physics with gpu, claude/substep-ladder). L0: EVOLUTION_WARP_SUBSTEPS=1 with no code, evolve 30 generations at 3M on one seed, re-test the top 300 at 2 and 4 substeps (about 25 minutes under the shared lock). L1: plus the realized-work ledger (about 40 kernel lines). L2: plus anchored friction (about 60 lines). L2.5: plus spin-adaptive substeps. Pass bar at every rung: median honesty ratio at least 0.9 at 4 substeps, p10 reported (a warning below 0.6), planted-foot slip 0.95 to 1.05, first_generation median within 0.05 m, realized friction work under 1% of muscle work. This decides the largest single lever.
4. The stub kernel (gpu, claude/stub-kernel). The real state layout, the shared tables, a 16-muscle loop from split L2 records, the 4-rod LDL with the W = 2 exchange, 8 tree solves and the 8x8 direct solve on synthetic data, 300 fake steps over 262k creatures; plus a 30-minute nsys warp-state sample of the lane-group kernel (the share of stalls that are barrier and short-scoreboard: under 30% means the per-lane layout cannot reach 45%). Gate: at most 128 registers, at most 32 B spill (per program region, stack frame and lmem reported separately), at least 45% issue-active at 16 warps, extrapolated at least 250M creature-steps/s on the mean body at 2 substeps. Below 35% issue the design goes to W = 1 at 255 registers for small bodies and the table drops 20%.
5. The mature host profile (cpu, claude/host-profile). A version-42 checkout built in its own worktree; EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED on save42.evo and night.evo for one generation: the seven archive sections per block, the boundary time, the candidate share, tells per block. Decides the archive track's shape (incremental novelty and a pool-side boundary if over 60 ms per block or 300 ms per boundary) and the ring floor (0.3 or 0.5 s).
6. breed_bench (cpu, inside claude/host-bounded). Ten blocks of 100k from a save or a grown archive with no GPU: thread time per child per emitter and per operator, plan per slot, allocations per child, a digest at 1 and 14 threads. Decides ruling 11: structural row above 9,000 cycles opens the frequency cap and burst pacing; parametric row above 3,000 opens recipes on the RTX; structural above 15,000 opens the structural port.
7. The one-day take-up test (gpu, inside claude/prologue-unpack). p2_speed on save42 in three forms: records from VRAM, records zero-copy from mapped pinned memory, genes with an in-kernel unpack. Any form within 2% of the first is acceptable.
8. The bucket test (data, claude/bucket-counters). A counter per (class, rounds) in today's lane-group kernel; p2_speed on save42 with and without. Gate: at least 5% faster, results identical per creature between runs of the same build (determinism), not against another kernel. Runs this month and is the first kernel-side merge.

## 7. Decisions the owner must make

1. Anchored friction (what a planted foot is: it holds where it landed and slides when the cone binds, with an energy store like the tendon's) and spin-adaptive substeps. Both are physics changes with a qd::VERSION bump and a re-evolved population. Asked when the ladder's rung passes its bar.
2. The growth-step body rule (a child gains at most 4 nodes and 4 muscles over its parent) as a body limit in the operators' fit check. Asked when the dump's three tail numbers exist.
3. The calibration session (section 6, item 1) and the four root settings it tests.
4. The disk: prune the finished worktrees (74 exist), keep at most 10, remove a worktree the day its branch merges; an agent refuses to seed below 30 GB free. The disk was at 51% with 220 GB free at the end of the debate.
5. The maximal-coordinate physics itself (a new physics3.rs reference, the contact solve as a direct complementarity system, one drift projection per step). The owner said yes to reduced coordinates on 2026-09-28; this is a second formulation change and it is asked explicitly, with docs/physics.md's changed paragraphs in the track.

## 9. How the debate closed

Five rounds. Round 2 removed a phantom lever (the settle phase, which the CUDA kernel does not run), merged four names for one steps lever into one ladder, and settled that breeding stays on the CPU. Round 3 produced one converged pipeline and the generation-51 population from save42.evo. Round 4 found eleven defects (in-kernel confirmation, zero-copy take-up, the mapped-word spin, rate-derived ring constants, two bucket-queue races, two physics holes in the drift projection and the push-out, the explicit tendon past its stability bound, R1 against nurseries, R4's sprinter drift, the archive-following cap against the reserve) and closed all of them in the design. Round 5: every expert backs the design above and none rejected a ruling. The levers still open are the power budget, the substep rung and the kernel's issue rate, each a 1.5 to 1.6x factor decided by a measurement in the first two weeks, so no further round was needed. Round 6 (2026-10-01) was the revision round after phase 0: gpu, physics, data and cpu counted the stub's instructions section by section (about 290 to 415 warp instructions per creature-step of physics, the rest a runtime tree, three active-set rounds and spills), agreed that the lane-group kernel is MIO-bound at about 50M, backed the per-lane general kernel now and per-plan baked kernels as the measured track behind it, replaced the 3-round active set with one warm-started round plus a PGS polish, and found the host tax near zero at today's rate so the recipes and port triggers do not fire yet. Round 7 (2026-10-01) followed the owner's removal of the golden-reference premise: physics wrote the lean physics, gpu counted it (25 to 30% above physics's own count at 2 and 4 substeps, which the plan carries), ga re-targeted the operators and set the search pass numbers, ml set the guards (the whole-trial energy balance for the ligament, the realized-work ledger for per-node contacts, a two-substep ramp on the trapezoid, the retest at 4x the production substep count) and ruled R4 conditional under stamina pacing. Track names in the experts' round-5 files map onto the tables above (data's ring-constants is ring-size, pinned-arenas is host-arenas plus prologue-unpack, persistent-queues is persistent-kernels, tables-candidates is bar-table plus candidate-bit; physics's ladder is substep-ladder, perlane-w2 is lane-w2, adaptive-substeps is substep-default, physics-cuts is lane-cuts, audit-rerun is inside audit-lane; cpu's host-threads is inside frames; igpu's ui-textures is inside delta-snapshot).

## 10. What would change this plan

Each in the first two weeks: the FMA calibration landing at 2.0 GHz or below at the cap (3T: the 5T column is deleted, every stall-removal lever is worth nothing, 2M/s needs L2 or L2.5 plus the growth rule plus the full ladder with no margin); the stub below 35% issue-active (lane-w1 opens, the table drops 20%); the ladder failing every rung (cheap-screen opens, the sustained target is stated as 1.2M/s); the dump showing entrants unpredictable at 5 s per cell (cell-bars is dropped and the ladder is worth 1.3x, not 1.7x); breed_bench's structural row above 15,000 (structural-rtx opens); rows 2 and 3 showing the host tax under 9% at 4 threads (the cap and pacing are withdrawn and every row gains 1.1x); the tail dump showing any 17-plus body entering an archive (W = 8 is built before W = 4).
