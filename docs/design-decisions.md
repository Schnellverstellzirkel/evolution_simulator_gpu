# Design decisions

What the game does in search, physics and speed, and the number that decided each choice. Each item stays true until a direct measurement of a change shows the game is better or faster without it. Ideas that lost are in `docs/rejected-ideas.md`.

## Search

Fitness is horizontal center-of-mass distance and nothing else. Behavior (ground contact, cadence, body height, lifted feet) picks the archive niche and never changes the score. The physics rules that end a trial (a fall, a broken joint, head shaking above 8 g) keep distance the only objective.

The search is MAP-Elites with four emitters: CMA tuning, anatomy mutations, novelty and random immigrants. Immigrants only seed empty archives. Each island also keeps two protected archives that compete only inside themselves.

A nursery of new random bodies takes 5% of its slots. Half of them hold new random bodies and the rest breed from the nursery's members. Every 10 generations the survivors enter the island archive where they beat the elite of their cell, and the nursery starts over.

A nursery of reshaped bodies takes another 10%. The island turns away a child of its structural or novelty emitter whose body plan differs from its parent's and that takes neither a cell nor a reserve place. Those bodies enter the reshaped nursery instead of dying. There they compete for cells only with other turned-away bodies, the nursery's slots tune them with the usual emitters, and every 10 generations the island takes those that beat its elites. The nursery keeps all of them. Its archive uses the refined layout, so a new body plan competes only against bodies of its own shape and size, and the nursery holds four times as many bodies as one with a cell per way of moving. Cheney et al. (2018) protect a lineage whose body just changed from selection while its controller readapts, and Hornby's ALPS (2006) lets individuals compete only with others of their own age. Here the protection is a separate archive, so a new body plan meets the island's elites only when it can beat them.

Each nursery has its own screen bar, because one bar stopped 2 in 3 of the nursery bodies that were still standing at 5 s. The slots of the nurseries come out of the old nursery's 10% and 5% more of the island's, so an island keeps 85% of its slots.

Measured on main 11ab504 (the owner's save at generation 1,512, 300k a generation, 40 generations with a world change before generation 1,520, four seeds each, power saver on, and nothing here depends on timing), at generation 1,551 the islands held 15,014 distinct body plans against 13,688 (+9.7%) and all archives 31,544 against 14,670. The global archive held 3,883 plans against 3,724 (+4.3%) and the effective clades of the islands were 1,329 against 1,230 (+8.1%). The island cells held by a body that graduated from a nursery went from 73 to 3,109 of 24,910 (12.5%). At the graduations at generations 1,530, 1,540 and 1,550 the islands took 3,669, 2,358 and 1,879 reshaped bodies and 150, 92 and 64 of the nursery of new random bodies, against 86, 75 and 47 graduates of the one nursery of main. QD was 139,693 against 136,462 (+2.4%) and the best distance 34.8 against 34.1 m. The nurseries cost GPU work, because a reshaped body is as big as the elites it comes from and runs the full 5 s screen, and the early rungs skip it: 296.9 steps per creature against 283.7 (+4.6%). On main c0d9587, where the rungs guard is the older one and the paths of the runs differ from generation 1,516, two seeds gave 14,860 plans in the islands against 13,864 (+7.2%), 31,274 in all archives against 14,833, QD 140,731 against 139,278 (+1.0%) and a best distance of 33.9 against 33.95 m. The peak RSS at 300k was 4.9 GB for both builds, and two runs of one seed agreed at every generation. The same design with a coarse layout for the reshaped nursery and 5% of the slots instead of 10% (three seeds) held 14,141 plans in the islands (+3.3%) and 19,473 in all archives, and 1,253 island cells held a graduate. Its QD was +0.9% and its best distance equal, and it cost 291.8 steps per creature (+2.9%). Its end-to-end rate at 3M on the owner's save was 217k creatures/s against 236k for main (power saver, two clean runs each).

The emitter shares stay at the 35% CMA, 35% structural, 30% novelty prior. A reward-following bandit tied with the fixed shares.

Four islands are fully isolated and a fifth hub island receives copies of each isolated island's fastest tenth every 25 generations. Islands that exchanged their best tenth every 5 generations converged on one design within about 15 generations. At 241 generations one seed reached 847 m with migration every 5 generations, 1,243 m with none and 1,556 m every 25.

An island's archive starts with one cell per way of moving (6 ground contact, 8 cadence, 6 body height on a log scale and 5 feet, 1,440 cells) and keeps its fastest creature there. Body height is logarithmic so small and giant bodies do not share one cell. After 30 generations it is refined: each way of moving gets a cell for each body class, 2 shapes by the start pose's width over height (compact under 1.5, long from 1.5) times 2 sizes by node count (small up to 10, large 11 and more), 5,760 cells, and every elite moves to its class's cell. The global archive never breeds, so it is refined from the first generation and has finer classes: 4 shapes (tall under 0.9, square to 1.4, wide to 2.5, long) times 4 sizes (up to 8 nodes, 9 to 10, 11 to 13, 14 and more), 23,040 cells. A nursery of new random bodies never refines, and a world change keeps the layout of every archive, so the elites tested again in the new world keep the cells of their body classes. A 64-entry morphology reserve per island gives new body plans offspring without counting toward coverage or QD score. In a 5-seed run the reserve admitted 3.8 times as many first-time body plans at equal best distance (112.8 m against 112.0 m).

The classes exist because evolved archives are plateaus. At generation 590 of the owner's game 249 of the 1,440 elites tied the best distance within a millimetre, 1,297 were within 1% of it, and the 400 fastest elites replayed on the GPU to distances within 2 mm of each other. The top distance belongs to the world and many bodies reach it, so the first body to reach it kept its cell and its relatives took the neighbors: the largest clade held 766 of one island's 1,370 cells, 891 body plans filled the 1,440 cells of the global archive, and the 36 effective clades of the owner's save at generation 1,512 were 96 clades in 1,431 cells. A new body plan cannot win a cell on distance there, and one elite per way of moving cannot keep two designs. The classes give each kind of body its own cell.

The islands refine after 30 generations because a refined archive climbs more slowly at first. Refined from the first generation, the islands of one fresh game at 1M creatures a generation reached 32.0 m at generation 40 against 38.5 m for the same seed with one cell per way of moving, and the best of each island was lower (seed 41). A better gait spreads through a coarse archive as its children replace the elites of every body class, and through a refined one a class at a time. Refined later, the climb held: four games continued for 20 or 30 generations from a save at generation 30 or 40 gave a best distance of -1.4%, +2.6%, +4.2% and -1.9% and a movement-grid QD of -2.3%, +3.5%, +1.9% and +0.7% against the same games left coarse, with 3.6 to 3.8 times as many body plans in the islands. At generation 30 the best of a fresh game at 1M has reached 81% to 88% of its value at generation 60, so the refine comes after the steepest part of the climb.

The global archive can have finer classes at no cost to the climb, because nothing breeds from it. On the owner's save at generation 1,512 (500k creatures a generation for 30 generations, seed 38) it held 12,145 body plans against 3,571, 1,165 body types (node and muscle counts) against 712 and 1,003 clades (222 effective) against 299 (73), while the best stayed 34.07 m and the movement-grid QD 42.6k. Classes of 4 by 4 for the global archive alone gave 11,808 plans against 3,429 on the earlier main, and classes of 3 by 3 for every archive 7,069 plans, 4,894 in an island on average (+93%), at a save of 170 MB. A fresh game at 1M for 40 generations on seed 41 held 7,345 plans in the global archive against 602 and reached 40.6 m against 38.5 m with the same island archives. The history's median, worst and mean read the best elite of each way of moving, so they mean what they meant with one elite per way: over all the elites the median of the owner's save would fall from 31 m to 5 to 10 m as the weak cells of the classes fill. The save is the cost: 152 MB against 103 MB for the owner's save, because a global elite takes 3.3 KB. The rate does not suffer: the owner's evolved save at 1M creatures a generation, 8 generations in one pause with power saver on, ran at 143,876 creatures/s on the main before this and 147,915 with it (6.95 and 6.76 s a generation), with a peak resident memory of 9.6 and 10.6 GB, and the archive and breeding seconds of a generation at 500k stayed at 1.1 and 1.3 s. The islands keep 2 by 2 classes for the same reason (3 by 3 classes in every archive cost 67% more save, and `qd::ISLAND_CLASSES` is the constant to change).

A parent of a rare clade is preferred where the distances are level. A clade is the elites that share the oldest recorded ancestor. In the owner's save the effective clades of the global archive fell from 95 to 69 over 25 generations as the largest clade grew from 8% to 14% of its cells, because a body of the largest clade replaced the elite of a class cell wherever its distance was higher. Local competition picks parents in a tournament of 8 elites and cannot tell level elites apart, so a bonus of up to 1 for the elite of a rare clade (1 minus the log of its clade's elites over the log of all of them) decides among them. At the full weight, with no scaling, the effective clades of the islands on the owner's save rose from 44 to 102 on average and the global archive's from 69 to 139 (30 generations, 500k a generation). It slows a climb, because a climbing island has few level elites and the bonus then takes parents from the fast lineage: the islands of a fresh game at 1M had an island QD 27% lower at generation 40 and a best of 35.1 against 38.5 m (seed 41). So the bonus is scaled by twice the share of the island's elites within 10% of its best distance (at most 1) and only counts in a refined island. On the owner's save that kept 78 effective clades in an island (1.8 times) and 222 in the global archive (3 times), and the games continued from generation 40 had a best distance and QD of -0.4% and +0.1% (seed 41) and -7.8% and -1.4% (seed 42) against the games left alone, where the loss of seed 42 was one island's best (40.9 against 47.0 m).

Fresh games at 1M creatures a generation for 60 generations with all of this (seeds 41, 42 and 43, the islands refined at generation 30) reached a best distance of 51.5, 41.5 and 40.3 m against 42.7, 41.0 and 40.3 m for the same seeds on the main before it, and a movement-grid QD of 33.4k, 28.9k and 26.6k against 32.7k, 31.0k and 26.1k. The global archive held 10,346, 11,019 and 10,350 body plans against 653, 720 and 688 and 558, 513 and 674 effective clades against 167, 152 and 138, and an island 2,262, 2,467 and 2,376 body plans on average against 584, 668 and 638. The best distance of a fresh game varies by 10% between trajectories of one seed, so these runs show no loss rather than a gain.

A world change keeps the layout of a refined archive. Starting again with one elite per way of moving threw away the body classes of an evolved archive at every button press and every autochange step: an island of 5,760 elites kept at most 1,440 when its elites were tested again. The archive refills with evolved bodies, so spreading them over classes costs no climb. On the owner's save (500k creatures per generation, all five islands refined), 14 generations after the next autochange step of its schedule (mud from dry to damp) the archives held 5,557 cells, 3,718 body plans and 1,276 clades (effective 536) against 1,424 cells, 951 plans and 339 clades (165) with a coarse restart. The best distance in the new world was 32.43 m against 33.00 m and the movement-grid QD 39.8k against 38.9k. A slope of 8% gave 5,402 cells, 3,471 plans, 1,503 clades (738), 33.65 m and 35.6k against 1,421 cells, 955 plans, 450 clades (246), 33.17 m and 35.6k. The best distances differ by -1.7% and +1.4%, which is the noise of one run. Archive seconds per generation after the change (median of 11 generations) were 0.36 s and 0.52 s against 0.24 s and 0.31 s, and breeding 0.56 s in both runs. In the CPU chain at 3M the same change cost 0.80 s of archive and 2.24 s of breeding per generation, against 0.87 s and 2.41 s with the coarse restart (its stand-in scores refill a plateau, so that restart refined again at once). A save written before the first elite re-enters tells its layout by the elites it holds, so it forgets the layout. Through the same step with the final classes and parents (500k creatures a generation from the owner's save, the world changing before generation 1,520, 21 generations after the change) the global archive held 13,277 body plans against 4,006 on the main before them, 3,595 clades (1,635 effective) against 1,265 (580), and the effective clades of an island were 382 to 560 against 297 to 335. The best distance was 32.9 m against 34.1 m, with the best of each island within 5%, and the movement-grid QD 40.9k against 40.5k.

Every muscle shares one body clock. This gained 32% best distance on every seed in the first test and was the only change that made bodies of 8 or more nodes competitive (the best such body went from 34 m to 62 m).

The CMA emitter explores in normalized units with a large step (sigma 0.12). Each island also runs a separable CMA-ES in physical units on its fastest body plan. That optimizer needed three things to help: every sample got the same check trial (the fine checks are gone, see below), it stays on its body plan instead of chasing each new record, and it starts at half the base step. With them one seed went from 455 m to 958 m at 241 generations. A uniform sigma of 0.03 for the exploring emitter lost 40%.

Whole-body rescaling is a structural mutation, so evolution can grow bodies from 0.25 m bones to giants. Together with the log height axis it took one seed from 304 m to 856 m.

There are 57 anatomy operators and 13 compound operators on top of the 7 classic ones (`docs/anatomy-operators.md`). The first 30 anatomy operators gained 21 to 26% best distance and 16 to 28% QD over 10 seeds at equal evaluations, and the top 50 carried 28% fewer muscles. The owner wants more operator types and never fewer.

A child of the structural emitter gets one operator (the pick tries up to four times for one that fits the body), uniform over the slots: 7 classic, 50 with a slot each (37 anatomy and all 13 compound ones) and 2 shared groups, 59 in all. The parameter mutation after it is 0.035 of the gene scale, and a child of a compound operator does not get it. A compound operator changes several parts of a body together (a mirrored limb pair, a segment repeated with a gradient, a new part with its timing, a limb's whole controller, the gait of another elite).

The compound operators were measured against main with builds that differ only by them. In 16 paired resumes of young archives (100k creatures, 12 generations, 8 saves, 2 seeds each) the QD gain was 67.6% against 69.1%, which is -1.5 points of the starting QD with a standard error of 1.6. The best distance was 22.8 m against 23.4 m, the ring's mean GPU lanes 10.65 against 10.99 and the distinct body plans 594 against 600. The share of the best 300 elites with a mirrored limb pair rose from 8.1% to 10.9% (standard error 1.3). Resuming from saves of generation 8 (12 pairs) and generation 14 (10 pairs) for 12 generations, the end QD ratio was x0.98 (standard error of its log 0.05) and x1.02 (0.03), with the same best distance. Fresh runs of 16 seeds from generation 0 to 30 gave QD x0.98, x0.87 and x0.79 for three builds of the operators against main (best x1.08, x0.87 and x0.85). The standard error of such a ratio is about 0.2, so they neither show a cost nor exclude one in the first 30 generations. A compound child gets no parameter mutation because the audit shows it halves how often the child enters the archive (9.6% to 4.5% on the generation 1510 save, 8.3% to 1.3% on the generation 590 save).

On the owner's generation 1510 save (4 seeds, 12 generations at 300k creatures) the first generation after the load runs with the early screen off and all four seeds gave the same one, so it counts once: it gained 55.7 QD with the compound operators and 71.4 without. On a later build of main the arm with the compound operators gained 65.2 and the arm without 57.0, so the gap reversed. The 11 generations after it gained 15.6 (standard error 1.5) against 7.9 (2.9). Resuming again from the saves that ended those runs (4 of each kind, 6 generations) the arm with the compound operators gained 6.1 QD more in the first generation (standard error 2.8) and 1.5 more over the next five (0.7). In a dump of the next generation (300k children, on main with refined archives) a compound child entered an archive 3.77% of the time against 3.47% for another structural child and added 6.8 m against 6.9 m per 1,000 children. Before the archives refined, at 600k children, it was 0.70% against 0.65% and 5.1 m against 4.0 m. The mean lanes were 15.28 against 15.34, and breeding takes 3% fewer cycles per child on that save and the same on a young archive.

Cross-plan crossover grafts a limb with its muscles and rhythm from an elite of another body plan on the same island. Over 18 seeds it gave QD x1.25 and best distance x1.08.

Early screening stops a standard trial at 5 s when the creature is below the bar, the 5 s distance the top 10% reached. Screened creatures enter no archive. The 5 s distance keeps every creature of the final top 1% and 96% of the final top 10% (Spearman 0.887). At equal time screening gives 56% more best distance and 2.4 times the QD, at a cost of 14% QD at equal evaluations. Letting screened creatures open empty cells tied at equal time and lost per evaluation. The bar of the evolved creatures comes from every result, and each kind of nursery body (the nursery of new random bodies and the nursery of reshaped bodies) has a bar of its own kind. One bar for all of them stopped 2 in 3 of the nursery bodies that were still standing at 5 s, because evolved bodies reach many times what a new body does. The evolved bar is the 5 s distance that the top 10% of every result would have reached if the nursery bodies fell short of it, as most do, so about 11% of the evolved creatures pass, as they did when one bar served all. A bar from the evolved results alone let 10.4% through and cost 6.1% of QD at generation 1,551 (2 seeds). A bar from every result, with the elite-like children of the nursery of reshaped bodies in the pool, cost 2.3%, and the QD of the hub was 4% below main. In the owner's run at generation 1,512 (300k a generation) the best nursery body stayed at 2 to 4 m against 33 m in the island, and the islands kept 4, 0 and 0 of about 4,000 graduates over three graduations. With a bar per kind they kept 21, 23 and 46.

Standard results are final. Only a creature that would set a new record of its island or nursery gets a confirmation trial from the same pose at four times the standard rate, and it keeps the lower distance. The fine checks it replaced (every archive contender, a perturbed pose, four times the rate) took 40 to 45% of GPU time and needed contender rules, cell claims, check sharing and a 12-block decision lag. An earlier measurement found that a check at twice the rate let integrator exploits through: the top 50 kept a median 12% of their distance from an unseen pose against 44% with the four-times check (`search_ab` prints this share).

Trials last 20 s and physics runs at 60 Hz. Elites evolved at 30 Hz kept a median 38% of their distance when replayed at 60 Hz, against 90% for elites evolved at 60 Hz and replayed at 120 Hz.

The search is deterministic for a fixed seed on one GPU. Blocks are absorbed in ring order and bred only at absorption, and each block keeps the settings it was bred with.

## Scoring and replays

The GPU score is final. A replay is recorded by the scoring kernel with a frame output on a slot and queue of its own, so its result is bit for bit the archive score (`gpu_replays_show_the_gpu_score`).

A GPU that fails is reopened and its unfinished units run again with the same creatures, so they give the same results (`a_lost_gpu_is_reopened_and_gives_the_same_results`). A GPU that does not reopen stops evolution. A GPU out of memory keeps its unit, frees idle buffers and retries with fewer units in flight. Buffers above 1 MiB get 25% headroom, which cut peak GPU memory 15%.

Saves hold only the configuration, history, archives, CMA and emitter state, lineage and queued elites. The nurseries of reshaped bodies are left out: they refill from the bodies the islands turn away, and a save with them was 38% larger (109 MB against 79 MB at generation 1,634). A 3M game saves 3.4 MB in 0.06 s where the full population took 1,389 MB and 24 s. Loading breeds the ring from the archives. Autosave is off. An older `qd::VERSION` is turned down by the header before the load starts, except version 53, whose archives load with each elite placed in its cell from its stored descriptor (`qd::OLDEST_LOADABLE`). The lineage of a living elite is saved without its creature, because the archive holds it and loading puts it back, and only the global archive's elites and each island's fastest 10 keep their ancestors, as far back as the lineage tab reads (400). The owner's save at generation 1,512 went from 104.2 MB to 44.8 MB, and it is 100 MB after 20 more generations in which its archives refine (43 MB with the old layout).

## Physics

The physics is a planar articulated tree in reduced coordinates (`docs/physics.md`). Bones stay rigid by construction, so the projection passes, the rebuild and the lift that a point-mass chain needs are gone. Evolution finds every hole in a physics model, and each rule below closes one:

- Hill's force-velocity relation bounds muscle power. Without it the best creature threw itself 19 m in one throw.
- Contacts are solved together at velocity level, and friction can only oppose the slip. Solved one at a time, sticking contacts carried the load of sliding ones and nodes reached 30 m/s.
- The spin cap is 15 rad/s. At 40 rad/s a short end bone whipped into the ground and 2,887 of 5,761 N s of friction pushed a node along its own slip (the kick sled, 408 m). At 15 rad/s the same body went 0.8 m.
- Joint limits are inelastic stops. A spring limit let a joint pass unopposed and return 25 kJ against 4.9 kJ of muscle work.
- Feet are planted against the end pose of the step. Planting against the start pose left 42% of an elite's friction pushing along its slip. Now it is 0.0 N s.
- Muscle force and energy scale with the mass a muscle drives. Before, a 100 N muscle drove a 0.05 kg limb at 2,000 m/s^2 and one elite gained 338 J against 26 J of muscle drain. With the scaling it gains 9 J.
- Only active contraction is charged to the energy store. Charging the damper and stretched muscles too tied within the noise but paid for work no muscle did.
- Bones feel air drag. Tendons store and return energy. With tendons QD was 13,162 against 6,437 and 12% of the muscles of sampled elites carried one.
- The contact solve keeps the 4 deepest contacts per step. Eight contacts with cold sweeps ran 4.1M creature-steps/s, four with warm sweeps and planting rounds ran 22M and one ran 62M.

The CUDA kernel is the only physics. The game is NVIDIA-only, and nothing else simulates creatures, so there is one implementation to change and no engines to keep in agreement.

## Speed

The game runs CUDA (`src/cuda_engine.rs`). It was 1.8 times the Vulkan kernel it replaced on an evolved population and equal on a fresh one. The kernels compile without a register cap, which was 3% faster than a 128 cap.

Kernels compile per world (the effects that are on), lane class, recording and fine physics. A world change used to stall the engine thread for 2 to 5 s per press, 52.8 s over 29 presses at 400k creatures, because the fine and recording kernels compiled on first use and `cuModuleLoadData` waits for the kernels running on the GPU (0.1 to 0.9 s on the engine thread, even from the disk cache). Now background threads compile and load every kernel of the current world (three at a time: scoring, then fine, then recording) and one thread at nice 19 does the same for every world one effect level away, scoring first. The engine adopts what is loaded. Revisited worlds and neighbours that finished wait 0 s. A neighbour pressed before its kernels finished waited 0.8 and 1.3 s, and a jump of several effects (a preset) 1.0 to 1.5 s. The disk cache keeps the 200 newest kernels, and a hit touches its file. The status line says "Compiling the new world" once the wait passes 0.3 s. `kernel_wait_seconds` in the stage log is the engine thread's wait.

Work units of 1 s gave 186,700 creatures/s end to end against 168,600 for 3 s units, with 2.5 GB less peak memory. The worker keeps 8 general threads. Adding 8 CPU evaluation workers beside the GPU lowered the rate from 137,800 to 121,300 creatures/s, before the CPU engine was deleted. Archive refreshes recompute only the cells near a change, which cut the archive stage from 3.4 s to 2.1 s per generation at 3M. The archive keeps its cells in a dense table, its body plans as 64-bit keys (crossover partners, island reserves and CMA emitter slots no longer hash topologies), its neighbor scores in packed rows, and it picks the fastest 512 and the 32 least visited elites without sorting all of them. Archive plus breeding wall seconds per generation at 3M on the owner's save, CPU only with stand-in scores (generations 1,515 to 1,518, two runs each): 5.32 and 5.77 s for the code before the classes, 4.14 and 3.64 s with the same layout and these changes, 4.38 and 3.81 s with the classes. Process CPU seconds per generation: 23.9 and 24.6, 21.5 and 20.2, 22.2 and 20.1 (`examples/archive_bench.rs`). With the plateau rule the archives start coarse and refine in the first two generations (generations 1,512 to 1,515): 4.08 s and 21.1 CPU s. Breeding packs children in batches, and the CPU chain is now under a quarter of a generation, so the GPU is the wall.

The game holds a ring of creatures in flight, not a resident 3M population with a spare arena, which reached 23 GB. Each block is one engine unit handed over without a copy. The ring's shape is an experiment constant (`storage::RingShape`), chosen when a game starts, saved with it and written into every generation's statistics. A shape that followed the measured rate would change a seed's search, so it never changes while a game runs. Today the shape is 8 blocks of 196,608 (1,572,864 creatures in flight): blocks wait on confirmation round trips of 1 to 6 s, and at 3M per generation 8 blocks ran 336k to 507k creatures/s in generations 1 to 10 against 155k to 424k for 4 blocks (peak RSS 6.2 GB). The sizing rule is in the code and takes over by itself once the host time per block measures at 0.2 s or less: a block of 50 ms of GPU work (32k to 256k), and a ring of 5 host times per block or the boundary plus 2 blocks, between 0.3 s and 1 s. It is off because a block asks for about 40 confirmation trials (8 per archive, mostly for the nurseries) whatever its size, so a short ring starves the GPU until confirmations have their own slots. 3M creatures in blocks of 32k asked 800 to 3,900 confirmations per generation against 400 to 1,200 in blocks of 196k, and the p95 host time per block was 1.6 to 3.4 s against a ring of 0.9 s. Side by side on a shared GPU (worker_rate at 3M, generations 3 to 7, 2026-10-01) the old ring gave 43.0k creatures/s and 5 blocks of 32k gave 17.0k, with up to 24 s of GPU idle time per generation. With the short ring a world change threw away at most 163,840 creatures, and two runs of one seed gave one statistics digest over 10 generations. At 1M creatures per generation `worker_rate` peaked at 5.2 GB RSS, and the ring does not grow with the generation size. Long sessions grow bodies, and every creature then costs more. Autosave is off by default.

A ring block breeds into its own gene arena. When a block is absorbed, its `Population` is bred again in place (`Population::breed`): every run of 4,096 children gets a part of the arena sized from what the same positions held last time plus a quarter, and each child is written there once, as it is bred. There is no per-child allocation and no second copy of the genes, and breeding one block into a reused arena makes 0.0002 allocations per child (`breed_bench`). The pack writes into registered host buffers (`HostVec`, `cuMemHostRegister` once) that the GPU copies from, so the per-slot staging copy is gone, and a buffer that outgrows its mapping is extended with `mremap` instead of mapped and faulted in again. The global allocator (`src/block_alloc.rs`) keeps freed blocks of 2 MiB and more for the next block of their size. At 1M creatures per generation `worker_rate` went from 450,000 minor page faults per generation to 50,000 (generations 3 to 8), and 5,000 to 15,000 of those are memory that was given back and faulted in again (the rest is the resident set growing as bodies grow). Breeding went from 0.51 to 0.45 s per generation and the rate did not change. Peak RSS rose from 2.3 GB to about 3 GB. Transparent huge pages cut the faults to a few thousand, but compaction stalled single generations for 2 to 5 s on this machine, so they are not used. Page faults per generation are not under 1,000 while bodies grow, because new arena and lineage memory is new memory.

A block's confirmations used to need two to four rounds. The first goes out when the block's standard results are in and comes back in 0.3 s, but it holds at most 512 candidates per archive, and the rest could only be asked at the front of the ring, one round at a time, because blocks are absorbed in order. On the owner's generation 1,510 save about 2,000 candidates per block tie the archives' 34.07 m plateau and every one fails its fine trial, 33k per generation. A follow-up round took 1.3 s to reach the GPU and come back, and the absorption and breeding after it 0.45 s, so a block cost 1.8 s of host time against 1.0 s of GPU time. The engine ran no unit 24.5% of the time and the game ran 116k and 122k creatures/s. A verdict now remembers how many confirmation results it read per archive (decaying by a quarter per block, not saved) and the next block asks for 512 plus twice that in its first round. When results are in and none raised the record, a later round asks for four times as many as have failed. What a verdict decides does not change. Then 92 of 97 blocks needed one round, the engine was idle 4.6% of the time, and the game ran 171k and 173k creatures/s (four runs alternating the two builds on one save, exclusive GPU).

A creature costs about as much per step as the lanes it holds, so a body runs on the fewest lanes that hold its nodes and its muscles (4, 8, 16 or 32). Evolved bodies grow muscles faster than nodes: on the same save 21% of the ring had 16 nodes or fewer and 65 to 96 muscles (13 nodes and 76 muscles on average) and ran on 32 lanes with half the lanes idle. The 16-lane class now runs 6 muscle rounds (96 muscles) and the other classes 4, so only bodies of 17 nodes or more use 32 lanes: 4% of the creatures and 9% of the kernel time. Shared memory for the per-muscle state is what limits the other classes (24,192 B per block on 16 lanes, 4 blocks per multiprocessor). The 6-round kernel spilled more registers (158 B of spill stores against 124 B) until the loop over muscle rounds was rolled (`#pragma unroll 1`). Together the two changes cut kernel time 12% on 100k ring creatures (ncu, 478.8 ms to 420.7 ms), and a body that moves from 32 to 16 lanes costs 66% of its instructions, not the 45% a model of lane counts gives.

The muscle forces of a round reach the bones through shared memory. Evolved hubs carry 20 to 40 muscle ends over a few rounds, so every lane used to run as many slots as the busiest bone needed (about 12 per round where the average bone has 3), and that loop was 13% of the instructions and 18% of the stall samples on 16 lanes. A lane now adds up at most 4 ends of one bone, the other chunks of a hub go to lanes that carry no ends that round, and the hub's lane adds their sums with shuffles (`warp_kernel::plan_round`). That cut kernel time another 5% (ncu, 100k ring creatures, 420.7 ms to 399.4 ms).

Confirmation trials have a slot of their own on each GPU. They used to share the 4 slots of standard units, so a confirmation unit (about 2,300 creatures of 4,800 steps) waited for a slot to free up and then ran beside three standard units. It now goes to a fifth slot first, on a stream of the highest priority, and replays keep theirs. A stream cannot preempt a running block, so the confirmation kernel starts when blocks of the standard kernels exit: its round trip is 3.6 to 4.3 s at the median with 4 standard slots and 1.1 s with 2. A block asks for its confirmations as soon as its results are in and its turn at the front comes 8 blocks later, so either wait hides behind the other blocks and the engine never ran empty in the trace of any run. On the owner's generation 1,510 save (3M per generation, seed 38, generations 1,515 to 1,518, exclusive GPU, two runs each) 4 standard slots gave 190.2k and 186.1k creatures/s without the confirmation slot against 198.6k and 198.5k with it (+5.5% on the means), and 2 standard slots gave 179.0k and 177.7k against 195.9k and 191.2k. Four slots stay: two hold 3.1 GB of GPU memory and 10.2 GB of host memory against 6.0 GB and 13.0 GB for four, and they are 2.5% slower with the confirmation slot. The statistics digest is the same in all ten runs, so the slot layout does not change the search.

The early rungs arm on a plateau. A rung armed only while the rule that would have been in force stopped at most 3% of the entrants the 5 s screen keeps. On the owner's generation 1,512 save (3M per generation, the world with six effects) the audit lane sees 57 to 93 such entrants per generation and the fitted rule would stop 21 to 27 of them at R1 and 6 to 23 at R2, so both rungs stayed off and 55% of all steps (286 per creature) went to creatures that the 5 s screen then stopped. The entrants it stops are creatures that fall early and improve a niche of weak bodies. The ones that reach the 5 s bar and enter an archive, the creatures the guard exists for, were never stopped (0 of 3 to 30 per generation), and of the creatures above the bar the rule stops 0 to 7 of about 2,200 per generation. Counting the creatures above the bar instead, everywhere, armed the rungs on that save and cost a young game its climb: fresh start, 3M per generation, seed 38, 50 generations, best distance 53.02 m with the entrant guard against 46.75 m (51.8 m at generation 24 against 42.7 m), QD 36,089 against 39,108. It armed one generation earlier and kept R2 armed past generation 13, where the entrant guard turns it off because it stops the entrants the archive grows from. So the entrant guard stays while the archives climb, and the guard on the creatures above the bar (over the last 4 generations, at least 1,000 of them, at most 3% stopped) takes over once the global archive's best has stood for 30 generations. The saved audit state keeps its layout: the two counts of a generation are packed into the two words that held the entrant counts, so an older save reads as it was written.
