# Round 4, GA/QD: red team

Attacks on Design K (items 4, 5, 8), answers on the ladder and the cap (14 to 16), the glitch list (23). Every number below that depends on rung shares is an estimate until the dump exists.

## Design K

### 4. The W = 8 class against screening the tail at R1

The cost arithmetic first, in gpu's own units (executed thread-slots per creature-step): a W = 8 creature is 38,400, a mean W = 2 creature about 9,000. A mean creature's whole trial under the mature ladder is about 300 x 9,000 = 2.7M slots. A tail creature stopped at R1 (60 steps) is 2.3M; one that reaches R3 is 11.5M; one that survives R3 and runs full is 46M, 17 mean trials.

So the question is not "does a 27-node graft child move" but "how far does it get". My expectation from what the operators do: a doubled or grafted body carries 50 muscles at their inherited phases on a skeleton whose lever arms changed, so it spasms. About half fall inside a second (free, the fall ends the trial), a third are stopped at R1, the rest reach R2 or R3, and under 1% survive R3 (no top-300 elite has more than 9 nodes, and the archive's 17-plus entries are reserve entries). Weighted: 0.5 x 30 + 0.3 x 60 + 0.2 x 200 = 73 steps at 38,400 = 2.8M slots per tail creature, one mean trial each. Then the 9% tail costs about 9% of the GPU with the W = 8 class and R1, and about 70% on the lane-group kernel (gpu's own number), which is why the lane-group kernel cannot be the tail's home. The failure mode: if the tail's survivors at R3 are 5% rather than under 1%, the tail costs 0.09 x (0.05 x 46M) / 2.7M = 8% more, still bounded. The number the dump must show: the share of 17-plus offspring alive at step 60 with com_x above 0.05 m (R1 passers), the share alive at step 300, and their `entered` field. If under 30% pass R1 and under 1% pass R3, the W = 8 class plus R1 costs under 10% and the archive-following cap is not needed for rate. If over 60% pass R1, the class costs 20% and a growth rule (item 16) is needed.

Is W = 8 worth building? Yes, but last, to retire the lane-group kernel. Before it exists, the growth-step rule in item 16 shrinks the tail to about 1%, which makes the lane-group fallback bearable in the interim (1% x 8.5 = 8.5% of time). Build order: W = 2, the growth rule, W = 4, W = 8.

### 5. The exploit I would evolve

Three candidates, in the order I would bet on them.

The whip that does not recoil (the drift projection). Positions advance by p += v HS with v exactly on the rod constraints at velocity level. A rod turning at omega stretches by about (omega HS)^2 / 2 per substep: at the spin cap of 15 rad/s and HS = 1/60 that is 3.1% of its length, at 1/120 it is 0.8%. The projection then shortens the rod by that amount once per step, moving both nodes along the rod and leaving velocities alone. Linear momentum is conserved (internal, mass weighted). Kinetic energy is unchanged, so the energy ledger sees nothing. Angular momentum is not conserved: pulling the nodes inward at unchanged velocities shrinks r x m v by the same 3%. A step that removes 3% of a limb's angular momentum without touching energy or linear momentum is a reactionless torque, and a body in ground contact turns reactionless torque into propulsion: swing a limb at the cap, let the projection eat the recoil, keep the trunk's counter-rotation, plant, repeat. It bites at exactly 1 substep, where the stretch is 4x larger than at 2, and it is invisible to the three ledgers as specified (momentum, first law, realized friction work). Detection: a fourth ledger, angular momentum about the centre of mass across every internal operation (rod solve, projection, muscles), with the same pass bar as the momentum ledger (under 1e-5 per step per unit), in physics_audit and in the honesty test as the ratio of elite distance at 1 versus 4 substeps for elites whose limbs reach the spin cap more than 10% of the time. What would have to change: either the projection moves velocities too (a velocity-level correction that conserves L, which costs one more tree solve per step) or the position update rotates rather than translates rods (second-order update), or the spin cap comes down at 1 substep. Physics should say which before L2 runs.

The chattering anchor. The anchor's store is credited with the friction work absorbed on the realized displacement per substep and debited when the anchor returns work. At 1 substep a 1 cm slip is realized as 0.6 m/s within one solve, then the row saturates at the cone and x_a moves; the absorbed work is lambda_t times a displacement the solver produced after clamping. Evolution's move: alternate planted and sliding on successive steps at the contact rate, loading the store at high normal force (the cone allows a large lambda_t) and unloading it at low normal force with a favourable phase. The store bounds the energy, so this is not free energy; it is a ground spring with a timing evolution controls, and springs with controllable timing become tendons that never tire (the tendon at least has a stiffness gene and a mass). Detection: per elite, the anchor's returned work as a share of muscle work (turnover); an elite above 50% is running on the ground spring. Bar: physics_audit reports it, and the honesty test compares elites' distance with anchors on and off at 4 substeps. What changes if it bites: the anchor returns at most a fraction of its store per step, or the store decays with a time constant (the tendon's precedent).

Flight-step limits. A joint limit as a compliant row in a DT step has no restitution only if the solve is fully implicit for that row. Any explicit part at 1/60 s returns energy to a limb slammed into its stop in flight: the 25 kJ spring-limit exploit from design-decisions.md in a new coat. Guard: energy returned by limit rows under 1% of muscle work per elite in physics_audit. Cheap to guard, so third.

### 8. What holds the muscle count, and generation 100

The measured curve on save42 answers the slope: elite muscles mean 4.6, 10.6, 13.9, 17.1, 18.2, 18.8, 19.2 at generations 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 45, 50; p90 6, 18, 24, 34, 26, 27, 28. The p90 peaked at 34 at generation 25 to 30 and fell to 27 to 28 by 40. Muscle mass (0.05 kg plus 1 kg per metre) is what did that; the old build's 4x fall by generation 30 was measured before muscle mass and before the mass-scaled force. Design K's slope is not the old slope shifted; it is flat from generation 30 on for elites. Extrapolation for generation 100 with nothing new: elite mean 21 to 23, p90 30 to 32; offspring mean about the same plus the operators' additions, p90 about 35 (the operators add muscles more often than they remove them, and the dump's operator histogram will say by how much).

Rate at that body: muscles are 50 to 60% of the step, so cost per step scales as 0.45 + 0.55 x m / 19. At mean 22 that is 1.09x; at the chair's hypothetical mean 25 and p90 40 it is 1.17x on the mean body and 1.6x on the p90 body, a population rate of about 0.8x of generation 51, not 0.25x. The physics holds elites, which is measured. Nothing holds offspring, and the growth-step rule of item 16 (at most 4 muscles per child over its parent) holds them near their parents. Detection over a long run: a ring-offspring histogram per generation (mean and p90 of nodes and muscles, one line in EVOLUTION_STAGE_LOG) is the sustained-rate gauge and costs nothing.

## The ladder and the cap

### 14. Is the circuit breaker fast enough for the nursery?

No, and the nursery should be exempt from R1 and R2. The nursery is the one place where slow starters are the point: fresh random bodies and their children for 10 generations, competing only against each other. R1's discriminant is fit on the audit lane's rows, of which nursery rows are 10% (about 2,300 per generation at 1 in 128), and its 1-in-1,000 miss budget is measured against passers of the bar, which for the nursery is its own arena bar. A per-cadence-band breaker at 3 generations of 100 misses per 10k stops a class for 3 of the cohort's 10 generations before anything notices, and the audit lane's 1 in 128 is the only leak. That is a third of the development window.

The cost of the exemption: nursery slots are 10% of creatures; without R1 and R2 they run to R3 (300 steps) unless they fall, and random bodies fall a lot. Estimate 0.1 x (300 - 120) = 18 steps per creature, 6 to 8% at maturity. If the dump shows the nursery's steps share above 8%, a second version gives the nursery its own discriminant with a breaker at one generation. For R1 to be safe for nurseries without exemption the dump must show that among rows with `entered = 2` under 0.1% would have been stopped by the global rules in every cadence band. I expect the long-period bands to fail that.

### 15. Does an emitter whose whole population is stopped at R4 drift toward sprinters?

In principle yes, in practice it is bounded and measurable. The emitter tells on d(10) for all 200 samples, so its rank is a 10 s rank. Among the whole population 5 s and 20 s distances have Spearman 0.887; among survivors 10 s and 20 s will be higher (my guess 0.95). The residual is a bias toward gaits that are fast early. Two things bound it. The condition is that every child of the emitter is on pace below the weakest neighbouring record: an emitter in that state has nothing to contribute to the archive and `converged()` restarts it. And the moment a child beats the neighbourhood it runs full and enters the tell on its 20 s distance with the improvement key on top, so the update is pulled by full-trial results whenever there are any. The drift acts only while the emitter is entirely losing.

Does it show within 40 generations of search_ab? Not in best distance or QD, so those gates would miss it. The signal is the front-loading of entrants: mean final / d(10) among archive entrants, 2.0 for a steady gait, lower for a sprinter that fades. Bar: under R4 that mean may not fall more than 5% against control over 40 generations, per island; it comes from the elite table's stored d(600), so it is free. If it fails, the fix is to rank stopped children by d(10) x r_10, R4's own scale, so stopped and full children are comparable.

### 16. Does the body cap kill the reserve's purpose?

Yes, partly, and I withdraw it in favour of a rule that only bounds growth per child. The reserve's purpose is that a new body plan gets offspring without competing on distance, so it can grow into a competitor. A cap at the island's largest behaviour elite plus two holds a 12-node reserve plan on a 9-node island to children of 11 nodes: it cannot grow, so the reserve keeps plans it cannot develop. That is the reserve's purpose lost for exactly the plans it exists for.

The alternative: a growth-step rule, a child gains at most 4 nodes and 4 muscles over its parent. It is a limit on an operator's step, like the bone length cap is a limit on a gene, not a limit tied to the archive. Every operator stays; a branch copy or a trunk repeat fits only when the copied part is at most 4 nodes, and the picker retries up to 4 times as today. A lineage can reach 32 nodes in 6 generations through the reserve, each step alive. The tail's clusters at 25, 29 and 30 nodes are the signature of doubling operators on 12 to 15 node parents; under the rule they disappear and the 17-plus share falls from 9% to about 1% (bodies that grew there in steps). Combined with R1 the tail then costs about 1% of the GPU. The screen-only alternative with no rule at all is R1 plus the W = 8 class: about 9% of the GPU by item 4, which is the price of not deciding; it is acceptable until W = 8 exists only if the interim lane-group fallback carries 1%, not 9%, so the growth rule goes in first either way. Measurement: the offspring node and muscle histogram from a generation-50 ring before and after; search_ab at equal time, 10 seeds, best not below 0.97x and reserve admissions per generation not below control. The owner decides the rule as a body limit.

## 23. Glitch risk, my domain

- Per-arena, per-cell and streaming bars, R2, R3: they only stop trials; they cannot admit anything. Risk is misses, guarded by the audit lane. No gap.
- R4: same; the sprinter bias in item 15 is a selection bias, not a glitch, guarded by the front-loading bar.
- 1,024 emitters and 20 optimizers: ten times the local search intensity on the fastest designs. The 4x same-pose confirmation still guards integrator exploits on records. The gap: pose-fragile gaits (docs: the top 50 kept 12% of their distance from an unseen pose with the 2x check, 44% with the 4x check) will be found faster by more optimizers, and no current guard measures them. `search_ab` prints the share kept from an unseen pose; the bar I propose is that it does not fall below the current game's under the new emitters. Whether pose fragility is a glitch is the owner's posture call.
- u16 muscle genes: a gait tuned to a quantization edge. Guard: the f32 rescore of the top 300 at ratio 0.99. Gap: none if the rescore holds.
- Tells on truncated distances (R3 and R4): a search bias, guarded as in item 15.
- Growth-step rule, counter RNG, 12-uniform gaussian, nursery exemption: no glitch surface.
- The archive-following cap: withdrawn.

## One defence

gpu's round 3 treats the cap as the lever that gives back the tail's 20%. The growth-step rule gives back the same 20% (the tail goes to 1%) without tying the search to the archive's incumbents, and it is one predicate in the operators' fit check. The number that would make gpu right and me wrong: if the dump shows the 17-plus tail comes mostly from parents already above 13 nodes (lineages that grew in steps), the growth rule does nothing and the cap is the only rule that helps. Then the choice is the cap or 9% of the GPU, and I would take the 9%.
