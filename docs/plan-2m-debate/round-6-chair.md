# Round 6, chair: revision after phase 0 (gpu, physics, data, cpu)

Theme: where the instructions go and what removes them. Phase 0 refuted the plan's largest lever. The numbers are on origin/main in docs/plan-2m-measurements.md and docs/building.md ("Power rows"); the stub is examples/lane_stub.rs and shaders/lane_stub.cu (1,177 lines, with developer switches NO_MUSCLES, NO_CONTACT, NO_DAMP, NO_FRICTION, NO_LEDGER, USE_STASH, MSTATE_REGS, MUSCLE_UNROLL, and a TRACE mode). Read them before writing. Write arch/round-6-<domain>.md, under 2,000 words, with a revised multiplier for every lever you own and a revised ceiling. 25 minutes.

## What was measured

1. The W = 2 per-lane maximal-coordinate stub (8-node, 19-muscle synthetic bodies, 2 substeps): 1,160 warp instructions per creature-step against the debate's estimate of 256 (gpu round 3: 255 at W = 2). 72.9M creature-steps/s at 1.89 GHz locked, about 96M at boost; the gate was 250M. Issue-active 47.9% at 14.9 warps (gate met). 128 registers with a 424 B stack frame and 614 B of spill stores (gate missed: 32 B). Occupancy does not move the rate: 16, 12 and 8 warps give 1,160, 1,142 and 1,074 instructions per step at nearly equal kernel time. Breakdown: muscles 16%, Delassus assembly 15%, active-set solve 14%, tree batch A 10.5%, projection 5.5%, forces 5%, contacts 5%. LOP3 is 20% of all instructions, from runtime rod-topology bit tests and packing. Stalls: long scoreboard 31%, wait 27%, short scoreboard 14%, no-instruction 12%. Joint-limit and spin rows are not in the stub, so the real kernel costs more.
2. The lane-group kernel's warp-state sample (Nsight Compute on the save42 population): issue-active 61.5 to 62.8% at 15.5 warps; barrier under 0.5%, short scoreboard 18%, wait 33%, long scoreboard 17.5%, branch resolving 7%. The debate assumed 20 to 32%. After the contact-solve round it runs 53 to 55M on the generation-10 dump (1.22x), issue slots 53 to 60% busy, one eligible warp per scheduler, a fifth of instructions are control flow, and the muscle section stalls on global record loads. Projected Jacobi was rejected: random bodies gained 2.1 m.
3. Host, after host-bounded: breeding 10,957 cycles per child (parametric 8,084, structural 17,260). Archive stage 55 ms median per 196k block (30 ms at 100k), boundary at most 41 ms, candidates 2.1%, tells 4.5 per block. The serial chain today is about 290 ms per 196k block.
4. Power, under the shared lock (the exclusive rows are still owed): the register-dense FMA probe reached 2.64 warp instructions per SM per clock of the 4.0 peak and hit the 100 W cap (39% of samples at the SW power cap, clock down to 1,965 MHz, 0.55 nJ per warp instruction all-in with another process on the GPU). The MIO probe issued only 0.36 warp instructions per SM per clock at full clock and 61 W: 0.18 shared loads and shuffles per SM per clock. An MIO-heavy kernel is bound by the MIO pipe long before it is bound by power.

## What this does to the plan as written

The per-lane layout is worth about 3x at mature bodies (96M against the lane-group's 27M on the generation-51 mix), not 8.9x to 14.8x. The debate's kernel row rested on 256 warp instructions per creature-step; the stub executes 4.5x that, and it is not occupancy-bound, so the issue-rate argument (30% to 45%) was the wrong argument: the lane-group kernel already issues at 62% and the per-lane stub at 48%, and both are limited by instruction count. The plan's compound table drops accordingly: with everything else unchanged, 2 substeps and R1 to R3 give about 0.3M/s at generation 51 and the best row (L2.5, R1 to R4) about 0.6M/s. Sections 2, 3, 4 and the phase-2 tracks of docs/plan-2m.md will be rewritten from this round.

The host numbers also cross two triggers of ruling 11: the parametric row (8,084) is above 3,000, which opens recipes on the RTX; the structural row (17,260) is above 15,000, which opens the structural port question. Both are before host-vector and host-order, which were to bring them to 2,500 and 7,000.

## Questions

Q1 (gpu, physics, data). Is the general-tree runtime topology the cost? The stub reads a rod's pivot, parent, kept siblings and grandparent from a packed topology word every time it touches a rod, and predicates every tree update on lane and level; LOP3 alone is 20% of instructions and the Delassus assembly plus the two tree batches are 40%. Would kernels specialized per body topology class remove it: NVRTC compiles one kernel per common body plan with the tree baked in as constants (rod parents, the clique structure, the elimination order, the muscle end nodes), bodies are bucketed by plan at submit, and a warp runs one plan. The rejected-ideas file records that a kernel specialized for one body plan was 10 to 20% faster on a single-plan population and gained nothing in mixed units, on the old kernel; say why this would differ (the old kernel's cost was not topology bit tests) or why it would not. How many plan classes cover 90% of a mature ring? ga's save42 numbers: elites 8.6 nodes mean, ring offspring p50 7 and p90 12; the `species` history records (nodes, muscles, count) per generation and the archive holds about 7,500 elites, so the count of distinct bone trees (topology up to canonical bone order) is measurable from a save. Give an estimate and the measurement. What does a per-plan kernel cost in compile count (worlds times plans times classes), cache size, and cold-world idle? Is there a middle path: topology baked per class of (node count, depth, branching pattern) rather than per exact plan, with the muscle end lists still runtime?

Q2 (physics, gpu). The direct active-set solve costs about 680 instructions per round (Delassus assembly 15% plus the solve 14% of 1,160 at 2 substeps, about 340 per substep) and the warp runs the maximum round count. The lane-group's Gauss-Seidel (4 sweeps plus a clean sweep on at most 4 contacts, 16 to 20% of its step) costs about 200 warp instructions per substep and was measured to hold elites at 2 substeps (median ratio 1.03). Is the direct solve worth it, and for what: the 1-substep honesty (exactly planted feet at velocity level) or nothing measurable? What is the cheapest solve that keeps the ledgers: PGS on the Delassus rows without forming the full matrix, PGS with the warm start, or a 2-round active set capped at MAX_ROUNDS 1? Give the instruction count of each and the honesty risk. Projected Jacobi is dead (random bodies gained 2.1 m); say why in one sentence so nobody retries it.

Q3 (physics, gpu). What is the real instruction floor per creature-step for this physics, counted from the stub's breakdown rather than from a design table? Take the stub's 1,160 at 2 substeps for 8 nodes and 19 muscles and say, section by section, what is inherent (the muscle model at 19 muscles: the waveform, Hill, energy, tendon, four force accumulations; the rod solve; four contact rows; the ledgers; the integration) and what is overhead (topology bits, predication, packing, redundant lane work, spills, the stash). Then the same floor for the lane-group kernel's reduced-coordinate step. State both at 1 substep and at spin-adaptive substeps. The plan will carry your floor, not the debate's 256.

Q4 (gpu, data, physics). Given the lane-group kernel already issues at 62% with barrier stalls under 0.5%, is the lane-group layout plus instruction trimming the better path? Its known waste: a fifth of instructions are control flow, the muscle section stalls on global record loads (the split record was designed for per-lane; it applies here too), every tree pass pays the full per-level cost for all lanes at every level (lane use 25 to 30%), and 430 MIO instructions per warp-substep against an MIO pipe measured at 0.18 issues per SM per clock. Quantify: if the MIO pipe is the wall for the lane-group kernel, what is its MIO share now and what rate does removing the tree-level barriers and half the shuffles give? Which of the per-lane stub's lessons transfer (the split muscle record in L2, the muscle state as one word, the batched tree solve, the topology word)? Compare three paths with a number each at the generation-51 mix and 2 substeps: (a) lane-group kernel plus trimming, (b) per-lane general-tree kernel as stubbed with the overhead removed that can be removed, (c) per-plan specialized kernels in either layout. Say which you back and why, and what the stub or a 2-day experiment on the lane-group kernel would show first.

Q5 (cpu, data). The host rows crossed the triggers. With parametric at 8,084 and structural at 17,260 after host-bounded, and host-vector and host-order still to come: what do you now expect them to reach, and if the parametric row stays above 3,000, do recipes on the RTX open now (data's insurance) or after host-vector? If the structural row stays above 15,000, is the structural port on the RTX scheduled, or the SoA operator rewrite, or the frequency cap alone? Give the decision rule with the date. Also: the archive stage measured 55 ms per 196k and the boundary 41 ms, both under their gates; say what that changes in the ring floor and the candidate-bit track.

Q6 (everyone). The power probe says a register-dense kernel hits the cap at about 2.64 warp instructions per SM per clock and 1,965 MHz, and an MIO-heavy one is pipe-bound at 0.18 MIO issues per SM per clock. Restate your kernel's rate at those two limits: the executed warp instructions per creature-step divided into 24 SMs times 2.64 per clock times 1.965 GHz (about 124 G warp instructions/s) for the FMA-bound case, and the MIO instructions per creature-step divided into 24 times 0.18 times 2.49 GHz (about 10.8 G MIO issues/s) for the MIO-bound case; the rate is the smaller. Give the number for your path.
