# Round 4, chair: red team

Theme: attack the two strongest designs. For each attack: the failure mode, the number at which it bites, how it would be detected, and what would have to change. Write arch/round-4-<domain>.md, under 2,500 words. Attack outside your own domain; defend your own only where an attack below is wrong, in one paragraph each.

Round 3 converged on one pipeline. Every file lands on the same table: 2 substeps with R1 to R3 gives 0.9 to 1.5M/s; 2M/s sustained at generation 51 needs 1 substep, or the full ladder plus the 5T budget plus the body cap. The two designs to attack are the two halves that carry the risk:

Design K, the kernel (gpu round 3 plus physics round 3). One maximal-coordinate source templated on W, each lane owning 4 nodes and up to 16 muscles; W = 2 for up to 8 nodes (70% of offspring), W = 4 for 9 to 16 (21%), W = 8 for 17 to 32 (9%); no W = 1; the lane-group kernel retired after T2. Chordal rod LDL, up to 12 extra rows (contacts, joint limits, spin) with a direct active-set solve re-solved at most 3 times, drift projection once per step, three ledgers, anchored friction with an energy store, flight steps at 1 substep, the lagged matrix, warm-started active set. Registers about 140 at W = 2 under a 128 launch bound (10 to 15 spilled), 115 at W = 8; shared about 150 B per lane. Muscle record float32 split into 32 B per step and 24 B per substep; L2 traffic 0.7 to 0.8 TB/s. Take-up unpack in the kernel with pinned rounding intrinsics, gate bit-equal on 262k. DMA per block into VRAM (gpu) against zero-copy reads at take-up (data). Stub gate: at most 128 registers with at most 32 B spill, at least 45% issue-active at 16 warps, extrapolated 250M creature-steps/s on the mean body at 2 substeps. Rates: W = 2 365 to 610M, W = 4 190 to 320M, W = 8 78 to 130M; harmonic mean 240 to 400M with the tail, 300 to 500M with the cap; times 1.85 at 1 substep. Substep ladder L0 to L3 with a 0.9 median honesty bar, physics's odds 15, 30, 50%. In-kernel confirmation of island-record candidates at 4x rate by the same lane group (0.3% of GPU time), which drops the ring to its 0.3 s floor. The three cuts (waveform once per step, ledger in flight, lagged matrix).

Design P, the pipeline (data round 3 plus cpu round 3 plus the ga and ml ladder). Breeding on the CPU: bounded arrays, counter-based RNG (splitmix, 12-uniform gaussian), vector parametric path at about 2,000 cycles per child, operator-sorted and parent-major structural path at 6,000 to 12,000, plan at 600, commit on candidates at 500; 18.4 G cycles per 3M, 3.1 to 3.6 cores busy at 2M/s, host tax 7 to 9% at the cap. Genes written once into pinned SoA arenas (1.35 KB mean, 6 KB for the tail; 2.7 GB/s up at 2M/s). Persistent per-class kernels taking refs from device queues through an atomic head with a host-written tail, bucketed by muscle count in widths of 4 (1.15 to 1.25x, testable this week on the lane-group kernel), starvation spin on a mapped word, drain flags, block slots split 92 to 4 by class share with re-split on a 10-point move, 4 reserved slots (3 confirmation, 1 replay with background pre-recording of new records). Results ring in pinned memory at 80 B with fp16 rung distances in the 7 free floats, descriptor bins and a candidate bit against a CPU-written occupant table (superset argument), completion words per block. Ring depth clamp(5 x p95 host chain, 0.3 s, 1 s); blocks of 50 ms, floor 32k, cap 256k; no waves. Versioned elite slab so breed(k+1) overlaps absorb(k); 1,024 CMA emitters with tells per 200 samples in ring order; per-arena and per-cell bars in a 173 KB per-block table; R1 (Fisher discriminant on 6 features, 1 in 1,000 miss budget), R2 (7 features with the neighbourhood bar), R3 (per-cell bar with calibrated factor), R4 (54-cell neighbourhood minimum, r4 at the 99.9th percentile, optimizers exempt); the audit lane at 1 in 128 by hash; per-cadence-band circuit breaker; the fit at the generation boundary on an 8-generation window; the archive-following body cap (largest elite plus two per island); the streaming arena bar per block. World change: stop appending, drain, requeue, results of live creatures excluded; loss under 10k creatures. Replay 20 to 30 ms.

## Attacks the chair wants answered, by target

Design K:

1. Registers. 140 needed under a 128 bound is a spill by construction. The old per-thread kernel died of spills (243 to 255 registers, up to 4.5 KB local). Who says 10 to 15 spilled registers at W = 2 stay in L1 and cost under 5%? What is the number if the spill is 64 B per lane? At what spill does the stub gate fail and W = 2 fall to 12 warps, and what does that do to the 365M?
2. Issue rate. 45% issue-active at 16 warps is unmeasured for a kernel with shared-memory muscle scatter, an active-set loop whose round count is the warp maximum, and PCIe or L2 reads at take-up. The lane-group kernel measures about 20 to 32%. Name the mechanism that gets from 30% to 45% and the number at which the whole table drops a row.
3. Warp maximum. Every loop bound is the warp's maximum: node count within the class, muscle count (up to 16 per lane), contact rows (up to 12), active-set re-solves (up to 3), flight or ground. data's bucketing fixes muscle count only. What is the expected cost of the others at generation 51, and does the 15 to 25% divergence allowance cover it?
4. The 9% tail at W = 8. 1,200 warp instructions per creature-step for 27 nodes and 50 muscles, 78 to 130M. If the cap is refused by the owner, the tail costs 20% of the rate for bodies that never enter the top 300. Is a W = 8 class worth building at all, or should the tail be screened at R1 by construction (a 27-node random graft child does not move)? What does the dump have to show?
5. Physics honesty. Anchored friction stores energy and returns it; the store is bounded by absorbed work, but the absorbed work is measured on the realized displacement, which is the quantity the integrator gets wrong at 1 substep. Can evolution pump the anchor (plant, load the store through a large friction impulse, release with a favorable phase)? The drift projection moves positions without velocities: at 60 Hz with 1 substep, a rod that stretched 1% under a 100 N muscle pull is projected back, which is a position jump without a velocity change; is that a free energy source the ledger misses, and at what step size does it bite? Flight steps at DT with joint limits as compliant rows: what is the stiffest row and is it stable at 1/60 s? Name the exploit you would evolve.
6. The substep ladder odds. physics gives L2 a 50% pass. If L2 fails, every design falls to 0.9 to 1.5M/s and the owner's 2M/s is not reached on this laptop without the 5T budget. Is there a rung between L2 and "2 substeps" (for example 1 substep for W = 2 bodies and 2 for larger, or 1 substep on ground steps and 2 on the first contact step) that keeps most of the 1.85x with a smaller honesty risk?
7. In-kernel confirmation. The re-run at 4x rate inside the scoring kernel needs the substep length as a runtime parameter (a second compiled variant today), doubles the live time of a record candidate on its lanes (4,800 steps at 4x cost), and reads the island record from a table that changes under it. Determinism: the decision to confirm depends on the table version at take-up, which depends on timing; the host still checks, so the score is invariant, but is the GPU work invariant, and does a stale table ever skip a confirmation the verdict needs (the cascade case)? Is it worth the complexity against data's 3 reserved slots?
8. Sustained across generations. Muscles are 50 to 60% of the step. At generation 51 the p90 is 29 muscles. What holds the muscle count, and what is the rate at generation 100 if the mean goes to 25 and the p90 to 40? The old build fell 4x by generation 30; does Design K have the same slope, only shifted?
9. Kernel count. Per world (14 effects on or off, compiled per set), per class (3), record or not, 60 Hz and 4x: the prefetch thread compiles 1 to 2 s each. On a world change the drain waits for a cold compile; data says 1 to 2 s of GPU idle. How many variants exist, is the disk cache enough, and what does the player see?

Design P:

10. Zero-copy against DMA. data reads genes over PCIe at take-up (40 to 50 loads at 1 to 2 us, pipelined, 10 to 30 us per creature); gpu wants a DMA per block into VRAM. Which one, and what breaks the other: PCIe latency at take-up stalling a warp of 32 creatures that each take up at different times, or VRAM budget and a copy engine that competes with the results ring? Name the measurement that decides it in a day.
11. Persistent kernels and the desktop watchdog. A persistent kernel on the RTX holds 92 block slots forever; the replay and confirmation slots are the only free ones. The RTX does not drive the display, so no watchdog; but a driver reset, a CUDA error or an out-of-memory in the owner's other GPU work (agents share the GPU) now kills a kernel that owns the whole ring. What is the recovery path, and what does the game do in the 1 to 2 s a kernel restart takes? Does the persistent design survive the owner's rule that agents run GPU work beside the game?
12. Starvation and the mapped tail. The kernel spins on a host-written word over PCIe when the queue is empty. At 2M/s and a 100 ms host chain a single late block (a save, a generation boundary of 300 ms, a snapshot) starves 9,216 lanes. data sizes the ring at 5x the p95 chain, but the generation boundary is unmeasured and the archive stage on a mature save could not be measured (save42 is version 42, main is 43, the disk is at 98% with 9 GB free). What is the boundary's cost on a mature archive with 1,024 emitters and 5,000 tells per generation, and does the ring cover it?
13. The host tax. cpu's own number is 3.1 to 3.6 busy cores and 7 to 9%, above the 2 to 3 cores every table assumed, before breed_bench exists and before the structural rows are measured (6,000 to 12,000 cycles is a 2x range). What is the tax at 12,000, and at what point does the insurance (recipes on the RTX) have to be built to keep the host under 3 cores? Who owns the decision and when is it made?
14. The ladder's honesty. R1 stops a creature at 1 s on six features fit to last generation's audit rows. The circuit breaker per cadence band fires after 3 generations of 100 misses per 10k. A lineage that starts slow (a new body plan in the nursery, a long-period gait) is stopped for 3 generations before the breaker notices, and its audit-lane children (1 in 128) are the only ones that get through. Is that fast enough for the nursery's 10-generation window? What does the dump have to show for R1 to be safe for nurseries, and should the nursery be exempt from R1 and R2?
15. R4 and the CMA. Exploring emitters' children stopped at R4 enter the tell with a 10 s distance. ga says the top half of the ranking is unchanged; ml says the same. But an emitter whose whole population is stopped at R4 (a mature cell where every child is below the neighbourhood minimum) tells on 10 s distances for all 200 samples, and its covariance adapts to "fast at 10 s". Is that a drift toward sprinters, and does it show in search_ab within 40 generations?
16. The body cap. The largest elite plus two per island is a body limit. Immigrants and nursery bodies are exempt; the reserve is not mentioned. A new body plan needs to grow to compete; the cap holds it to the island's incumbent size plus two. Does the cap kill the morphology reserve's purpose, and what is the alternative that only screens (R1 by construction) rather than limits?
17. fp16 rung distances, a changed Result, and saves. The Result layout changes meaning of 7 floats; qd::VERSION bumps; saves grow by up to 16 MB for the audit window. Is anything in the UI, replay_match, physics_audit or size_report reading the reinterpreted floats? What breaks silently?
18. World change with rungs. The rungs disarm on a world change and rearm after 8k audit rows (a quarter generation). During that quarter generation every creature runs to the 5 s bar or 20 s: the rate drops to the 480-step rate, 1.5x slower, for about 2 s at 2M/s. Fine. But the per-cell bars are off until re-tested elites have new rung distances, which is "at most one generation". Is the game 1.6x slower for a whole generation after every button press, and does the player see that as a stall?
19. Determinism of the bucketed queues. A lane takes only from its warp's bucket, buckets run dry at different times, warps retire and the launch is re-split from the next block's histogram. Results are still addressed by index, so scores are invariant. But the set of creatures live at a world change is now a function of bucket timing; that is allowed. Is there any path where a creature is taken twice or never (a ref between head and tail when a warp retires)?
20. 60 FPS. 14 rayon threads at nice 10 plus a worker plus the CUDA engine thread plus egui plus the compositor on 16 hardware threads; the pool runs 3 ms chunks; the UI thread has a 1 ms slice. Nobody has measured frame time under the new breeder. What is the failure mode that survives all of that (a 25 ms commit holding the snapshot mutex, a par_sort of 100k keys on the worker, a 300 ms generation boundary), and what is the gate?

Cross-cutting, everyone:

21. The 100 W budget. os's table cannot run until after the debate. Every row of every table is stated at 3T and 5T. Which single measurement, in order, most reduces the uncertainty, and what is the plan if the FMA calibration lands at 3T: is 2M/s then off the table on this laptop unless 1 substep passes?
22. The desktop. The Radeon does UI only; the RTX is compute only. What in Design P touches the desktop's safety at all (the persistent kernels do not; the UI textures on the Radeon are egui work)? If nothing, say so in one line so the plan can close the item.
23. Glitch risk in one list: every change in K and P that could let an exploit into an archive, with the guard that catches it (ledger, confirmation, audit lane, first_generation, physics_audit) and the gap if any. The owner's spirit is natural selection of non-glitchy movers.
24. Disk. The machine is at 98% with 9 GB free; a target directory is 4.5 GB. The plan has 6 to 8 parallel worktrees. What does the plan need from the owner before any track starts?
