# Round 2, CPU domain: answers, attacks, and what breaks my own plan

## D3.1 Reconciling os's 350k cycles per creature with my 73k

Both numbers are right and they measure different things. os ran search_ab for one generation of 262k on the 09-28 build. That run does not breed 262k children. On start the ring breeds every slot, RING_SLOTS = 786k, three children per creature evaluated, and the first block's creatures are random bodies through `initial` plus `repair`. Breeding alone in that run is 786k x 12.5 us = 9.8 s of thread time, 37 us per evaluated creature. Add the NVRTC compile (one to two seconds of one thread, 20 to 35k cycles per creature at this population), the old engine's three segment repacks, 170k page faults, and rayon's spin at the end of every `par_chunks` fork-join. That accounts for the 350k. The steady-state number is what the game's own stage timers give at 3M: breeding 12.5 us plus packing 4.7 us plus archive about 2 us of thread time, 19 to 20 us, 75 to 80k cycles at 4 GHz. The CPU chain's real ceiling today: 8 threads at 100% over 19 to 25 us is 320 to 420k creatures/s, and the ring-order latency (the worker's serial path per block, 0.35 to 0.45 s) gives 300 to 440k/s. data derived 340 to 420k/s independently. I keep 300 to 440k/s and still want the null-engine run to make it one number.

## D3.2 The fixed-array rewrite: size and whether any operator's output changes

The code: evolution.rs 2,325 lines, anatomy 4,566 lines, 6,891 total. Container sites across both: 158 `Vec<` in signatures and locals, 28 `vec!`, 129 `collect`, 41 `clone`, 80 `push`, 14 `extend`, 6 `retain`, 6 `remove`, 3 `insert`, 2 hash maps. RNG call sites: 160, of which 23 are gaussians.

The rewrite: one bounded array type of about 150 lines (`Bounded<T, N>` with `Deref<Target=[T]>`, push, insert, remove, retain, extend, truncate, swap), so slice code (indexing, iteration, `len`, `iter_mut`) compiles unchanged. `Creature` becomes `Bounded<NodeGene, 32>`, `Bounded<Bone, 32>`, `Bounded<Muscle, 96>`, and clone copies only `len` elements. Config caps bodies at 32 nodes and 96 muscles already (config.rs lines 105 and 106) and `class_of` refuses anything larger, so the capacities are the game's own limits and no body that exists today is affected. The temporaries inside operators (the 129 collects are mostly candidate index lists) become `Bounded<u8, 32>` or stack arrays. Estimate: 400 to 600 lines of one-line mechanical edits in the operators, 150 lines for the type, 100 lines for the counter-based RNG, 400 lines for the vector parametric path. About 1,200 lines touched or added out of 6,900; no operator's logic line changes. Two weeks for one person including the property tests.

Output changes: from the containers, none, because the logic and the limits are the same. From the RNG, every child changes, because the stream changes; that is the "new seed" question ga must answer. One edge: `repair` caps nodes at `min(64)` while the config cap is 32, so a body of 33 to 64 nodes is today repaired and then refused by pack; with fixed arrays the operator's "does not fit, try again" path fires instead. That only affects bodies pack already rejects. The operator count stays 64. Nothing is removed.

Where the 4,700-cycle estimate breaks: if the structural operators are dominated by donor reads and tree walks rather than by allocation, the scalar path lands at 15 to 20k cycles instead of 8k and the weighted cost is 8 to 10k. The gate is the `breed_bench` number after the container rewrite alone, before the vector path: if a structural child is above 12k cycles, data's RTX port of the operators is the better plan (section D3.5), and I say so now rather than after the vector path.

## D3.4 One gaussian rule

I withdraw the polynomial Box-Muller with pinned FMA. Three compilers (rustc, NVRTC, ACO) each get one chance per transcendental to differ in the last bit, and the twin test would be permanent maintenance. I take igpu's rule: integer-exact.

The rule: the uniform is a 64-bit hash of (seed, generation, slot, gene index, draw index) with a splitmix finalizer (integer ops only). A gaussian is the sum of twelve 16-bit uniforms taken from three such hashes, minus 6 x 65535, converted once with `as f32` (one rounding) and multiplied by one constant (one rounding). Every step is an integer op or a single IEEE rounding, so Rust, CUDA and GLSL give the same bits by construction, and no test against a twin is needed. Cost: three hashes at about 10 integer ops each plus twelve adds and two float ops, about 45 integer instructions per gaussian; on 16 AVX-512 lanes (two 256-bit halves) that is about 3 cycles per gaussian, 160 gaussians per parametric child, about 500 cycles, which is within the 1,500-cycle path estimate. On a GPU lane it is 45 integer instructions, half-rate on Ada, 90 issue slots per gaussian. The distribution is Irwin-Hall with 12 terms: variance exactly 1, kurtosis 2.9 against 3.0, tails cut at 6 sigma (2 in 10^9 of draws). Every mutation here already passes through a clamp, and CMA-ES does not depend on the sampler's fourth moment. ga must confirm that a sum-of-uniforms gaussian and a changed stream count as a new seed and not a changed search. If ga refuses the kurtosis change, the fallback is a 32-term sum (kurtosis 2.96, 8 hashes, about 8 cycles per gaussian), still integer-exact.

## D3.5 Attack: data's RTX breeding, with the CPU rewrite as the baseline

Take my rewrite as true: 3.5 core-seconds per 3M. At 2M/s that is 2.3 cores busy all the time. Zen 4 cores under mixed scalar and vector load at the all-core clock draw about 4 to 6 W each, so 10 to 14 W of cores plus about 5 W of uncore: 15 to 19 W package. os measured 11 to 15 W package with four threads packing and breeding at today's rate, which is consistent. With os's frequency cap the same work takes 3.3 cores at 2.5 W: about 13 W package.

What data's plan adds against that baseline, at 2M/s:

- Recipes for parametric children only (data's P2, no structural port). The CPU keeps the structural 40% at 8k cycles: 3.2k cycles per child weighted, 2.4 core-seconds per 3M, 1.6 cores, 10 to 14 W package. Saving against the CPU-only baseline: 3 to 5 W. At the power cap the RTX gains about 0.5% per watt (perf goes roughly with the square root of power near the top of the curve; 5 W of 100 is 2.5%), so 1.5 to 2.5%, minus the 1% of RTX time data assigns to materialization. Net: about 1%. Cost: the versioned slab, the reclamation, and a second implementation of the parametric emit and repair. For 1% it does not pass.
- Structural operators on the RTX too (data's P3). Package drops to idle, 6 to 8 W; saving 8 to 12 W against the baseline, 4 to 6% at the cap, minus data's 1 to 3% of RTX time. Net: 2 to 4%. Cost: a port of 64 operators, repair and canonical order, data's own estimate three to four weeks, and either a second implementation of everything (a determinism seam forever) or deleting the CPU breeder after the port, which also moves the property tests and mutation_audit to the device. And data flagged the register cost of take-up inside the persistent kernel: if materialization pushes the kernel past 128 registers, occupancy falls and the 2 to 4% is gone.

The number that makes data's plan win: the measured cycles per child after my container rewrite. Below 7k weighted (at most 3.5 cores at 2M/s), the CPU plan costs 1,200 lines and 2 weeks and the RTX plan buys 1 to 4%. Above 12k weighted, the CPU needs 6 to 7 cores, 35 to 45 W package, which by the 21% measurement costs the RTX 15 to 20%, and then data's port is worth 15 to 20% and wins clearly. The second number that could flip it: os's calibration slope. If a watt at the cap is worth 1% of rate rather than 0.5%, data's full plan is worth 5 to 9% net and becomes a track after the kernel is within 1.5x of the goal. I ask data to state the clock tax they assume in W and %, as the chair asked, so we are on one curve.

What data's plan gives that the CPU plan cannot: a host ceiling above 4M/s and host RSS under 1 GB. Neither matters at 2M/s; preallocation solves the second.

## D3.6 Attack: igpu's Radeon breeding

Same baseline. igpu's plan has the Radeon do packing and the parametric 60%, at 3 to 8 W, and the CPU keeps the structural 40%: 2.4 core-seconds per 3M, 10 to 14 W package as above. Total package: 13 to 22 W. The CPU-only baseline is 15 to 19 W (13 W capped). The Radeon draws from the same package limit as the cores, so it moves watts within the package and returns none to the RTX. Net watts: zero within the error of both estimates. Net rate: zero. Radeon packing is moot once the RTX unpacks genes on the device, which data, gpu, os and I all put first. What the Radeon plan costs: a third implementation of the parametric emit (Vulkan compute, RADV, shaderInt64), an external-memory-host import nobody has tried against NVIDIA's host registration here, and the lockup timeout. `modinfo amdgpu` on this kernel says "default: 2000" ms: igpu read it right, os did not. A hard 2 s per dispatch on the display device.

The number that makes the Radeon plan win: the CPU rewrite failing at above 12k cycles per child while the RTX port is refused. Then the Radeon at 5 W doing 60% of the work is the only device left, and it saves 20 to 25 W of package, 10% of RTX rate at the cap. It is a hedge, and a hedge should be built only when the thing it hedges has been measured to fail. I ask igpu to concede breeding until the `breed_bench` number exists, and to keep the power split measurement, which everyone needs.

## D4 The frequency cap and what falsifies the power slope

Ownership: os owns the CPU frequency cap and the energy preference. I withdraw my P4 into os's P3 and keep only the game-side pieces that need no root: spreading breed threads over all 16 hardware threads at nice 10 and SCHED_BATCH, and pinning the rayon pool to one SMT sibling per core with the UI, worker and engine threads on the others, which is os's P4. os writes the measurement; I supply the load.

What falsifies "every 10 W the CPU takes costs the GPU 5 to 6%": the claim rests on one point, 174k to 137k with 8 threads, and os's trace already weakens it. In os's GPU phase the RTX ran at 2,490 MHz flat, 72 to 81 W, no limiter active, with four host threads busy. So at today's kernel the GPU is clock-bound with 10 to 25 W of headroom, and CPU watts cost it nothing until it reaches the cap. The 21% drop then has two candidate causes: Dynamic Boost lowering the RTX limit from 100 to 85 W while the kernel needed more than 85, or host starvation, because in that measurement the 8 threads were the CPU evaluation engine competing with packing and the engine thread's polling on the same pool, so the GPU got gaps rather than fewer joules. The test: p2_speed with an 8-thread synthetic AVX-512 load pinned away from the engine thread, logging clocks.sm, power.draw and the limiter reason at 100 ms. If clocks.sm stays at 2,490 and the rate holds, the slope is zero today and my claim is false at this operating point. If clocks.sm drops with the software power cap active, the slope is real and gets read off the trace. Under sustained runs the claim gets stronger, not weaker: os's counters show minutes at the power cap, and at the cap the slope is the square-root curve, about 0.5% per watt, so 10 W is about 5%. My revised claim: zero below the cap, about 5% per 10 W at the cap, and the game at 2M/s runs at the cap.

## D7 Preallocation and huge pages, one line with os

Agreed: preallocate each ring block's arrays and the pinned wave buffers once and reuse them forever; huge pages are one optional `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)` on those arrays, kept only if dTLB misses drop measurably, which at os's 2.2M misses in 92G cycles they will not. I drop P5's huge-page claim and keep its preallocation, which os's fault count and swap finding (3.2 GB swapped under a churning ring) make more important than I said.

## What breaks the proposals I still back

- P1 (write the child once as a 900 B genome into pinned memory; the RTX unpacks): breaks if the device unpack cannot reproduce `Model::new`'s float constants bit for bit, which is the same gate data set (dump both, compare 262k). Also breaks if take-up unpack costs registers in the persistent kernel; then the unpack is a separate per-wave kernel writing lane records to VRAM, which keeps the CPU out but costs 0.5 GB per wave.
- P2 (container rewrite, vector parametric path, integer-exact RNG): breaks at above 12k measured cycles per structural child, as stated, and breaks if ga rules the Irwin-Hall gaussian a search change.
- P3 (ship plans for the parametric 60%): I demote it. At the CPU baseline it is worth about 1% and a second implementation. It comes back only if the breed_bench number fails or the power slope is above 1% per watt.
- P8 (16-block ring): I defer to ga and data on block size and keep only the invariant: CPU latency per block must stay below the GPU time of the other blocks in flight.
- The archive on the CPU at 30 to 60 ms per block: unmeasured on a mature save; the round-3 number decides it.
