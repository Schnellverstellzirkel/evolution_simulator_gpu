# Round 2, data flow: answers, withdrawals, attacks

Sources: examples/p2_speed.rs, src/cuda_engine.rs submit_as, scratchpad/warp/final.txt, and the round 1 papers of cpu, ga, gpu, os and igpu.

## D1. The 13%: host work, not tail

Chair, the 13% is not a free 1.15x for the game. p2_speed's wall clock starts before submit, which packs, stages and uploads. GPU-busy is the start-to-stop event pair, and the start event is recorded on the main stream after the HtoD copies, the stop after the wave streams join. So GPU-busy excludes the pack, the staging memcpy and the upload, and includes the kernel with its tail. The 13% of wall time p2_speed loses is host-side, on a single wave with nothing to overlap. Derived from a 4.37 s wave (262,144 at 60k/s): pack 0.32 s is 7.3%, the staging memcpy of about 400 MB at 8 GB/s is 1.1%, the HtoD at 11 GB/s is 0.8%, the fresh Vec page faults os measured are about 1%, and the rest is the class split into three batches and poll granularity.

In the game, 4 slots overlap, so the DMA and the readback hide behind other slots' kernels. What stays unhidden is the CPU time of pack and memcpy, which every paper already counts under the clock tax. So the game gains 2 to 4% of wall time, plus the tax. Not 15%. The measurement exists already: `Snapshot.engines` carries each engine's busy share, and the unhidden fraction is one minus it.

The tail, derived: at 45M creature-steps/s over about 1,536 resident lane groups a group steps every 34 us when the GPU is full and about 12 us alone. The last creature a wave hands out runs at most 1,200 steps, so the drain lasts 4 ms (screened) to 15 ms (a survivor). The cost is block granularity: a 128-thread block holds its SM slot until its last group finishes, so each of the 96 blocks idles about half its lanes for 10 to 15 ms per wave. That is 0.7 block-seconds against 422 per wave today: 0.2%. Streams hide nothing here, because the slot is held, not free. At 2M/s the same 15 ms against a 130 ms wave is 5 to 6% at today's per-step latency, 1 to 2% with a kernel 5x lower in latency. The fix is 30 lines: the take-up counter spans waves. The kernel reads the wave's `count` from a device word the host bumps per block (one queue per lane class and world), so a block never runs dry at a wave boundary. On a world change the host stops bumping and the kernel drains, which is the retarget path the ring already has.

## D3. Breeding, packing, the prefilter, the gaussian

### The structural port: withdrawn

Size: src/evolution/anatomy is 5,160 lines, plus about 800 lines of evolution.rs that the operators need (structural_mutation_from, apply_structural_operator, graft_from, repair, canonicalize_bone_order). About 6,000 lines of Rust over Vecs, which becomes 4,000 to 6,000 lines of CUDA over fixed arrays, plus a test per operator against the CPU version.

Rate without it, taking cpu's numbers: structural children are 40% of offspring (35% plus the novelty emitter's 18%), so 1.2M per generation at cpu's 8,000 cycles is 9.6 G cycles, 2.4 core-seconds per 1.5 s generation: 1.6 cores busy. Their upload by value is 1.2M x 1.1 to 2.5 KB = 1.3 to 3 GB per generation, 0.9 to 2 GB/s on a 12 GB/s link. The host ceiling from structural breeding alone on 6 cores is about 7M/s. So the port buys the clock tax of 1.6 cores. By cpu's own rule (5 to 6% per 10 W) that is 4 to 6% of GPU rate for 6,000 lines and a second implementation of every operator. I withdraw P3. It comes back only if cpu's rewrite misses its 8,000 cycles by 3x or more.

### What RTX breeding adds beyond the clock tax: little, if cpu's numbers hold

cpu's vector path is 1,500 cycles per parametric child. That is 1.8M x 1,500 = 2.7 G cycles per generation, 0.7 core-seconds, half a core at 2M/s. Breeding those children on the RTX (my P2, cpu's P3, ga's P6, they are the same proposal) saves half a core, 2 to 3 W, 1 to 2% of GPU rate by the same rule. As a throughput lever it is nothing. I assumed a 21% tax for 8 threads and linear in threads, so 2.3 cores for all of cpu's 3.5 core-seconds is 6 to 8%. That is what the CPU-only design costs at 2M/s, and it is acceptable.

What device breeding really buys is bytes: 16 B recipes against 1.1 to 2.5 KB genes, so ring memory and upload shrink 100x. That matters only if the ring must be deep, and D7 says it need not be. So recipes are demoted to insurance with a decision rule: cpu runs breed_bench on a mature save after the rewrite; under 8,000 weighted cycles per child, breeding stays on the CPU and recipes are not built; above it, or if plan_offspring (which nobody has costed) is over 2,000 cycles per child, recipes for the parametric 60% go to the RTX.

Two things I keep whichever way that goes. First, genes are written once by the breeder into pinned host memory in SoA form and the RTX reads them at take-up, zero-copy or by one DMA per block; os and cpu proposed the same and I concede the format to cpu. The pack and its 4.9 us per creature disappear either way, because the lane group unpacks from genes (my P1, gpu's pack kernel and cpu's prologue are the same thing; I prefer it inside take-up so no lane buffer exists, and I ask gpu for the register cost). Second, whoever breeds a child is the only one who ever computes it: entrants bred on the device are read back, never regenerated from a plan. That removes the determinism seam the chair worries about.

### The archive prefilter

cpu's 30 to 60 ms per 196k block is a reading of the code, not a measurement: descriptors at 2,000 cycles x 196k as a 12 ms burst on 8 threads, the sequential island offers, and refresh_behavior_scores at an unknown cost. At 2M/s a block is 100 ms, so 60 ms fits with no margin for a generation boundary in the same block. Who measures: EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED prints the seven sections of archive_block; one generation on save42.evo (scratchpad/warp) and on night.evo, scheduled by the chair under the shared lock. I predict under 60 ms per block with refresh_behavior_scores under 20 ms, and candidates (results that beat their island cell) under 2% of a mature block.

What I keep from P5 is the cheap half: the lane group computes the descriptor bins from the metrics in its registers, reads its island cell's occupant fitness from a 29 KB device table, and writes a candidate bit into the result. About 50 kernel lines and one L2 read per creature. The CPU skips the descriptor pass and iterates candidates in position order; the sequential commit is unchanged. The candidate genome slab is dropped unless recipes come. The old rejection (kernel code in three places for 5%) stands; this form is one place, and its value is the 8-thread burst.

### The gaussian rule

My rule: one implementation breeds a given child and its genes are read back, so no two implementations must agree bit for bit, and the gaussian is whatever is cheapest where it runs: cpu's polynomial Box-Muller at about 25 cycles per lane on the CPU, __logf, sqrtf and __cosf at about 20 instructions on the RTX. A CPU-only game is then a different search from a GPU game, as the physics already makes it. If the chair rules that the CPU twin must reproduce device children bit for bit, cpu's pinned-FMA polynomial is the rule for both, at about 40 instructions on the RTX, because the kernel unit compiles with --fmad=true and --prec-sqrt=false and device breeding code would need explicit __fmaf_rn, __fmul_rn and a correctly rounded sqrt. igpu's integer-exact sum of 12 uniforms costs 100 to 150 integer ops and changes the tails; it is right only when two devices must agree and the polynomial cannot be pinned, which is not our case. The counter-based RNG keyed by (seed, generation, slot, gene, draw) is in all three plans and I back it. ga owns whether the changed stream is "a new seed"; I expect yes, since qd::VERSION bumps anyway.

## D7. Ring depth and block size

The ring must cover the longest host pause, not a round number. At 2M/s after cpu's rewrite: the per-block chain (archive 30 to 60 ms plus breeding 196k at 3.5 core-seconds per 3M on 4 threads, 60 ms) is 100 to 120 ms; a confirmation round trip (a 4x-rate trial, about 70 ms alone, plus up to 25 ms for a block slot at 100 ms waves on 4 streams) is 100 to 150 ms; a generation boundary is unmeasured, my guess 50 to 300 ms; a save clone and a snapshot are tens of ms. Worst coincidence about 0.3 s. A 0.5 s ring covers it with 1.7x margin. I withdraw 2 s: it was over-insurance with the cost the chair names.

The rule: ring depth = 5x the measured p95 of the per-block host chain, floor 0.3 s, cap 1 s of GPU work at the measured rate. At 2M/s that is 1M creatures: 2.5 GB of pinned genes by value (30 GB RAM, fine) or 16 MB of recipes. At today's 167k/s it is 84k to 167k, which says today's ring of 786k is 5 to 9x too deep: a button press throws away 4.7 s of work, 26% of a generation. That is a bug in the product today and the same rule fixes it now.

Block size: ga wants 49k blocks for 4x more CMA tells per generation. The GPU tolerates any block above about 20k (launch cost is nothing, the spanning counter fixes tails). The host chain tolerates blocks whose fixed costs (refresh_behavior_scores, the verdict, the boundary) stay under 20% of block time, and needs absorb and breed to overlap once blocks are under about 100k at 2M/s, because 49k blocks are 25 ms of GPU each and the serial chain is 35 ms. That overlap is legal only with an append-only, versioned elite table on the host, so breeding block k reads parents by version while block k+1 commits. That is the versioning I proposed for the device slab, applied on the CPU; cpu should own it. So ga and I converge on: block = 50 ms of GPU work, floor 32k, cap 256k. At 2M/s that is 100k, at 1M/s 49k (ga's number), at today's rate 32k. ga gets its tells 2 to 4x more often than today at every rate; the RING_BLOCKS constant becomes a function of rate and depth.

## Attacks on others, with the number that would have to hold

- cpu, 1,500 cycles per parametric child: it assumes 160 gaussians at 2.5 cycles each on 16 lanes and clamp, repair and copies under 800 cycles. If breed_bench shows over 5,000, the ring goes by recipe and the parametric path goes to the RTX. cpu also has no number for plan_offspring, which walks least_visited lists and hash maps per child; at 2,000 cycles it is 6 G cycles per generation, more than the vector emit. It needs its own line in breed_bench.
- cpu, P8 (a generation in flight, 16 blocks of 196k): rejected by the world-change cost above. A 3.1M ring discards a whole generation per button press.
- os, results written by the kernel into host memory: 160 MB/s of uncoalesced PCIe writes, which write-combining handles. Accepted, with one condition: the sequence word only tells the host a block is complete; absorption stays per block in ring order.
- os, zero-copy records read at take-up over PCIe: 12 to 32 transactions at about 1.5 us each is 20 to 50 us per creature against a 15 to 40 ms trial, 0.1 to 0.3%. Fine, but it keeps the CPU pack. Zero-copy of genes with the unpack on the device keeps the benefit and drops the pack. I back that form.
- igpu, P3 (packing on the Radeon): nothing to pack once the RTX unpacks from genes. P4 (parametric breeding on the Radeon): it displaces 0.7 core-seconds per generation by cpu's number, 2 to 3 W, and adds a third implementation and an untried shared-memory import. It would need cpu's rewrite to miss 3x and the power table to show 2.3 busy cores costing over 10% of RTX rate. Closed from my side until both are measured.
- gpu, the 500 B genome: genes are 1.1 KB at 7 nodes and 2.5 KB at the p90 body with 40 muscles (Muscle is 52 B). 500 B needs 16-bit muscle genes, a search-space change for ga to rule on; I would not gate the data flow on it. A 0.5 s ring by value is 2.5 GB either way.
- ga, P1 bar table: yes, a per-block table travels with the block's settings like Config does, one L2 read per rung. It is the same mechanism as my occupant table and they should be one table.

## What breaks what I still back

- Device unpack at take-up: breaks if it pushes the kernel above 128 registers. gpu answers; the fallback is a per-wave prologue kernel writing lane records to VRAM (0.5 GB per wave, no PCIe, no CPU).
- Ring sized by latency: breaks if the generation boundary is over 0.5 s on a mature save. EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED prints it, in the same run that measures the archive.
- Spanning counter: breaks if a block still runs a survivor from wave k when the host retargets wave k+1; the drain must finish first. That is the existing rule (running blocks enter no archive) at creature granularity.
- Candidate bit: breaks if a stale occupant table lets candidates exceed 5% of results; then the CPU pays the descriptor pass for those. It cannot reject wrongly, because occupants only rise.
- Blocks of 50 ms: breaks if absorb and breed cannot overlap, which needs cpu's versioned elite table; without it the floor is 100k at 2M/s and ga gets 2x more tells instead of 4x.

## Positions after round 2

Withdrawn: the structural port; the 2 s ring; the candidate genome slab. Insurance with a decision rule: recipes for parametric children. Kept: unpack from genes on the device, genes written once into pinned memory, entrants read back and never recomputed, the candidate bit and descriptor in the kernel, ring depth by measured latency (0.3 to 1 s, which also fixes today's 26% world-change loss), blocks of 50 ms with a 32k floor, the take-up counter spanning waves, no per-creature use of the Radeon. Measurements for the chair to schedule: EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED for one generation on save42.evo and night.evo (archive sections, boundary time, candidate share), and cpu's breed_bench with a plan_offspring line.
