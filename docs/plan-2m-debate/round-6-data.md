## Q6 first, because it decides Q4

The two limits, applied to each path at the generation-51 mix and 2 substeps. FMA-bound rate = 124 G warp instructions/s over executed warp instructions per creature-step; MIO-bound rate = 10.8 G MIO issues/s over MIO instructions per creature-step; the rate is the smaller.

- Lane-group kernel today: gpu counted about 6,800 warp instructions per warp-step for 4 creatures, 1,700 per creature-step, and about 430 MIO instructions per warp-substep, 215 per creature-step. FMA-bound 73M, MIO-bound 50M. Measured 53 to 55M on the generation-10 dump. So the lane-group kernel sits at the MIO wall now, which the warp-state sample agrees with (short scoreboard 18%, wait 33%, barrier under 0.5%). Barrier removal is worth nothing; only removing shared loads and shuffles moves it, and halving MIO gives a 100M cap with the FMA limit at 73M taking over. Trimming a fifth of control flow lifts the FMA limit to about 90M. Path (a) tops out near 80 to 90M unless both happen.
- Per-lane general-tree stub: 1,160 warp instructions per creature-step, FMA-bound 107M; its MIO count is the muscle scatter to shared memory, about 12 per muscle per lane-substep, 228 per 16 creatures per substep, 28 per creature-step, MIO-bound 380M. Measured 96M at boost. It is instruction-bound with 3.5x of MIO headroom.
- Per-plan specialized per-lane kernel: if the topology bit tests (LOP3 20%) and the lane-and-level predication in the Delassus and tree batches (I take 12 of their 40%) go, about 790 per creature-step, FMA-bound 157M; with the cheaper solve gpu and physics answer in Q2 (about 200 instead of 340 per substep) about 510, 240M; at spin-adaptive substeps about 1.5x of that. MIO stays under 30, never the limit.

So the data path serves the per-lane kernel, general first and per-plan when measured, and the numbers it must carry are 100M now, 160M with specialization, 240M with the solve, times 1.5 at L2.5.

## Q1. Bucketing by plan at submit

What a plan is: the bone tree up to canonical order, which is `qd::Topology` (node count plus the edge list; muscle attachments stay runtime). Node positions, lengths, masses and muscle records are runtime constants in every case; only the tree structure is baked.

How many plans cover 90% of a mature ring: not measured, and it is measurable from save42 in an afternoon (a canonical rooted-tree hash of Topology over the ring and the archive, cumulative share sorted by count; the `species` history cannot answer it, because it records only node and muscle counts). My estimate: 65% of offspring are parametric and keep their parent's tree; each island's optimizers sit on 4 designs; the archive holds about 7,500 elites over 5 islands but its elites cluster on lineages. I expect 100 to 300 distinct trees in the ring and 20 to 40 covering 90%, with the coverage curve steep at the top (the four optimizer designs per island alone may hold half of all CMA children). The unlabeled rooted-tree counts (48 at 7 nodes, 115 at 8, 286 at 9, 719 at 10) bound the space and say the tail is long but thin.

Why this differs from the rejected single-plan kernel: the old kernel's cost was projection passes with loops over runtime node counts; baking the plan removed loop overhead, 10 to 20%, and mixed units gained nothing because warps mixed plans. The stub's cost is topology reads and predication, LOP3 alone at 20% and the two tree batches plus the Delassus assembly at 40%, which straight-line code removes rather than shortens. The other half of the old failure, mixed warps, is exactly what bucketing at submit prevents by construction. So I expect 30 to 40%, not 10 to 20%, and the measurement comes first: one day, the stub with its 8-node tree baked as constants against the general stub on the same synthetic bodies. Under 25% gain, per-plan is rejected and the general kernel stays.

Cost in kernels. Today a world compiles 9 (3 classes x standard, record, fine). Per plan: 30 plans x 3 variants per world plus the general kernels, 30 to 60 s of compile per cold world on 3 threads, and about 15 MB of cache per world (cubins are 130 to 220 KB), so ruling 12's eviction at 200 files becomes eviction by bytes at 300 MB. The GPU cannot wait 30 s, so the general kernels compile first (2 s in parallel) and take every queue, and the plan kernels arrive in the background over the next minute, each taking over its queue when loaded. Neighbour-world prefetch covers only the top 10 plans of the 14 single-toggle worlds.

The middle path, baking (node count, depth, branching pattern) rather than the exact tree, loses most of the gain: the elimination order and the clique structure are properties of the exact tree. Exact trees for the top plans and the general kernel for the rest is the middle path, and the queues make the line cheap to move.

## Q4. Which path, and what the queues look like

I back (b) now and (c) as the next measured track. (a) is at the MIO wall at 50M and needs both an MIO halving and a control-flow trim to reach 90M, with lane use of 25 to 30% at every tree level; (b) is at 96 to 107M with 3.5x of MIO headroom, so every instruction removed is rate; (c) is (b) with its largest overhead removed, 160M by my count, as a compile-time change. What transfers from the stub to (a): the split muscle record in L2 and the muscle state as one word, perhaps 10% there; the batched tree solve and the topology word are per-lane constructs.

The queues if warps run one plan: per class, one queue per plan for the top plans and one general queue for the rest, each with its head, tail, drain flag and muscle buckets as ruled. A plan kernel's warps take only from their plan's queue. The general kernel's warps take from the general queue and, when it is empty, from any plan queue whose kernel is not resident yet or which is deeper than its warps can drain in a block period, at the general kernel's efficiency. Nothing ever starves: the general kernel can run every creature. Warps per plan kernel come from the plan's share of lane-steps over the last 20 blocks, re-split at the same 10-point rule, and a plan whose share falls under one warp's worth is folded back into the general queue. Submit does the bucketing: the breeder already sorts by (class, exact muscle count, depth); the sort key gains the plan hash in front, and the block header lists the per-plan ranges. Determinism is unchanged: results by index, the same three requeue rules, and which kernel ran a creature does not change its bits because the plan kernel is the general kernel with the same arithmetic in the same order, which is the gate (bit-equal results between the plan kernel and the general kernel on the plan's creatures).

What to run first: the one-day baked-tree stub variant. If it passes 25%, the queue work is a week and it lands with the per-lane kernel's track, not after it.

## Q5. The host triggers

Parametric 8,084 and structural 17,260 after host-bounded both crossed the rule's 3,000 and 15,000. Two things changed under the rule. The kernel ceiling fell to about 100 to 240M creature-steps/s, so a 3M generation lasts 6 to 14 s at 300 steps, not 1.5 s, and the host has 4 to 10x more time per creature. And rows 0 to 2 show no clock drop with 2 or 4 busy threads (2,490 to 2,476 MHz, no limiter, 68 to 74 W); their rate drops are time-slicing with other agents' GPU processes. The host tax today is about zero and stays zero until the kernel reaches the cap, about 2.6 warp instructions per SM per clock by the FMA probe, which the per-lane kernel does not reach before per-plan specialization.

So: recipes on the RTX do not open now. They open when two conditions hold at once, measured, not estimated: the game's own run shows the power limiter active in more than 10% of samples (tools/power-sample.sh beside the game), and breed_bench after host-vector shows the parametric row above 3,000 with the host above 3 busy cores at the then-current rate. Decision date: the day host-vector merges plus one power sample of the game, which by the plan is two weeks out. Until then the insurance stays a design, not a track.

Structural at 17,260: no port. At 0.5M/s a generation is 6 s and the structural 40% costs 21 G cycles, 5 core-seconds, under one core. host-order (the SoA rewrite, targeting 7,000) is the track; the frequency cap is os's row 5, taken only if a power sample of the game shows the limiter active. The port's 5% at 6,000 lines got worse when the kernel got slower. Rule: if host-order leaves structural above 12,000 and the limiter is active, the cap first, then the port question reopens; date: host-order's merge plus one sample.

What the archive and boundary numbers change. The archive stage at 55 ms per 196k (30 at 100k) and the boundary at 41 ms are both well under the ring rule's terms, so the ring floor of 0.3 s stands and the boundary clause I added in round 4 binds nothing: 5x the p95 chain is the rule, and today's chain of 290 ms per 196k block is 4% of the 6.8 s the current ring holds at 55M creature-steps/s, so the ring is far too deep at today's rate, which ruling 4 fixes at experiment start. The candidate bit loses most of its value: candidates are 2.1%, and the prefilter the device descriptor would remove is 14 to 16 ms of the 55, about a quarter of the archive stage and 5% of the host chain. The track becomes the bar tables alone (ga's rungs need them) with the descriptor and candidate bit as an optional 50-line follow-on, scheduled after everything else in my list. The versioned elite table for breed and absorb overlap also loses its urgency: the chain is 290 ms against blocks of 1.7 s.

## Revised multipliers for my levers

At the generation-51 mix, on whichever per-lane kernel lands:

- Bucketed queues by muscle count: 1.1 to 1.25x for per-lane kernels (stub muscles are 16% of the step at uniform 19 muscles, so a warp max of 32 costs about 11% there, more once the solve shrinks). One day on the general stub with a mixed-muscle population.
- Per-plan bucketing: costs nothing on the queue side; enables Q1's 1.3 to 1.4x if the baked-tree stub confirms it.
- Spanning counter 1.02x; reserved slots 0.96x; DMA plus prologue 0.99x.
- Host tax: 1.0x at today's operating point, 0.85 to 0.93x once the kernel reaches the cap, by os's rows 3 to 6.
- Pack removal and pinned arenas: rate-neutral; RSS from 5 to 9 GB to under 3 GB.

## Revised ceiling

Kernel rows I carry: general per-lane 100M, per-plan 160M, per-plan with the cheaper solve 240M, at 2 substeps, times 1.5 at L2.5. With my factors (1.2 x 1.02 x 0.96 x 0.99 = 1.16) and 300 steps per creature: 0.39M/s, 0.62M/s, 0.93M/s at 2 substeps; 0.58, 0.93, 1.4M/s at L2.5. With R4 at 50% on top, 235 steps: up to 1.8M/s on the best row. 2M/s at generation 51 needs the per-plan kernel, the cheaper solve, L2.5 and R4 together, and none of the four is measured yet; the first three have one-day experiments (baked-tree stub, PGS-on-rows stub variant, the substep ladder on the current kernel). Generation 100 without a growth rule: 0.65 to 0.82x of those.
