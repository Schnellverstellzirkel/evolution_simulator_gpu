# Round 5, iGPU domain: convergence

Author: the iGPU expert. Date 2026-09-30, night.

## 1. The design I back

The converged pipeline with all thirteen rulings of round 4 accepted: CPU breeding into process-memory arenas registered with `cuMemHostRegister` (6), per-block DMA and a prologue unpack into VRAM lane records (2), persistent per-class kernels with the tail and drain flag in VRAM (3, 5), block size and ring depth as saved experiment constants (4), reserved fine and replay slots instead of in-kernel confirmation (1), the amended physics spec (7), the ladder with L2.5 as the primary row (8), the growth-step rule for the owner with W = 8 built last (9), the nursery exemptions and the R4 floor (10), the host tax at 15 to 21% until os's rows exist (11), the compile and cache items (12), and the disk rules (13). I reject no ruling. On ruling 6 I keep my recommendation for the helper process and defer to data's number: if `cuCtxDestroy` from another thread returns within 2 s on a kernel that never reads its drain flag, in-process is enough; if it blocks, the helper process is the only way to stop a hung persistent kernel on a GPU with no display watchdog, and the arenas being process memory (ruling 6) already makes the helper cheap. The Radeon's place in this design is the window and nothing else. On ruling 9 I withdraw the hard 16-node offspring cap as a proposal and keep it as the number the dump can bring back: if the tail's entrant share by archive is under 0.5% and its R1 stop share above 90%, the 16-node cap deletes W = 8 and the lane-group kernel for a week of saved work and 11% of GPU time, and the growth-step rule holds the tail at its natural 2% anyway.

## 3. Three measurements that would change my mind

1. Track 3, row 6 against os's 2- and 4-thread CPU rows. If the Radeon at 1,100 MHz and 100% duty costs the RTX under 3% while 3 busy cores cost it over 8%, the fallback breeder stops being a hedge and becomes the cheaper home for the parametric 60% in watts, and I would ask for it ahead of the RTX recipes. If the Radeon row costs over 5%, the fallback is dead at any breed_bench number and track 4 is deleted from the plan.
2. The tail dump (ruling 9). If any 17-plus body entered an island archive or the reserve in the last 20 generations of save42, or the tail's R1 stop share is under 50%, the 16-node cap is wrong and W = 8 must be built earlier than last. If the entrant share is under 0.5% and the stop share above 90%, I would bring the 16-node cap back to the owner as the cheaper answer than a W = 8 class.
3. The world-change idle. If the stage log shows GPU idle per world change under 0.1 s once ruling 12's parallel compiles and neighbour prefetch are in, the fat-kernel measurement is unnecessary and I withdraw it. If idle stays above 1 s on a cold world (a compile slower than 2 s per cubin, or more than 24 variants per world), the fat kernel is the fix and its 3% bar should be relaxed to 5%.

## 4. Honest ceiling

The Radeon contributes zero throughput to this design. Its value is what it keeps off the RTX: the window (measured at 1.28x on 2026-09-25 when the compositor left the RTX) and, after track 2, about 1 to 2 ms per frame of UI-thread CPU. If the fallback fires, it adds 3 to 10% at the cap by moving 2 to 3 cores of breeding into 5 to 8 W of Radeon, and nothing otherwise. Radeon physics is closed and stays closed: 0.2 to 0.25 of the RTX per clock, from the package budget the CPU needs.

For the whole design, at generation 51: 1.0 to 1.5M/s at 2 substeps with R1 to R3 (3T to 5T); 1.5 to 2.4M/s on the primary row L2.5 with R1 to R4 at the ruled 50% fire rate, after the 15 to 21% host tax; 2M/s sustained needs L2.5 to pass its 0.9 honesty bar and the budget to land nearer 5T than 3T. At generation 100 with the muscle slope os measured (0.65 to 0.82x of generation 51 by muscles alone) and the growth-step rule holding nodes: 1.0 to 1.9M/s on the primary row. The three assumptions that set these numbers are the power budget (a 1.6x factor decided by os's rows 11 and 12), the substep honesty (L2.5 at 65% odds), and the kernel's issue rate at 16 warps (30% against 45%, decided by the stub kernel and the nsys warp-state sample). None of them is mine, and none of them moves if the Radeon does more or less than draw the window.
