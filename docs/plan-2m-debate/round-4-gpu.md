# Round 4, GPU domain: attacks on Design P, defence of K where the brief is wrong, glitch list

## Attacks on Design P

### 10. Zero-copy take-up stalls the warp, not the creature

data prices take-up at 40 to 50 pipelined PCIe loads, 10 to 30 us per creature, against a 1.5 to 15 ms trial: 0.2 to 1%. That divides the latency by the wrong denominator. In a per-lane warp the take-up is a divergent branch: while one lane's group (2 lanes at W = 2) walks its PCIe loads, the other 30 lanes sit masked and their creatures do not step. The loads are also not one flat batch: the header gives node_start and muscle_start, the counts give the array lengths, so there are at least three dependent PCIe round trips (header, arrays, then the muscle end nodes and sensors), 4 to 6 us floor even with everything else pipelined, and 10 to 30 us in practice.

The number: a lane finishes a creature every 300 steps on average, so a 32-lane warp takes up once every 300 / 32 = 9.4 steps. At 5 to 15 us per step that is one take-up per 50 to 140 us of warp time, each costing 10 to 30 us of warp stall: 10 to 40% of the warp's time in take-up. Other warps on the scheduler fill part of it; at 4 warps per scheduler with 3 ready, the throughput loss is a third of that, 4 to 13%. Not 0.2 to 1%.

Detection in a day, no new kernel: allocate today's `lanes`, `muscles`, `ends` and `heads` buffers with cuMemHostAlloc(DEVICEMAP) instead of cuMemAlloc, one line in ensure_buffers, and run p2_speed on save42 both ways; the lane-group kernel reads its record once per creature exactly as the per-lane kernel would. A drop over 3% kills zero-copy. What has to change: DMA the block's genes into VRAM on the copy engine at submit (2.7 GB/s at 2 M/s, no SM time; the results ring is written by SMs through the mapped page, so no contention). VRAM: a 0.3 to 1 s ring at 1.35 to 2 KB is 0.8 to 4 GB, which is why the ring should sit at its 0.3 s floor. Even from VRAM the unpack must issue all its loads before the first use, or the dependent-latency argument returns at L2 scale.

### 11. Persistent kernels: the fault path exists, the CPU failover does not

A CUDA error in another process does not touch our context; contexts are isolated. What kills every context is a GPU reset (an Xid from a hung kernel anywhere, a driver fault, a thermal trip). Today the scheduler moves work to the CPU engine on GPU loss, and that engine is being deleted. So Design P needs the recovery path spelled out, and it is cheap because of the pinned arenas: the genes of every block in flight are in host memory. On error: destroy the context, reopen (the GPU powers up in 1 to 2 s under runtime D3), load the cubins from the disk cache (about 100 ms), re-append every block in flight from its arena with head = tail, relaunch. Live creatures are lost; their blocks are re-run whole from the same genes, so the history is the one a run without the fault would have produced, because absorption is in ring order and a block's results are a function of its genes and settings. What the player sees: a "GPU restarted" notice and 1 to 3 s without new results. The loss is the ring depth, 0.3 to 1 s of work, against 4.7 s today.

Agents beside the game: compute contexts time-slice with instruction-level preemption, so a persistent kernel is preempted for an agent's kernel exactly as a 1 s wave is today; the rate drops by the agent's share whatever the design. Two things are worse: VRAM, where the DMA variant of item 10 must keep the ring at its floor so the game stays under 2 GB; and a stuck drain. If a kernel fails to observe the drain flag, cuCtxDestroy blocks forever and the engine cannot restart. The host needs a 2 s drain timeout that destroys the context from another thread and takes the recovery path above; the stage log records drains over 100 ms.

### 12. Starvation: the mapped tail floods PCIe, and the p95 rule misses the generation boundary

Two defects. First, the spin. Every group whose queue is dry polls a host word over PCIe every 2 us. At 4,608 groups (W = 2) that is up to 2.3 G reads/s, each a 64 B PCIe transaction, against a link that carries about 190 M such transactions per second. The moment the queue runs dry the pollers saturate the upstream link, and the very thing that ends the starvation, the host's DMA of the next block and its tail write, is slowed by the pollers. A starvation of 50 ms becomes one of hundreds. Detection: PCIe RX utilization in nsys during a forced starvation (submit nothing for 100 ms). What has to change: the tail lives in VRAM and the host writes it with cuStreamWriteValue32 on the submit stream (one PCIe write per block), or one lane per block polls the mapped word and broadcasts through shared memory (96 pollers, 50 M reads/s, 25% of the link, still too much). VRAM tail, host-written, is the answer, and the same for the drain flag.

Second, the ring rule. At 2 M/s with 100k blocks there are 30 blocks per generation, so the generation boundary is a p97 event and clamp(5 x p95 chain) never sees it. cpu's boundary estimate is 125 ms on one thread, unmeasured, on top of the block's 80 to 100 ms chain: 225 ms against a 0.5 s ring, covered at 2 M/s; at 1 M/s the ring is at its 0.3 s floor and 185 ms still fits. What does not fit is `refresh_behavior_scores` on a mature archive if it is quadratic: 7,500 elites per island squared is 56 M distances, seconds on one thread, on every island that inserted, which at maturity is every block. data's incremental fix is a condition of the design, not an option. Measurement: EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED on an archive grown by breed_bench's --evolve at 200k (no version-43 mature save exists), per island in milliseconds per insert. The rule should be clamp(3 x max chain over the last two generations, 0.3 s, 1 s): a starvation costs the whole GPU for its length, extra depth costs memory and world-change loss.

### 13. The host tax at 12,000 cycles

At 12,000 cycles per structural child the structural row is 14.4 G per 3M instead of 7.2 G, the total 25.6 G, 6.4 core-seconds, 4.3 cores busy at 2 M/s, 22 to 25 W of package: 11 to 12% at the cap. Recipes on the RTX remove only the parametric row, half a core; the host stays at 3.8 cores, so the chair's insurance does not keep it under 3 when the structural row is what grew. What does: os's 3.4 GHz cap on the breeding cores (the same core-seconds at 12 to 14 W), os's burst pacing if Dynamic Boost is utilization-keyed, and cpu's operator sort. The decision is cpu's at breed_bench: structural above 9,000 at the mean body opens the frequency cap and burst pacing first, recipes only if the parametric row is above 3,000. The larger unknown is not cycles but whether the boost controller charges the RTX for 3 cores at 25% duty as if busy, which os's row 19a measures and which moves the tax between 5 and 21% for the same work.

### 19. Bucketed queues: one lost-ref path and one stall path

Lost ref: in data's pseudo-code a lane does atomicAdd(head), gets `got`, spins while got >= tail, and returns when the drain flag rises. Its `got` is now above tail and claimed. The host later appends refs at those positions and relaunches: position `got` is behind the new head and is never taken; the block's remaining count never reaches zero; the completion word never flips; ring-order absorption stops behind it forever. What has to change: after a drain the host sets head = tail on every bucket before relaunch (the kernel has exited, so no race), and every claimed-but-unserved index is re-appended. A hard check: at every block completion the host asserts head <= tail per bucket. Double take is impossible (one atomic per ref).

Stall: 48 buckets (width 4 over 1 to 64 muscles, three classes) share 368 warps by lane-step share, so a bucket under 0.3% gets no warp and its refs wait for a re-split that fires on a 10-point share move, possibly never. One creature in a rare bucket blocks its block's completion and, through ring order, every block behind it; "a warp whose bucket empties retires" makes it worse as a block drains. What has to change: a lane whose bucket is dry takes from the next bucket up within its class (one extra width of divergence, guaranteed progress), and every non-empty bucket gets at least one warp at launch. Detection: block completion latency p99 against its mean in the stage log; a block older than one ring depth is the alarm.

### 20. 60 FPS: what survives the scheduling changes

Nothing in Design P touches the UI thread's critical path: the snapshot is a swap under a mutex held for microseconds, commit and par_sort run on the worker and the pool, the window renders on the Radeon. Two things survive on paper. cuMemAllocHost for a grown arena page-locks 100 to 300 MB, 50 to 150 ms, synchronously on the engine thread; it stalls neither kernels nor the UI, but it is a chain spike, so arenas should grow one block ahead. And the HPET clocksource at 1.4 us per read: harmless at the engine's 1 ms poll, ruinous for any per-creature timestamp on the absorb path (2 M reads/s is 2.8 core-seconds per second). Gate: the frame-time histogram at p99 under 16.7 ms and control latency at p99 under 20 ms at full rate, plus no Instant::now inside per-creature loops, enforced by a grep in CI.

## Defence of K, only where the brief is wrong

Item 1, spills. The old per-thread kernel's 4.5 KB of local memory was runtime-indexed arrays: every node access went to local memory. That is a different thing from a register allocator spilling 10 to 15 scalars at the contact phase's peak, which costs one STL and one LDL per spilled value per use, about 30 to 60 MIO instructions per substep of 3,100, 1 to 2%, in an L1 that has 50 KB left after the 77 KB carveout (64 B x 512 lanes = 32 KB of spill fits). The gate is 32 B per lane because above it the spill starts hitting the muscle loop, not only the contact phase. If it fails, W = 2 runs at 3 blocks per SM (12 warps, 170 registers, no spill): a latency-hiding loss of about 10% while clock-bound and near zero at the cap. 365 M becomes 330 M, not a row.

Item 2, issue rate. The lane-group kernel's 20 to 32% is an issue-active figure, so its lanes are already not the point; the throughput gain of K is the lanes (4 to 6x fewer executed slots), and the issue rate only needs to rise from about 30 to 45%. The mechanisms: the 35 syncwarps per substep are gone (each drains the warp's pipeline), the 250 shuffles per substep become 9 per creature-substep, and the dependent level-by-level chains become 16 independent muscles and 8 independent tree solves that the scheduler can interleave. The take-up stall of item 10 is the one new stall, and DMA removes it. If nsys shows under 35% issue-active on the stub, the table drops 22% and stays one row down.

Item 7, in-kernel confirmation. The GPU work is not timing-invariant (a stale record causes an extra confirmation); the score is, because the host verdict still asks for any confirmation the record cascade needs and ignores one it does not. The runtime substep length must be passed as precomputed DT and HS floats in Params, because a runtime `1.0f / rate` under `--prec-div=false` is not correctly rounded and would break bit equality with the 4x kernel. Against data's 3 reserved slots (3% of the GPU forever for 1.6% of steps), it costs 0.3% and no slots. It is not load-bearing: if T5 is late, data's slots do the job and the ring is 0.2 s deeper.

Item 9, kernel count. Conceded in part. Per world: 3 classes x scoring or recording x 60 Hz or 4x, 12 kernels at 1 to 2 s each, scoring first, cached on disk by source hash, so a never-used world costs 2 to 4 s of GPU idle on its first press, as today. Mitigation: prefetch the worlds one button away from the current one at idle priority (14 neighbours x 3 scoring kernels, about 60 s in the background, once per machine).
