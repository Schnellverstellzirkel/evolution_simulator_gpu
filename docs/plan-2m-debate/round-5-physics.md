# Round 5, physics: the design I back

## 1. The design

One physics, in maximal coordinates: node positions and velocities as state, rods as exact equality rows eliminated by a chordal LDL, contacts, joint limits, spin cap and stretched tendons as at most 16 extra rows solved by a direct active set, one drift projection per step in which contact and anchored nodes have infinite mass, penetration recovery as a split impulse, anchored friction with an energy store, and four ledgers (momentum, angular momentum about the center of mass, realized friction and projection work, first law in flight with the tightened allowance). Spin-adaptive substeps (L2.5) as the primary target, 2 substeps as the fallback, decided by the ladder on the current kernel before any per-lane code. Per-lane classes W = 2, 4, 8 with gpu; the lane-group kernel serves the tail until W = 8 exists. Screening by R1 to R3 with the nursery exempt, R4 with the emitter-median floor, the audit lane's entrants re-run at 4x before commit. I accept all thirteen rulings; on ruling 7 the fourth ledger is in (section 5).

## 3. Three measurements that would change my mind

1. The ladder. L2.5 median honesty ratio under 0.8 with p10 under 0.5: I drop every 1-substep variant, the design is 2 substeps everywhere, and the cheap-fidelity screen becomes track 5 instead (bar: 95% recall of the full-fidelity top 10%, dilution under 25%).
2. The stub kernel. Issue active under 30% at 16 warps, or spill above 64 B per lane at W = 2: the direct Delassus solve loses, track 3 switches to Gauss-Seidel with a tree solve per row (about 110 registers, 1,000 more instructions per substep), and every rate row drops by 0.7x.
3. The realized-work ledger on today's elites. If the replays of the top 300 show realized positive friction work under 0.1% of muscle work and L0 passes at 0.9, the anchors, the split impulse and the store are complexity without a defect behind them; L0 ships as a setting and tracks 1 and 5 shrink to a day.

## 5. The angular-momentum ledger: in the spec and the audit

ga is right and the infinite-mass rule does not answer it. A position-only rod projection moves the two nodes along the rod direction d by mass-weighted amounts, so the change of angular momentum is Delta L = -mu_red x stretch x (d x v_rel), with mu_red the reduced mass and v_rel the relative velocity, which is perpendicular to d after the rod row. That is -mu_red x stretch x L_rod x omega: always against the rod's own spin, never zero while the rod turns. Per step it removes (omega HS)^2 / 2 of the rod's spin angular momentum: 0.35% at 5 rad/s and 1/60 s, 3% at the cap. Energy and linear momentum are untouched, which is why the other three ledgers miss it. The exploit is orientation control in flight: a body with zero total angular momentum spins a limb one way and its trunk the other; the projection drains the fast limb's share and leaves the trunk turning, about 0.7 rad/s from a leg at the cap, and the landing turns that into distance through a legitimate ground impulse. The infinite-mass rule stops feet sliding and does nothing here.

So the fourth ledger is in the spec as a correction, like the momentum balance, and in the audit as a residual. Per substep: L = sum m_i (r_i - r_com) x v_i before and after the internal operations (rod solve, extras, muscles, damping, projection), with the external torque impulses (contacts, drag, buoyancy about the center; gravity and wind are uniform and give none) subtracted. The residual is removed as one uniform rotation about the center of mass, Delta omega = -residual / I_com with I_com = sum m_i |r_i - r_com|^2. Cost: about 14 flops per node per substep plus the two sums, 2 to 3% of a 6-node step, no cross-lane traffic in a per-lane kernel. The correction adds kinetic energy of order I_com Delta omega^2 / 2, which the first-law ledger sees and takes back in flight. physics_audit reports the residual before correction with the 1e-5 kg m^2/s per step bar, and the correction's magnitude per step, so a projection that drains too much shows as a number rather than as a gait. physics3.rs gets a test that every internal row's columns sum to zero force and zero torque about any point, which is the algebraic form of the same invariant.

With the ledger in place the projection stays position-only, which keeps it cheap; a velocity-consistent projection would inject momentum and energy instead.
