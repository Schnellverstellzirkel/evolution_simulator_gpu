# Round 4, CPU domain: red team

## Design K

### 1. Registers and spills

The claim: 140 needed at W = 2 under a 128 bound, 10 to 15 spilled, "L1 resident and cheap". The history on this machine says otherwise twice. The first persistent-lane engine compiled to 155 to 168 registers and ran at 148k against 244k creatures/s (rejected-ideas). The old per-thread kernel reached 243 to 255 registers with up to 4.5 KB of local memory and was 1.65x slower than today's lane-group kernel, which itself sits at 128 registers with 24 to 32 B of spill and measures 20 to 32% issue. So the stub gate (128 registers, 32 B spill) describes today's kernel's register state, and today's kernel is the thing K must beat by 6 to 10x.

Why a spill is not L1-cheap here: a spill is a store and a later load through the LSU, and the shared-memory muscle scatter and the L2 record reads already own the MIO queue. 15 spilled registers is 60 B per lane, not 32 B. The number at which it bites: the largest live set is the Delassus rows and the factor, inside the active-set loop, so each re-solve pays 15 extra loads at about 30 cycles on a dependent chain: 3 rounds x 15 x 30 is 1,350 cycles of latency per substep per warp against about 3,200 instructions, hidden only if the other 3 warps on the scheduler are out of phase. They are not, because warps run the same program on bodies sorted to be alike.

At 64 B per lane the compiler has spilled the whole factor. Then W = 2 goes to 3 blocks per SM (12 warps, 170 registers, gpu's fallback), and the facts file has the price: halving warps cost 0.66x on the old kernel; 16 to 12 warps is about 0.85x. 365M becomes about 310M at the top of the range and the table drops half a row. Detection: `warp_regs.rs` prints registers and spill bytes per kernel; the stub must print the spill byte count per program region (ptxas -v plus `cuobjdump -sass | grep STL` counted by section), not one total. What must change if it fails: the muscle state (16 registers as 16-bit pairs) goes to shared memory at 4 B per muscle per lane, which is 64 B per lane, 8 KB per block, within the 150 B per lane budget, at the price of one shared load per muscle per substep.

### 2. The 45% issue rate

The lane-group kernel measures 20 to 32% at 16 warps. K asks for 45% at the same warp count with the same MIO-heavy shape (shared scatter, L2 record reads, an active-set loop). The round-3 files name what is removed (shuffles, syncwarps, idle tree lanes) but not what covers the remaining stalls. What keeps issue near 30% is correlated stalls: the four warps on a scheduler run the same program on bodies sorted to be alike, so they reach the muscle record read (24 B per muscle per substep from L2, 0.7 to 0.8 TB/s by K's own count, about 40% of AD107's L2 bandwidth) together and all four wait 250 to 400 cycles under that load. Latency is hidden by warps or by ILP; K has 4 warps per scheduler and a dependent LDL chain, so it needs the warps out of phase, which take-up regeneration provides only after the first falls.

The number at which the table drops a row: at 30% issue instead of 45% every K rate scales by 0.67: W = 2 at 245 to 410M, harmonic mean with the cap 200 to 335M, and the chair's rows become 0.6 to 1.0M/s at 2 substeps with R1 to R3, 1.1 to 1.9M/s at 1 substep. That is the row the stub must rule out. Detection: the stub gate as written, plus one more reading: nsys `smsp__warp_issue_stalled_long_scoreboard` and `mio_throttle` shares at 16 warps. What must change if issue lands at 30 to 35%: the muscle constants move from L2 into shared memory in 16-bit form (gpu's packed record, which ga must accept as quantization), which removes the correlated L2 stall; at 16 muscles x 8 fields x 2 B that is 256 B per lane, over the 150 B budget, so the class would run 12 warps. There is no free fix.

### 3. Warp-maximum loop bounds

data's bucketing by muscle count in widths of 4 fixes one of five bounds. The others at generation 51:

- Node count within the class: sorted by exact count, no waste at W = 2. Fine.
- Contact and extra rows, up to 12: this is the big one. physics counted the Delassus rows (500) and the direct solve (300) at 4 contacts, 8 rows. Row building scales with the row count, the LDL with its cube: at the warp's maximum of 12 rows the two cost about 750 and 1,000, 950 more per substep, a third of the 2,900-instruction step. A warp mixes hoppers (0 contacts in flight, the top elites are airborne 40 to 67% of the time per docs/anatomy-operators.md) with sliders (4 contacts plus limit rows), so the warp maximum is near 8 to 12 for most substeps while the lane mean is 3 to 4. physics allowed 10% for this; the arithmetic says 20 to 35% at generation 51.
- Active-set re-solves, up to 3: the warm start brings the mean to 0.2 per substep (physics), but the warp runs the maximum over 32 creatures; with 32 draws at 0.2 the probability that at least one needs a re-solve is 1 - 0.8^32, which is 99.9%. So the warp pays 1 re-solve nearly every substep and often 2. That is the "0.2 re-solves" claim made void by the warp maximum: 4 to 8% becomes 15 to 25% of the substep.
- Flight or ground: a per-lane mask, cheap, unless the flight step (1 substep in flight, 2 on the ground) is warp-uniform; then a warp with one grounded creature runs 2 substeps for all 32 and the flight lever is gone for mixed warps.

Sum: the 15% allowance at W = 2 covers node sorting and muscle buckets and nothing else; the extras and re-solves add 25 to 45% on top at generation 51. Detection: a histogram per substep of (lane's own row count, warp maximum row count) and (lane re-solves, warp re-solves) on the stub with real bodies from a mature save, before any rate is quoted. What must change: sort take-up by the parent's airborne share (in the elite's metrics already) so warps hold hoppers with hoppers; and cap re-solves at 2 with the third round replaced by clamping, which physics must rule on.

### 9. Kernel variants and cold compiles

Keying today: 11 world flag bits, 3 classes, 2 fidelities (60 Hz and the 4x confirmation), record or not: 12 variants per world set, 1 to 2 s of NVRTC each on one thread, cached as cubins under ~/.cache/evolution-simulator/cuda by source hash (498 MB already). K makes the substep length a runtime parameter, which folds the confirmation in: 6 per set. The sets are 2^11 in principle, but the player reaches them one button at a time and autochange adds one effect per 100 generations, so the prefetch thread can compile the autochange successor ahead. The cold case is a button press to a set never seen on this machine: the drain takes 20 ms, then three class kernels compile, 3 to 6 s serial, about 2 s on three threads. Today's cost is the same compile plus a 4.7 s ring discard, so K is not worse; the player sees a rate at zero for 2 s with no message. What must change: compile the standard variant of each class first and the record variant after (replays can wait, scoring cannot), on three threads, show the compile in the status text, and cap the cache at about 200 entries by age.

## Design P

### 12. The generation boundary and the mapped tail

What `end_generation` does today, sized from the code: `push_archive_stats` sorts the global archive's entries (about 10k, 1 ms) and builds percentiles and a histogram over elites; `prune_lineage` walks every elite's ancestry chain and retains the lineage map (tens of thousands of entries, 5 to 20 ms); `graduate_nurseries` and `migrate_islands` copy hundreds of elites (ms); `next_screen` runs `select_nth_unstable` over the screen log of 3M floats on one thread, 30 to 50 ms. In P the additions are ml's fit at the boundary (a 6-feature discriminant over the audit rows, 1 in 128 of 24M is 190k rows, under 10 ms) and ga's per-cell bar table (5 x 1,440 cells, a neighbourhood minimum over 81 cells each, 1 ms). The 1,024 emitters add nothing at the boundary: their 5,000 tells per generation run per block in ring order as samples accumulate, at about 0.2 M cycles each, and the boundary only refreshes the emitter weights and `last_used_generation`. Estimate: 60 to 120 ms serial on the worker, at most 200 ms with a mature lineage map. The 0.3 s ring floor covers it with 100 ms of margin, and the boundary block also carries a normal 25 ms chain. Where it breaks: a boundary over 300 ms starves 9,216 lanes for the excess. Detection: `EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED` already prints "Generation boundary: x s"; the gate is under 150 ms on a mature save, and the two fixes if it fails are a streaming percentile for the screen bar (a 1 cm histogram of screen_x updated per block, O(1) at the boundary, which is the same structure as ga's streaming arena bar) and `prune_lineage` moved off the boundary onto the pool. I could not measure a mature boundary this debate (section 0 of my round 3); it is the first number the profile run produces once a version-43 mature save exists.

### 13. The host tax at 12,000 structural cycles

At 12,000 per structural child (the top of the 2x range) the weighted emit is 0.6 x 2,300 + 0.4 x 12,000 = 6,200, plus plan 600 and commit 500: 7,300 cycles per creature, 21.9 G cycles per 3M, 5.5 core-seconds at 4 GHz, 3.65 cores busy at 2M/s, a tax of 9 to 10% at the cap, 4.3 cores at os's 3.4 GHz cap. The recipes insurance removes the parametric row (4.2 G) and lands at 17.7 G, 4.4 core-seconds, 2.95 cores: it barely reaches 3, because the structural row is what grew. So the chair's trigger ("over 8,000 weighted opens recipes") is aimed at the wrong row. The rule I propose: measure the two rows separately in breed_bench at the end of track 2. If the parametric row is over 3,000 at the mean body, recipes on the RTX open (they remove that row entirely). If the structural row is over 9,000, recipes do not help enough; the options are a SoA rewrite of the operators' inner loops (the copy-limb, graft and retain paths, about 1,500 lines, 1 to 2 weeks) or data's structural port (weeks), and os's frequency cap converts the extra core-seconds into fewer watts either way (the same work at 3.4 GHz costs 55% of the energy, so 3.65 cores at 3.4 GHz is about the package power of 2.5 cores at 4.5 GHz). Owner of the decision: the orchestrator, at the track 2 gate, with the breed_bench rows and os's power slope in hand; the date is the end of track 2, about two weeks in. Until then every table should carry 3 to 3.7 cores, not 2 to 3.

The standing facts: the main target is 9.3 GB, a release-fast seed is 4.5 GB, the CUDA kernel cache is 498 MB, ~/.cargo is 891 MB, and of the 70 worktree directories under /home/amipo/workspace only four still carry a target (warp2, cuda-only, ui, finish: 23 GB together); the other 66 are bare checkouts. The filesystem is ext4, so `cp --reflink=auto` makes a full 4.5 GB copy per seed, not a shared one. The plan's 6 to 8 worktrees need 27 to 36 GB plus 4.5 GB for a version-42 build for the save42 measurements: that fits in 220 GB with room. What the owner should do before the tracks start, none of it blocking now: remove the 66 finished worktrees (`git worktree prune` after deleting the directories) and the four stale targets (23 GB), cap the kernel cache at about 200 entries by age (one line), and keep a floor: no agent seeds a worktree when free space is under 30 GB, which is the check that was missing when the disk hit 98%.

## 23. Glitch risk from the host half

Every host-side change in P that could let an exploit into an archive, with its guard and gap:

- Counter-based RNG and the 12-uniform gaussian: changes the noise distribution (tails cut at 6 sigma), not what can be scored. No path to a glitch. Guard: none needed; ga's search_ab on 10 seeds is the quality check.
- Bounded arrays: a body past 32 nodes or 96 muscles is refused at the operator instead of at pack. Same limit, earlier. Guard: the operator property tests.
- The vector parametric path: the one real gap. If its clamps differ from `repair`'s by one rule (short at most long, period at least the minimum, tendon in 0 to 1), a child carries a gene the physics never saw in tests. The git log has the precedent: a 1 m tendon on a 0.01 m muscle launched elites 5 m in one step. Guard: `repair` still runs on every child after the vector loop, and a property test asserts `child == repair(child)` for 1M vectorized children. Gap closed if that test exists; open if the loop is trusted alone.
- Commit on candidates against a stale occupant table: the superset argument holds for behavior cells (occupants only rise). Gap: the morphology reserve. A structural child that enters the reserve does not beat any cell occupant, so the kernel's candidate bit misses it and the reserve starves. Fix: one more float per island in the device table, the reserve floor, and a candidate if the score beats the island's reserve floor. Without it the reserve dies silently, which is a search loss, not a glitch.
- Overlapped absorb and breed through the versioned slab: a child bred from a parent replaced before its commit is the same case as today's ring lag. No new path.
- Parent-major and operator-sorted order: children are functions of their slot; order cannot change a child. Guard: the breed_bench digest at 1 and 14 threads.
- Threads and pinning: no effect on any score.

The host cannot admit a glitch that the kernel and the verdict would not have admitted today; its gaps are a starved reserve (fix above) and an unclamped vector child (test above).
