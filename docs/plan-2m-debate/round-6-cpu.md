# Round 6, CPU domain: revision after phase 0

## What the host rows say

After host-bounded, breed_bench on save42 gives 10,957 cycles per child: parametric 8,084, structural 17,260. The rows are honest measurements of code that has the containers and the counter-based stream but none of the vector path or the ordering. Reading origin/main: a gaussian is now `twelve_uniforms` over three splitmix draws keyed by gene, so a parametric child at the generation-51 mean body (8.8 nodes, 19.1 muscles, about 340 genes) spends roughly 340 x 18 cycles, about 6,000 of its 8,084, in scalar integer hashing and summing, and the rest in clamps, `clone_from`, `repair` and the write. A structural child runs its operator (up to four tries, donors read in place) and then `local_mutation` at scale 0.035, which is the same 6,000-cycle parametric pass again, so about 11,000 of its 17,260 is the operator and 6,000 is the embedded noise pass.

That decomposition is what host-vector and host-order act on, and it is why the two triggers of ruling 11 fired early: they were set for rows after both tracks, and the rows were measured before either.

## Q5. What the next two tracks reach

host-vector (16-lane AVX-512 over the gene-keyed draws, the same stream, so the digest does not change): the 6,000-cycle noise pass becomes about 340 genes x 50 integer ops / 16 lanes at about 2 ops per cycle, about 550 cycles, plus the clamps and wraps in the same loop, about 300. Parametric: 8,084 to about 2,300 (clone_from 150, noise 850, repair and canonical order 900, write 150, the CMA path's `values` build 250). Structural: the embedded pass drops the same 5,000, 17,260 to about 12,000. I hold the parametric gate at 2,500.

host-order (operator-sorted, parent-major, chunks of 512): 1.3x on the operator part from a warm instruction cache and donors in L2, 12,000 to about 9,000 structural, and the plan row sorted by parent so `sample_local_competitive` and the mate draw hit L2. Structural gate 9,000, not the 7,000 I wrote in round 5; the 17,260 measurement moved my estimate of the operator part from 8,000 to 11,000.

Weighted after both: 0.6 x 2,300 + 0.4 x 9,000 = 5,000 per emitted child, against 10,957 today.

The plan row needs its own number. host-profile reports plan at 57 ms per 196k block on the serial chain. If that is 8-thread wall it is 9,000 cycles per slot; if it is one thread it is 1,200. breed_bench prints "ns wall and ns of CPU per slot"; that line, on save42, is the first thing host-order reads, because a 9,000-cycle plan is the largest host row after structural and my 600-cycle estimate was a code reading. Either way host-order owns plan_offspring and the gate is 2,000 cycles per slot as ruled.

Recipes on the RTX: not now. The parametric row is above 3,000 because the vector path does not exist yet; host-vector is one to two weeks and is expected to land at 2,300, while recipes are a two to three week port plus a versioned device slab. The decision date is host-vector's gate: if the parametric row measures above 3,000 at the mean body after host-vector, recipes open. And with the kernel revised to 0.3 to 0.6M/s (below), the host at today's rows is 1 to 1.7 busy cores, so recipes would buy 1 to 2% of GPU rate; they matter only once the kernel row is back above about 1M/s. Both conditions, dated: recipes open at host-vector's gate (about two weeks) if the row is above 3,000 and the plan's kernel row is above 1M/s.

The structural row: not the port, not yet the SoA rewrite. host-vector and host-order are expected to bring 17,260 to about 9,000. If after host-order the row is still above 12,000, the SoA rewrite of the five operators that breed_bench's per-operator table ranks highest by cycle share (the copy-limb, graft and junction families, about 1,500 lines, two weeks) opens. The port opens only if the SoA pass leaves it above 12,000 and the kernel row is above 1.2M/s, because below that the host is one core and a second implementation of 64 operators buys nothing measurable. The frequency cap is os's row and opens whenever the host measures above 2 busy cores at the running rate, which at 0.3 to 0.6M/s it does not. Owner of the decision: the orchestrator at host-order's gate, about three weeks from now, with the per-operator rows and os's rows 1 to 3 in hand.

What the archive and boundary numbers change. The archive stage is 55 ms median per 196k block (island offers 13 to 15, prefilter 14 to 16, global offers 13, CMA tell 8.5, novelty refresh 5.5 median and 12.9 max), the boundary 22 to 41 ms with prune_lineage at 21 ms, candidates 2.1%. All under their gates, so: no incremental novelty, no pool-side boundary, the ring floor stays 0.3 s. The whole chain today is about 290 ms per 196k block (archive 55, plan 57, emit 150, write 25); after host-bounded's rows on 8 threads the emit is about 67 ms, so the chain is about 200 ms per 196k, 100 ms per 100k, and 5 x p95 lands near 0.6 s. Since ruling 4 fixes depth at experiment start, the constant to record is 0.6 s at 100k blocks, and it binds nothing until the kernel is above about 1M/s. The candidate-bit track: the bit would delete the prefilter pass (15 ms) and shrink the two offer walks (26 ms) to the 2.1% of candidates, about 35 of the 55 ms. At 0.3 to 0.6M/s a 196k block is 0.3 to 0.65 s of GPU work and the 55 ms is fully overlapped, so the track is deferred: it opens when the measured chain p95 exceeds 40% of a block's GPU time, which by today's rows is above 1.5M/s. The 41 ms boundary needs nothing.

## Q1 from the breeder's side

If the kernel domain buckets warps by body plan, the breeder must emit a plan key per child and nothing else changes. The key is a canonical hash of the child's rooted bone tree: the parent array in canonical bone order (the breeder already produces canonical order, and `qd::Topology` already holds nodes and edges), hashed with the AHU tree encoding so mirror-equivalent limbs coincide, about 100 cycles per child, 1% of a structural child and 4% of a parametric one; parametric children inherit the parent's key at zero cost. The class sort at submit is a counting sort by (key, muscle bucket) over 196k children, about 3 ms on one thread, or free if the breeder writes each child straight into its bucket's arena at emit, which host-arenas' preallocated arenas allow (arenas grow one block ahead, ruling 12). How many keys cover 90% of a mature ring is measurable now, because breed_bench loads older saves (`load_any_version`): count distinct keys among save42's 7,500 elites and among one bred block of 196k, and print the cumulative share by rank. My estimate: 50 to 200 distinct trees among elites (the top 1,000 of one island share one plan per docs/anatomy-operators.md) and 500 to 2,000 among a block's offspring, because a quarter of children are one tree edit away from an elite; the top 30 keys cover 60 to 70% and the top 100 about 90%. That argues against one compiled kernel per exact plan (30 plans x 3 classes x 6 variants per world is 540 compiles at 1 to 2 s, 10 to 20 minutes cold per world set) and for the middle path: a general kernel whose warp reads its plan's parent array, elimination order and muscle end lists from a per-warp shared table loaded at take-up, with warp-uniform branches, so the LOP3 bit tests go without any per-plan compile. The breeder's key makes that grouping possible; the kernel domain decides whether the table beats the packed word.

## Revised host tax and ceiling

Per emitted creature after host-vector and host-order: emit 5,000, plan at most 2,000 (measured, not yet), commit and bookkeeping 500 (the 55 ms per 196k is 1,100 cycles per result on 8 threads; the candidate bit would halve it later). About 7,500 cycles, 22.5 G per 3M, 5.6 core-seconds at 4 GHz. Today, before the two tracks: about 13,500, 40 G per 3M, 10 core-seconds.

Ceiling from my seat, with the chair's revised kernel rows (per-lane at about 96M on the generation-51 mix, 3x the lane-group's 27M): 2 substeps with R1 to R3 gives 96M / 300 = 0.32M/s before tax, 0.30M/s after; the best row (L2.5 at 1.5 to 1.7x and R1 to R4 at 235 steps) gives 0.61 to 0.69M/s before tax, 0.58 to 0.65M/s after. At generation 100 with the 0.65 to 0.82 muscle slope: 0.2M/s and 0.38 to 0.53M/s. The host is not the wall on any of these rows and will not be until the kernel row is above about 1.2M/s; at that point the tax column above is the argument for recipes and the SoA rewrite, and not before.

What would flip me: a breed_bench plan row above 4,000 cycles per slot on save42 (then plan, not emit, is the host's first problem and host-order starts there); a structural row above 12,000 after host-order (SoA rewrite opens); os's rows showing more than 8% GPU loss per busy core (every date above moves earlier by one track).
