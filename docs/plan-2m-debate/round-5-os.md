# Round 5, OS domain: the design I back, my tracks, and the owner's calibration session

## 1. The design

I back the converged pipeline with every ruling of round 4: DMA per block into VRAM with a prologue unpack (ruling 2, which supersedes my zero-copy gene reads; I keep the results ring in registered process memory, which ruling 6 confirms), queue tail and drain flag in VRAM written with cuStreamWriteValue32 (ruling 3, which is the fix for the spin I attacked), frozen ring constants per experiment (ruling 4), registered arenas and the requeue-from-unabsorbed recovery with a pause after three failures (ruling 6), L2.5 as the primary substep target (8), the growth-step rule counting nodes and muscles as the owner's candidate (9), nursery and immigrant exemptions (10), the host tax carried at 15 to 21% until my rows exist (11), the stage-log lines and no Instant::now in per-creature loops (12), and the disk rules (13). I reject nothing. On ruling 6's open choice, my number for data: in-process is fine only if a hung persistent kernel can be ended; on Linux with the open kernel module, cuCtxDestroy from another thread blocks behind the running kernel, so the practical in-process kill is none, and the helper process (igpu) is the only path that returns in bounded time. If data measures cuCtxDestroy returning within 2 s on a deliberately hung kernel, in-process wins; if it blocks, the helper process is the design.

## 3. Three measurements that would change my mind

1. Rows 11 and 12. If K's mix costs under 0.45 nJ per warp instruction, the budget is 5T and my 3T lower bound is withdrawn; if over 1.0 nJ, the plan's table drops a row and I would push the MIO share of the kernel above the substep ladder as the first lever.
2. Rows 2 and 3 (4 and 8 CPU threads). If the tax at 4 threads is under 9% without a frequency cap, I withdraw the cpufreq cap and burst pacing and the host tax goes back to 8% in every table; if it is over 15%, the cap or the pacing becomes a prerequisite of 2M/s, not a setting.
3. Row 19a (the CPU rows at 20% duty). If bursting the same core-seconds costs the RTX the same as steady load, Dynamic Boost is energy-keyed, burst pacing is withdrawn, and only the frequency cap remains; if it costs under a third, cpu's breeder should pace itself per ring block.

## 4. Honest ceiling

Assumptions: the chair's kernel range (300 to 450M creature-steps/s at 2 substeps for the generation-51 mix, times 1.5 to 1.7 at L2.5), the steps ladder at 300 (R1 to R3) to 235 (with R4 at the 50% fire rate), the host tax at 15% until rows 1 to 3 exist, a thermal steady state at 0.93, and the muscle slope of 0.65 to 0.82 by generation 100 unless the growth rule holds it.

Generation 51, 2 substeps: 0.8 to 1.0M/s at 3T, 1.2 to 1.5M/s at 5T (R1 to R3); 1.0 to 1.3M/s and 1.5 to 1.9M/s with R4. Generation 51, L2.5: 1.3 to 1.6M/s at 3T, 1.9 to 2.4M/s at 5T (R1 to R3); 1.6 to 2.0M/s and 2.4 to 3.1M/s with R4. Generation 100 without a muscle rule: multiply by 0.65 to 0.82; with the growth rule holding the mean near 20 muscles, by 0.9. So 2M/s sustained at generation 51 on this laptop needs L2.5 plus either the 5T budget or R4 at its 50% rate plus the host tax brought under 9%; at generation 100 it needs the growth rule as well. At 3T with 2 substeps the machine tops out near 1.0 to 1.3M/s, and I would tell the owner that number rather than the goal.

## 5. The owner's calibration session, reduced

Before the session: no agent builds, no agent GPU work, the game paused by the pause script for each 5-minute window; each row is 60 s of p2_speed on the 262k dump from the power worktree, logged by tools/power-sample.sh (clocks, power, live limit, limiter reasons, APU PPT). The owner's actions, in the order the plan needs the numbers:

1. Rows 11 and 12 (one pause, no root): run `examples/power_probe fma` then `power_probe mio` for 30 s each. This fixes the budget. Row 13 (integer and shuffle) in the same pause if time remains.
2. Rows 0 to 3 (one pause, no root): p2_speed alone, then with tools/cpu-burn at 2, 4 and 8 threads. Row 4 (16 threads) and row 19a (4 and 8 threads at 20% duty) in a second pause. This fixes the host tax and decides burst pacing.
3. Row 19 (one 10-minute window the owner grants once, no root): p2_speed looped for 10 minutes. This fixes what "sustained" means and whether the SM clock should be pinned.
4. Radeon rows 7 to 10 (one pause, root for DPM): as root, `echo manual > /sys/class/drm/card1/device/power_dpm_force_performance_level` and select 800, 1,100 and 2,700 MHz in pp_dpm_sclk between rows; igpu's burner started from a terminal at 100% duty for rows 7 to 9 and at 30% duty plus 4 CPU threads for row 10; restore `auto` afterwards. This closes or keeps the Radeon fallback.
5. Settings rows (one pause, root): row 5, `cpupower frequency-set -u 3.4GHz` (or scaling_max_freq on every policy) with 8 threads, then restore; row 6, EPP balance_performance, then restore; row 17, `nvidia-smi -lmc` at the lowest listed memory clock, then at 5,001, then `-rmc`; row 18, platform_profile max-power with the live limit read, then restore. Each row is kept only if it moves the rate by 3% or more.
6. Optional, no pause: after the next reboot, `dmesg | grep -i "tsc warp"`; if the warp is gone the clocksource returns to TSC on its own. If it is not, check for a BIOS update; no kernel parameter until then.

Five pauses and one 10-minute window, about 45 minutes of the owner's GPU across an evening; root is needed only in steps 4 and 5.
