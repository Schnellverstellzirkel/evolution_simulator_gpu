## K7. In-kernel confirmation

Failure 1: the GPU work is not invariant, and gpu's file says it is. The lane confirms when its score beats the island record in the occupant table; which table version a lane sees when its creature ends depends on timing. Two runs of one seed run different confirmation sets. The score is invariant because the verdict asks for what is missing, so this is a measurement bug, not a determinism bug: gpu's case for the feature is that the host chain p95 drops from 100 to 150 ms to 40 to 60 ms, but with a timing-dependent confirmation set the p95 is timing-dependent too, and worst when the table is stalest, which is under load. Detection: two runs of one seed logging in-kernel confirmations against verdict requests; the ratio will vary by run. Change: size the ring from the worst case (no in-kernel confirmation) and treat the feature as latency insurance only.

Failure 2: the stale-high case. gpu says a stale record only causes an extra confirmation because records only rise. Two places make them fall. `graduate_nurseries` empties every nursery archive every 10 generations, and `reset_search_context` empties the islands on a world change. After a graduation the table holds the old nursery record for at least one block; every nursery creature that would set the new nursery's first record is below it, the kernel skips it, and the round trip returns at the generation boundary, on top of the boundary's own unmeasured cost, for 10% of the slots. Detection: verdict-requested confirmations per block plotted against block index modulo generation; a spike at the boundary every 10 generations. Change: write the table before committing a graduation, or exclude nursery arenas from in-kernel confirmation.

Failure 3: the replay rule. A replay comes from the scoring kernel and matches its score bit for bit (`gpu_replays_show_the_gpu_score`). Today a confirmation and the replay of a fine elite both run the compiled fine variant (`replay_of` with `Fidelity::fine`). An in-kernel confirmation runs through a kernel whose substep length is a runtime parameter. If the replay keeps the compiled variant, contraction and unrolling differ and the replay is not the score: every elite with `fine == true`, a few per generation, visibly not matching its card. Change: the replay of a fine elite runs the runtime-dt path with RECORD on, so the compiled fine variant is deleted and a recording variant of the runtime-dt kernel exists. One more variant for item 9, not fewer.

Cost against 3 reserved slots. In-kernel is 0.3 to 0.8% of the GPU (tens of records per 100k block times 4,800 steps against 23.5M lane-steps); three reserved slots are 3.1% of the SMs, busy or not. In-kernel wins 2 to 3% and loses a runtime-dt substep for every creature (about 0.5%), a second table word, two scores in the result, the stale-high fix and the replay variant. Its 0.3 s ring is worth having only if the ring depth is fixed, which item 19 says it must be anyway. Verdict: build the 3 slots first; revisit in-kernel confirmation only if the measured chain p95 with 3 slots puts a fixed ring above 0.5 s.

## K8. The muscle-count slope

What holds muscles is the physics: ga's table has the elites' p90 falling from 34 at generation 30 to 28 at 50 while the mean rose from 17 to 19, so the physics pushes the tail down, not the mean. Nothing holds offspring: the body cap is on nodes, and the muscle operators (split, add, graft) add muscles without nodes.

Design K's cost is linear in muscles at 70 instructions per muscle per substep, with muscles 50 to 60% of the step at 19. At a mean of 25 and a p90 of 40, the mean body costs 0.45 + 0.55 x 25 / 19 = 1.17x and the p90 body 0.4 + 0.6 x 40 / 29 = 1.23x. With data's buckets holding the warp maximum within 4 of its mean, the generation-100 rate is about 0.82x generation 51: 300 to 450M becomes 245 to 370M, and creatures per second at 235 steps 1.05 to 1.57M. That is a slope, not the old cliff: the old build fell 4x because W doubled at each node class (8 to 16 to 32 lanes per creature) as bodies went from 6 to 9 nodes. Design K is linear in muscles and near-linear in nodes inside a class.

The number nobody has: muscles per offspring at generations 75 and 100 on the new physics. Detection: `size_report` by generation on a long run, printing mean and p90 muscles and the implied rate factor per 10 generations. Change if the slope is real: a muscle-count analogue of ga's cap (the island's largest elite muscle count plus four), a body limit like the node cap, the owner's decision. It would hold the mean within 2 of the elites' mean and the rate factor above 0.9 over 100 generations.

## P17. The reinterpreted Result floats

I grepped every reader. `scheduler::to_metrics` reads fitness, ground_contact, vertical_oscillation, gait_frequency, height_sum, contact_lo, lift_lo, fall_time, head_shake, screen_x, screened and `feet()`. `feet()` counts the bits of `lift_lo` and `lift_hi`. With two fp16 distances in `lift_hi`, `feet()` returns the popcount of a float pair: a wrong feet bin for every creature, a wrong niche, and no error, because the archive accepts any bin. That is the silent break, mine to fix: `feet()` counts `lift_lo` only (32 nodes at most, so `lift_hi` was always zero). Gate: the fixed-seed history test before and after the layout change with the rungs off, identical.

`physics2.rs` and `cpu_v2.rs` use `previous_center_y`, `vertical_extremum`, `vertical_trend` and `gait_turns` as working state inside the struct and write `contact_hi` and `lift_hi` from a u64's high bits. The CPU engine is being deleted; the prototype stays and must move its working state into locals and write the same seven words as the kernel, or a field-by-field comparison differs in seven fields. No test does that comparison: `gpu_repeatability`, `screening` and `simulation` compare fitness bits and elite fitness. `size_report`, `warp_check`, `examples/common`, `ui.rs`, `worker.rs` and `replay_forces.rs` read none of the seven. Saves are safe: `GpuResult` is never serialized. The audit window is stored as 1,024-bin histograms, 128 KB; the chair's "up to 16 MB" was my withdrawn round-3 alternative.

## P18. The rate after a world change

Today the screen bar is disarmed on a world change and rearmed after a quarter generation (`arm_screen_early`), so the game already runs at the 480-step rate for a quarter generation after every press. My rearm needs 8k audit rows at 1 in 128, which is 1M creatures, half a second at 2M/s, a third of today's window. During it R1, R2 and R4 are off.

The per-cell bars are not off for a generation. `reset_search_context` moves every island elite into the reseed queue and `breed_block` puts reseeded elites first, so the elites are re-scored in the new world in the first blocks, and each re-score writes the elite's new rung distances. ga's table refills within the ring depth, 0.3 to 1 s. Until then a cell without a re-scored elite takes the streaming arena bar (ga's T1). So the player sees about a second at a 1.3 to 1.6x lower rate after a press, then the full rate. Not a stall, and smaller than today's window. Watch: steps per creature per block in the stage log over the 20 blocks after a press. Nurseries start over on a world change and need their own 2k audit rows for their per-arena fit (item 14), about 2 s.

## P19. Bucketed queues, and a larger hole

The ref hazard. A take is an atomicAdd on the bucket head, so no two lanes get one ref. The races are around a drain. Taken and never run: a lane takes ref i, sees the drain flag, exits; the host requeues from a head past i; i is never scored in the new world and its block never completes. Taken twice: the host reads the head and requeues [head, tail) while a lane is between its atomicAdd and the trial. Both close with two rules data's spec lacks: the host reads heads only after every kernel of the class has exited (an event, not the flag), and a lane that finds the drain flag after its atomicAdd runs the creature anyway, tags the result old-world, and the host requeues exactly the refs whose results carry the old tag. Detection: a block whose completion word never flips, or a creature scored twice, with a world change every block for 100 blocks.

The larger hole. data's determinism rules fix genes, config, bar table and world tag at breed time and make the occupant table result-invariant. They say nothing about the two quantities derived from measurements: the block size (50 ms of GPU work from a running mean of the rate) and the ring depth (5 times the p95 host chain). Both change a seed's history. A slot's child is bred at the absorption of its block from the archive as it stands then, and how many blocks were absorbed before that depends on how many are in flight and how large they are. Tells per 200 samples remove the block from the tell, not from the breed-absorb lag. Two runs of one seed with different measured rates, which is every pair of runs, produce different histories. That breaks the owner's rule and data's own gate, `two_continuous_runs_of_one_seed_agree`. Change: block size and ring depth are experiment constants set at the start (from the first measured rate, then frozen and saved), or they change only at a generation boundary with the value recorded in the history so a load reproduces it. No rate cost. This is the one stop-ship item in P.

## 14. R1 and the nursery

The chair's case is real: a slow-start lineage stopped at R1 for 3 generations loses 30% of its 10-generation window, with only its 1 in 128 audit children through. Three changes, none an exemption.

The discriminant is fit per arena type, islands and nurseries, both from audit rows: nursery rows are 2.3k per generation, 18k over the window, plenty for 6 features. A random body's features (no energy spent, no contacts, zero distance) are not the islands' distribution, and one shared fit would call every fresh body broken.

The nursery miss budget is 1 in 10,000, and a nursery band's breaker trips on an absolute count (10 entrant misses in one generation), not a rate over 3 generations, because a cohort's entrants are few and its window short. Cost: R1's nursery share falls from about 40% to 25%; at 10% of creatures that is 0.10 x 0.15 x 240 = 4 steps per creature.

R2 is off for nursery slots: its seventh feature is the parent's neighbourhood bar, which a fresh body lacks; ga already routes nurseries to the arena bar. Cost 0.10 x 0.25 x 150 = 4 steps.

What the dump must show for R1 to stay on for nurseries: among nursery rows with `entered == 2`, the share below the nursery threshold at the 1 in 10,000 budget under 0.1% per cadence band, and `kept` per cohort not falling in the equal-time A/B. Otherwise R1 is off for nurseries at 0.10 x 0.4 x 240 = 10 steps per creature, 4% of the mature mean. That is the whole exposure; the dump decides it in one pass.

## 15. R4 and the CMA

The exposure is smaller than today's. Screened children already enter the tell with their 5 s distance, 80% of every exploring emitter's samples; R4 adds a 10 s truncation on part of the remaining 20%. In `tell_exploring` mu is half the samples with log weights, so when more than half of an emitter's samples are stopped some carry weight ranked by 10 s pace. Whether that drifts toward sprinters depends on one number, Spearman of d(600) against final among survivors: 0.887 at 5 s, above 0.95 at 10 s I expect, and the dump prints it. Above 0.95 a 10 s and a 20 s ranking agree on the top half within CMA's sampling noise at mu of 100.

The chair's case, an emitter whose whole population is stopped, is bounded by the code: `EmitterStats::stale()` fires after 5 stagnant batches and the emitter is recycled. The drift acts for at most 5 tells on an emitter that is failing anyway. Detection in `search_ab` within 40 generations: entrant share from exploring emitters and the best-distance curve against control, and the median of final / d(600) among entrants, which rises if sprinters are selected against. Change if it shows: R4 off for children of an emitter whose previous tell had more than half its samples stopped, one bit per plan.

## 23. Glitch risk, my domain

- R1 to R4 stop trials only; a stopped creature enters no archive and its score is a true simulated distance. They cannot admit an exploit. Their risk is a selection bias in what survives (sprinters, fast starters). Guard: entrant misses per 10k per cadence band, the median of final / d(t) among entrants, the top 1% and 10% kept, every generation. Gap: the bias numbers are read by a developer; the player sees nothing.
- The audit lane: 1 in 128 creatures run with no rung and enter as today. No new path.
- The parent-profile feature and the fit: change what is stopped, never what is scored.
- The reinterpreted floats: never read by scoring; the `feet()` break changes niches, not scores. Guard: the fixed-seed history test.
- In-kernel confirmation, if built: the score stays min(standard, confirm), but the replay of a fine elite must come from the same runtime-dt path. Guard: `gpu_replays_show_the_gpu_score` extended to fine elites.
- The guard I would add for the whole plan: `physics_audit` on every new island record once per generation, printing friction work along the slip and first-law corrections, so a hole opened by K's physics changes (anchored friction, the drift projection) shows as a ledger number before it shows as a 400 m kick sled. A few hundred replays per generation on the replay slot, and the only guard that watches the physics rather than the search.
