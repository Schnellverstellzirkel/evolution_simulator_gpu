# Round 5, data flow: the design I back, tracks, measurements, ceiling, the helper decision

## 1. The design

The breeder writes genes once into process memory registered with cuMemHostRegister (per-block SoA arenas, 24 B headers, a sort order by class and exact muscle count); the engine DMAs each block to VRAM on a copy engine at submit, a prologue kernel unpacks 32 creatures per warp into VRAM lane records, and persistent per-class kernels take records through bucketed queues (a head per class and muscle bucket, the tail and the drain flag in VRAM written with cuStreamWriteValue32, the three requeue rules of ruling 5, the reserve floor word beside the occupant word). Results go to the registered results ring at 80 B with the descriptor bins, the candidate bit and the world tag written last after a system fence, and a completion word per block. Block size and ring depth are experiment constants fixed at experiment start from the first measured rate, saved, and changed only at a generation boundary with the value in the history (ruling 4); the engine measures the rate for the stage log only. Confirmations run on a persistent fine-fidelity kernel in 3 reserved block slots and replays in 1, with background pre-recording of new records (ruling 1). A world change stops appending, drains (20 ms, a 2 s timeout), requeues the un-taken refs, excludes live creatures by tag, and compiles a new world's three standard kernels in parallel with neighbour prefetch (ruling 12). Recovery after a context loss follows ruling 6. I accept rulings 1 to 6 and 11 to 13, take no position on 7 to 10, and reject nothing.

## 3. Three measurements that would change my mind

1. The three-form p2_speed of track 3. If in-kernel unpack from genes is within 2% of records from VRAM on the lane-group kernel, the prologue kernel and the VRAM record buffer (1.5 GB) are unnecessary and take-up unpacks in place; I predicted a 3 to 5% loss.
2. The per-block host chain p95 with the reserved fine kernel. If it is under 60 ms and the confirmation round trip is its largest term, ruling 1's revisit clause opens and in-kernel confirmation comes back in one-implementation form (every confirmation through the kernel, cascades re-queued); I predict 80 to 100 ms with the chain dominated by commit and breed.
3. The generation boundary on a mature archive from EVOLUTION_PROFILE_BREED. Above 0.8 s the boundary moves off the worker thread (streaming quantile for the screen bar, migration on the versioned elite table) before track 4 merges; I predict 150 to 400 ms, 500 to 800 ms on migration generations.

## 4. Ceiling

The data path is not the wall on any row: the host sustains about 4M/s at cpu's breeder numbers and PCIe about 8M/s at 1.35 KB per creature. The ceiling is the kernel, the substep and the host tax, and I take the chair's rows with three assumptions: the per-lane kernel at 300 to 450M creature-steps/s at 2 substeps (3T to 5T), 300 steps per creature under R1 to R3, and the host tax at 15 to 21% until os's rows exist. Then, with my data-path factors (bucketed queues 1.15x, no tails 1.02x, reserved slots 0.96x, prologue 0.99x):

- Generation 51, 2 substeps: 0.95M/s at 3T, 1.45M/s at 5T. L2.5 at 1.5 to 1.7x: 1.5M/s at 3T, 2.3M/s at 5T. L2 at 1.85x: 1.75M/s at 3T, 2.7M/s at 5T.
- Generation 100 with the muscle slope os gave (0.65 to 0.82x) and no growth rule: 0.65 to 1.2M/s at 2 substeps, 1.0 to 1.9M/s at L2.5; with the growth rule holding the mix near generation 51, the generation-51 numbers stand.

Honest reading: 2M/s sustained at generation 51 needs L2 or L2.5 to pass and the budget nearer 5T than 3T, or R4 at about 50% on top of L2.5 at 3T. At 3T with 2 substeps the laptop tops out near 1M/s whatever the data path does.

## 5. The helper-process decision

In-process, with the 2 s drain timeout, on one number: the time cuCtxDestroy takes to return when called from a second thread while a kernel loops forever on this driver (580.178, open kernel module, RTX off the display). NVIDIA's documented recovery for a runaway kernel on a non-display GPU is this pattern: poll an event with a host timeout, then reset the device from the host, which terminates the kernel. The test is 30 lines through the game's driver bindings: launch a while(1) kernel, call cuCtxDestroy from another thread after 2 s, measure the return, then cuDevicePrimaryCtxReset and one cuMemAlloc, because a known post-reset failure is the next allocation returning a destroyed-context error. Pass: a return within 5 s and a working fresh context; the engine stays in-process and ruling 6's recovery runs from the timeout thread. Fail: a child process with memfd-shared arenas and results ring, killed and respawned by the parent, at one IPC per block and per replay. The test runs under the shared lock on day one of track 4, and its result goes in docs/building.md either way. A hung kernel is a bug-only event here, since every lane group reads the drain flag each step, so the helper buys insurance against our own bugs, not the driver, and is not worth its cost unless the test fails.
